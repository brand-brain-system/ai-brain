# lp-03 Advertorial

**Use when:** There is a known rival to beat, or the audience trusts editorial more than ads. Head-to-head rounds or an "I tried it" first-person story.

**Buying phase it serves:** Exploration to Evaluation

**The brand needs:** rival_or_story, offer, product_page_or_checkout_url. **Helps to have:** expert_reviewer, reviews, price_per_use, sources.

## Why it works

It looks like an article. A byline, a disclosure, a problem the reader recognises, then either rounds against the rivals with a winner after each, or a first-person story from doubt to result. Every round or turn ends with a button carrying the code. Rugiet runs several of these on review-style sites.

## Section contract

| # | Section | Required | Its job | What to write |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S01 | disclosure | yes | Label it honestly | Sponsored / advertorial line and partnership disclosure |
| S02 | byline | yes | A real writer or reviewer | name, title, updated; reviewer only if a real named expert reviewed it |
| S03 | hero | yes | Article headline in the reader's words | headline: "[You] vs [Rival]: which [category] actually works?" or "I tried [product] for [problem]", 8-16 words; subhead 10-25 words; image |
| S04 | verdict | optional | Top pick box up front | heading 2-6 words; points 2-3 of 4-10 words; cta with the code |
| S05 | text | yes | Intro: how common the problem is and who tested | 2-3 paragraphs, 30-70 words; any stat sourced |
| S06 | benefits | optional | What to look for: criteria that match the product's mechanism | 2-3 criteria, title 3-8 words, body 15-35 words |
| S07 | comparison table | optional | Side by side, winner highlighted | 3-6 rows; only true rival facts |
| S08 | rounds | yes | Rounds (head-to-head) or story beats (first person), each ending in a button | 3-5 items: title 3-8 words; body 40-90 words; winner line 3-10 words (head-to-head only; concede one honest tie); cta 2-5 words |
| S09 | verdict | yes | Overall winner or result | heading 3-8 words; points 3 of 5-12 words; cta |
| S10 | faq | optional | Search-style questions | 3-5 Q&A |

## Adaptation notes

First-person mode: rounds become beats (the problem, failed fixes, discovery, doubt, first use, a full-day test, result) with no winner lines. No real writer or reviewer: use the brand's own editorial name and say so in the disclosure; never invent a person.

## Watch-outs

The article must cover the same product and angle as the ad (Honeylove's Popdust piece did not). Rival claims must be sourced and current; date the comparison.
