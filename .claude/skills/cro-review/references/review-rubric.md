# CRO review rubric

Use the questions that matter for this page. The rubric is a diagnostic surface, not a scorecard that must produce a finding in every category.

## 1. Traffic and message match

- What did the visitor click, search or expect immediately before arriving?
- Does the first screen repeat the same promise, offer, product and level of specificity?
- Is the page written for the same awareness stage and buyer situation?
- When no source ad is provided, is the assumed entry point stated rather than guessed?

## 2. First-screen comprehension

Within one scan, can a new visitor answer:

- What is this?
- Is it for me?
- What outcome or problem does it address?
- Why should I believe it?
- What should I do next?

The hero does not need to contain every answer, but it cannot require the visitor to decode the category or offer.

## 3. Argument and section order

- Does each section answer the next reasonable question?
- Does proof appear near the claim it supports?
- Is the page repeating rather than progressing?
- Does it ask for commitment before the reader understands the product or trusts the proof?
- Does page length reflect the novelty, price, risk and awareness level of the offer?

## 4. Proof and truth

- Are reviews verbatim and attributable?
- Are numbers, expert claims, comparisons and press marks sourced and current?
- Is product demonstration being mistaken for outcome proof?
- Are important claims softened or strengthened beyond the approved wording?
- Is missing proof acknowledged rather than replaced with generic trust furniture?

Unsupported proof is a verified fault. Thin proof is a craft problem. Whether a new proof block improves conversion is a test hypothesis.

## 5. Offer and action

- Is the price, discount, bundle, subscription, guarantee and expiry understandable?
- Do all CTA labels accurately describe the next step?
- Do buttons use the intended URL and preserve the offer?
- Are shipping, billing, subscription or eligibility surprises hidden until late?
- Is urgency real and specific?

## 6. Objections and risk

- Which objections are evidenced in reviews, comments, support or research?
- Does the page answer the strongest objections or only easy FAQs?
- Is the guarantee relevant and accurately described?
- Does the page distinguish the offer from the actual substitutes the buyer considers?

## 7. Visual hierarchy and mobile

- Is the intended reading order obvious without decorative explanation?
- Are type size, contrast, line length and spacing readable on a phone?
- Do images clarify product, use, scale or mechanism?
- Are sticky bars, pop-ups or repeated CTAs obstructing content?
- Does the page feel credible for the category and traffic source?

## 8. Function and accessibility

- Broken links, images, forms, anchors or safe interactions.
- Sideways overflow, clipped text or layout shifts.
- Missing labels, alt text, keyboard access or visible focus where material.
- Console errors or network failures that affect the experience.

Use the sibling `page-qa` skill for deterministic browser checks. Keep CRO interpretation here.

## Priorities

- `P0`: deceptive, legally risky, unusable or blocks the intended action.
- `P1`: breaks the core promise, comprehension, offer or proof chain.
- `P2`: meaningful persuasion or usability weakness.
- `P3`: polish or a lower-confidence optimization idea.
