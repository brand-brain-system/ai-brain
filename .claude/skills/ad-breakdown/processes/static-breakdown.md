# Process: static breakdown

A deep read of one static ad (or a carousel, card by card), part by part. Adapted from a practitioner's breakdown
prompts (Nov 2026), rewritten and regrouped; the "make your own version" part is left out on purpose, because
`recreate` and `iterations` own that.

A static has under a second to stop the scroll and one frame to do the whole job. So this lens reads space where the
video lens reads time: what the eye hits first and what it hits next, and whether that order is the order of the
argument.

Write into `../breakdown-template.md`. Every section gets a label (Verified, Inferred, Data-limited). Quote all text
verbatim. A section the ad doesn't touch says "Not in this ad", which is a finding too.

## Before you start

- The strategist's read is in the header, or "No hypothesis given" (`../SKILL.md`, step 2). Don't read it again until
  Part K
- You have the full-size image (each card for a carousel), the primary text, headline and link description from the
  ad, and the placement it was seen in if known
- For our ad: the numbers block in the header is filled and labelled. For a competitor ad: "Not our ad: no performance
  data"
- Terms mean what `brain-system/creative-strategy-context/creative-vocabulary.md` says (UMP = root cause of the
  problem, UMS = how the product fixes it, **proof of mechanism** = what makes the mechanism believable). Design terms
  that already have a home: `brain-system/creative-strategy-context/static-ad-design.md`

## Part A. Inventory

**A1. Everything on it.** All text, word for word, including fine print, badges, stickers, prices, review counts and
the logo. Every visual element (product, people, props, background, icons, arrows). The layout (grid, split, single
image, screenshot, note). The platform context: the primary text above, the headline and button below, and the
aspect ratio. A carousel: the same per card, in order

## Part B. Stop and eye flow

**B1. The stop.** What stops a scroll in under a second? Which element does the eye hit first? Is the hook the
headline or the image, and which awareness level does it talk to? Name the hook family (`callout`, `story`, `show`,
`claim`, `question`, `secret`; `winning-script-patterns.md`)

**B2. Visual hierarchy and eye flow.** The reading order: first, second, third, fourth. What forces that order: size,
colour contrast, whitespace, directional cues (arrows, lines, a pointing hand), a person's gaze. The pattern: Z, F,
single focal point, or a custom path. Does the reading order match the persuasion order (problem before answer, claim
before proof)? If not, which element is in the wrong place?

**B3. Gestalt.** How grouping does the work: proximity (what sits together reads as one idea), similarity (same colour
or shape reads as the same kind of thing), closure (the eye completes a shape or a sentence), figure and ground (what
pops, what recedes). Name one place it helps and one place it fights the message, if any

## Part C. Image, type and colour

**C1. Image strategy.** The type: product, lifestyle, UGC, before and after, screenshot, text only, illustration, meme,
stock, AI. Cover the words: what does the image say alone? Does it show the problem, the solution or the
transformation? Identification (that's me) or aspiration (I want to be that)? How visible is the product? What makes it
feel real or fake (a phone photo, a real hand, a messy kitchen, too-perfect skin)?

**C2. Typography and copy design.** The fonts and what they signal (handmade, clinical, premium, cheap). The size
hierarchy. Text colour and contrast against the background. Treatments: highlight, underline, strikethrough,
handwriting, emoji, a circled word. The text-to-image ratio, roughly

**C3. Colour strategy.** The dominant colours. The contrast of the key elements (headline, product, CTA). Does it match
the platform's interface (blends in, reads native) or clash with it (stops the eye)? Which colours are the brand's and
which are there only for attention?

## Part D. Audience and awareness

**D1. Awareness level.** Where the viewer starts, and the line or image that shows it

**D2. Sophistication.** How many similar claims the category has heard (S1-S5), and how this ad avoids looking like
every other ad in the category. Quote or point to the element

**D3. Who it's for.** The demographic it shows and implies, and the closest persona in `personas/personas-profile.md`.
Inferred

**D4. Trigger events.** The moment it speaks into, stated or implied

**D5. Behaviours targeted.** What the viewer does now that the ad names or shows

**D6. Past attempts.** The fixes and products the viewer has tried, and how the ad treats them

## Part E. Copy

**E1. Headline.** The formula: curiosity gap, direct benefit, social-proof lead, question, command, news, how-to,
listicle, before and after. Why that one for this viewer?

**E2. The rest of the copy.** The subhead's job (explain, prove, qualify). The body, if any. The CTA wording. The power
words, and which of them carry weight and which are filler. The primary text above the image: does it repeat, extend
or contradict the image?

**E3. Big idea.** The one idea the ad hangs on, in one sentence

## Part F. Desire and emotion

**F1. Main desire.** The one core want, in the customer's words where you can quote them

**F2. Secondary desires.** The motivations stacked on top

**F3. Life Force 8.** The primary one and at most one secondary (list: `creative-vocabulary.md`, "LF8"), with the
element that carries it

**F4. Valence and intensity.** A static is one moment, so there is no arc. The charge (toward or away), the intensity
1-10, and the element that sets it. Calibration (`creative-vocabulary.md`, "Emotional valence and intensity"): is it
over-agitated or under-agitated for this viewer?

**F5. Emotions targeted.** As the viewer would feel them: "I feel...", "I'm tired of..."

## Part G. Belief and mechanism

**G1. Belief transfer in one frame.** How one image moves a belief: by implication (it shows, you conclude), by
juxtaposition (two things side by side), by proof (a number, a review, a result), or by reframe (a new way to see the
problem). Which belief, and which device?

**G2. Objections.** On a static an objection is often answered in one line, a badge or a visual (a "no added sugar"
seal, a photo of the small size). List each one and how. Name the obvious one left open

**G3. Competitive disqualification.** How it rules out the alternatives. Is the enemy specific or vague? That is
`enemy`

**G4. Mechanism, compressed.** USP (a feature), UMP (the root cause it names), UMS (how the product fixes that cause),
kept apart. A static often carries the mechanism in a few words or a diagram. One mechanism with a "because" is
`mechanism: yes`

**G5. Proof of mechanism.** What makes it believable in the frame. Map it to `proof`

**G6. New, Easy, Safe, Big.** Each one quoted or pointed to. Name the missing one

**G7. Benefit stacking in limited space.** Which benefits made the cut, in what order, and what was left off. Does the
stack read in two seconds or does it need reading?

**G8. Social proof elements.** Stars, review counts, customer quotes, "as seen in", user counts, before and after,
certifications, awards, an influencer. Check each number against the proof inventory in `brand-lens.md` for our own ad

## Part H. Offer and CTA

**H1. CTA.** The CTA in the creative (words, placement, contrast) against the platform button below it. Does the button ask for the same thing as the creative?
Urgency, friction removed, the offer if any. Map it to `close`

## Part I. Native fit and positioning

**I1. Conversational patterns.** Slang, fragments, questions, first person, deliberate imperfection in the text. Quote
two. Does it read like a person or a brand?

**I2. Native integration.** Does it look like a friend's post, a meme, a screenshot, an editorial card, or plainly
branded? The format choice (square, vertical, carousel, story) and whether it suits the placement. Would a fast scroller
take it for organic content? What gives it away?

**I3. Positioning.** Friend or convert, authority, or a hybrid, with the evidence. That and any quoted speaker give
`voice` (`none` when no one speaks)

## Part J. Formula

**J1. The spatial formula.** The persuasion sequence the eye follows, as one line, for example:
`Headline callout (top) -> Product with result (centre) -> Three benefit ticks (right) -> Review quote (bottom) ->
Button`.

**J2. Archetype.** Name it if it matches one: Problem-Agitate-Solve card, testimonial screenshot, us vs them
comparison, listicle benefit stack, UGC repost, before and after split, meme format hijack, editorial or native
article card. Or "none, new pattern", and describe it. Then the shape (`symptom`, `enemy`, `story`, `objection`,
`authority`, `offer`, `other`) and the ladder step the headline opens on

## Part K. Close the breakdown

**K1. What the numbers say about the parts** (our ad only). CTR to Parts B, C and E, conversion rate to
Parts E, G and H, CPA and ROAS to the whole. Compare with the account's own benchmark for statics in the same period.
Every link is Inferred

**K2. Why it worked, in five lines.** Each cites a part. For a competitor ad: "why it is probably still running",
Inferred

**K3. Tag block and pattern check.** Fill both from `../breakdown-template.md`. `product_at` is `x` for a static

**K4. Your read vs the breakdown.** Now read the strategist's answers again and write the comparison (`../SKILL.md`,
step 8)

## Light pass (for batch-winners)

A1, B1, B2, C1, G3, G4, J1, J2, the tag block and K2 only. Mark the file "Light pass" in the header.
