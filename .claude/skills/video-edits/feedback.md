# Feedback log

What we've observed in runs. A log, not a rulebook: new lessons land here with a date, and once
confirmed across three runs they move into `SKILL.md`, leaving one line here saying where they went.
Write lessons generally; a brand's edit style goes in that brain's `craft-reasoning/video/edit-style.md`.

## Log

**2026-09-29, first run (test on a third-party 31 s clip, music + captions only)**
- Music: fal's `recommend_model` sent a music request to text-to-video models. Search "music" instead. `sonilo/v1.1/text-to-music` worked well: exact duration, described as licensed for commercial use, $0.0025/s, 2 options for ~$0.16. Also available: `sonilo/v1.1/video-to-music` (syncs to the video, $0.009/s), ElevenLabs Music, MiniMax Music 3
- Mix that worked: music -17 dB, sidechain-ducked under the voice, loudnorm -14 LUFS. For a punchline, fade the music out just before it so the line lands in silence
- Captions: chunk by max 3 words AND max 16 characters, and always break at a full stop. Without the character cap, long words wrapped to two lines; without the full-stop rule, chunks ran across sentences ("rule. And I'm")
- Filler words ("uh") left out of captions; audio untouched
- Transcription is near perfect in English (Scribe v2), but don't "fix" grammar in the transcript: the speaker said "trouble spot", keep it
- Windows: PowerShell `Set-Content -Encoding utf8` adds a BOM; read JSON with `utf-8-sig`. ffmpeg 9 uses `-fps_mode`, not `-vsync`
- the lead strategist picked music A ("more music and punch"): the version where the tension builds and the music drops out before the punchline
- **Too many full renders** (4 × 2 full-res passes) made his CPU cooler loud. Fixed with the CPU budget in `SKILL.md`: text-first caption checks, small previews, one cached video pass, audio-only re-mixes, low priority. The same fix pass then took 2.6 s as a preview instead of minutes
- Google Drive: a file in a folder shared by link downloads with curl from `drive.usercontent.google.com/download?id=<id>&export=download&confirm=t`, which avoids pulling base64 through the Drive MCP

**2026-10-09, first real raw-footage run (an e-commerce test brand, English UGC, 9 phone clips, no script; one-shot mode)**
- One-shot worked end to end: three ~70 s hook versions + trimmed cut in about 50 s of preview rendering, re-transcription matched the plan. No editing app, no MCP in the edit itself
- Transcription: join all clips' audio into one file with a 1.5 s gap between clips and keep the offsets (`offsets.json`) = one upload, one call (~$0.06 for 7 min). The fal MCP saves big results to a file; parse it from there
- No script → read the whole transcript first and build the arc from her lines (hook, problem, turn, product, proof, close). Moving a complete sentence to another place is fine; never inside a sentence
- Creators misname the product ("Breathe Right" for an e-commerce test brand in 3 takes). Check every take for the brand name and only use takes that say it right
- Captions must break at every cut, or a chunk runs across two takes ("myself, So when"); and only take words that *start* inside a piece, or the next take's first word leaks in
- Windows ffmpeg: `drawtext` needs an explicit `fontfile`, and this build has no `-pattern_type glob`; number frames instead
- Phones mix 30 and 120 fps and 4K/1080p; `fps=30` + scale per piece normalises them. Rotation is applied automatically
- Product packaging and insert cards can carry claims and offer codes ("ADD YEARS TO YOUR LIFE", a 10% code): note them in `edl.md` before using that B-roll

**2026-10-23, test of the Internet Archive (Prelinger) as a B-roll source: search, preview and 9:16 crop, five films, no brand (open, needs a second brand)**
- Search: `advancedsearch.php` with `collection:prelinger AND licenseurl:*publicdomain*` (1,876 of 10,468 films) works with no key. **Free-text search is noisy** (it matches descriptions, so "office OR typewriter" returned bank, health and civil-defence films). Searching `title:` and `subject:` was much better: the subjects are a fixed taxonomy ("Occupations: Clerical", "Computers: History", "Automation", "Communication: Telephone")
- Preview without downloading: ffmpeg reads the `_512kb.mp4` straight from `archive.org/download/<id>/` and makes an 18-frame contact sheet in about 5 s per film. Enough to choose clips by eye before any download
- **Resolution is the limit:** the sharpest mp4 on most films is 640x480 (the `_512kb` one is 320x240). A 9:16 crop of a 4:3 frame keeps 42 % of the width, so 270x480 pixels, blown up 4x for a 1080x1920 ad. Fine as a deliberate low-fi look, soft as plain B-roll. Prefer the 640x480 file, and try the film at full 4:3 width over a blurred or dark fill instead of cropping
- **A centred crop cuts people in half** (one operator at a console was half out of frame). Pick the crop offset per shot, or use the fill
- **Cut on shot boundaries, not guessed times:** four 4 s clips cut at times read off the contact sheet each straddled a cut. Run ffmpeg `scdet` on the film first and cut between changes
- Titles and credits are burned into the film (a production-company logo, "produced through the courtesy of the Department of Defense and Boeing"): keep the clip clear of them. Company names and logos (IBM building) also appear in the footage
- Quality of the material itself was good: the colour 1950s computer-room, punch-card and radar-dome shots and the black-and-white typewriter and secretary shots are strong contrast against a modern product
- Not tested: a full edit with the clips in `edl.md`, music, a second brand, any paid run. Rule 6 stands: this stays here until it has held on a second brand

- **Trials, to try on the first real ad and kept here until they hold on a second brand (rule 6):**
  - *B-roll planning:* plan the whole video before placing anything (the strongest shot goes to the line it fits most literally); the test for a fit is "would a viewer with the sound off get the line from the shot alone"; chain related slices into a mini story (tear, look inside, pull one out); never show the same moment twice; at most two separate spots per raw B-roll video; a line with nothing that truly fits stays on the person, since a near fit that shows something else is worse. Sits well with the 2-3 s pacing rule only if the gap can be filled by punch-ins or cards
  - *Best pieces from several takes:* a line may be built from the best phrase of different takes or recordings when head and hands sit in about the same place on both sides of the joint, or the joint is covered by B-roll. A switch between recordings also needs the same look (hair, light, product in hand). One row per take with a reason in the EDL

Ours says "about 110 % centred on the face"; try the eye anchor and measure the eye position every half second
  - *Captions broken by meaning:* small words start the next chunk and a chunk ends on a word that carries meaning ("a sleep drink", "go to bed" kept whole). Needs a Danish list of small words (og, at, en, et, til, i, på, som, der, jeg); try beside our 3 words / 16 characters cap
  - *A B-roll library per brand:* tags only for what can be seen (`unboxing:box`, `product-in-use:pour`), one shoot file per shoot day, a list of evergreen shots to bank from every product shoot, and only the picked moments kept (cut with 1 s buffer).
