# Ad teardown: reading what an ad shows, not only what its caption says

Mode B tags ads from their copy. That misses the video and the image, which is where most of the
persuasion happens. This process tears down a small set of ads one by one, so the audit can say what
the top ads *show*. Use it after B2, on the ads that matter most: the top 10-20 by delivery (the
Library's impression sort, or spend with account access), plus one or two from each concept.

Inspired by Motion's "AI prompts to analyse ads" (video and static, 30+ sections each), cut down to the
fields that change a decision. Written in our own words.

## Before you start

- **Watch or look at the real ad.** From the Library card (play the video, open the image) or the
  creative file from the team. Never tear down an ad from its caption alone: then it isn't a teardown
- **Write down what's on screen before judging it:** the first frame, the on-screen text, the spoken
  words of the first 3 seconds, the product's first appearance (second), the last frame and CTA
- Mark anything you couldn't see (sound off, cropped, a carousel card missing) as Data-limited

## The twelve fields

| # | Field | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | The first 1-3 seconds: what's seen, said and written. Which works without sound? |
| 2 | **Awareness** | What the ad assumes the viewer knows (unaware → most aware), and the line that shows it |
| 3 | **Sophistication move** | How it stands out from the category's worn claims: a new mechanism, a sharper proof, an identity, or nothing |
| 4 | **Main desire** | The one want it speaks to, in the customer's terms |
| 5 | **Emotion and intensity** | The dominant feeling (relief, fear, pride, curiosity…), toward or away, and 1-10 at its peak. Is that right for this persona's state? |
| 6 | **Persona and trigger** | Which card it speaks to, and the moment it shows (the cart seat, the gift occasion) |
| 7 | **Objection handled** | Which objection it answers, where, and how (demo, quote, guarantee, a line) |
| 8 | **Mechanism and proof** | How it says the product works, and what it shows to prove it (a demo, a stat, a review, a before/after) |
| 9 | **New / easy / safe / big** | How it frames the offer as new, easy, low-risk and worth it. Name the missing one |
| 10 | **Formula** | The sequence, with seconds for video: e.g. hook → problem → demo → proof → offer → CTA. For a static, the order the eye reads it |
| 11 | **Native or ad-like** | Would a fast scroller take it for a post? What gives it away? (Native style is fine; the ad must still be disclosed as an ad) |
| 12 | **What to keep, what to test** | One thing to keep in the next ad, one variable to change, tied to a field above |

## Rules

- Every field gets a label: Verified (seen in the ad), Inferred (read from what's seen), Data-limited
- Quote on-screen text and speech verbatim; describe visuals plainly; don't name a person on screen
  unless the ad names them
- A teardown says what an ad does, never whether it worked. Performance comes only from the account
- Several near-identical ads (same person, setting and body, different headline): tear down one and say
  how many twins it has. That count belongs in the audit's delivery note

## Where it goes

`<brain>/audits/<YYYY-MM>/ad-audit-<YYYY-MM-DD>/teardowns.md` or the Mode B run folder: one
section per ad, with the Library id and link. Add two columns to the Mode B sheet from it: **hook (seen)**
and **formula**, filled only for the torn-down ads.

For a deep read of one ad (the strategist's hypothesis first, the ad's numbers, and the shared tag block), run `ad-breakdown`.
