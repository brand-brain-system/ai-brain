# Winning script patterns: 100 tagged scripts

**Source:** a practitioner's study of 100 winning ad scripts (a long post and a one-page summary), handed in by the team on 7 Nov 2026. Author not credited by the owner's choice.
**Users:** the whole team, all brands. Read by `scriptwriting`, `hooks`, `ad-breakdown` (the pattern check and the `shape`, `hook_family`, `enemy`, `mechanism`, `product_at`, `proof`, `close` and `voice` tags) and anyone writing a brief.
**Status:** every number here is *Stated*: one practitioner's tags read off transcripts, not spend data. Not yet tested on our accounts. The mapping to our hook taxonomy and the checklist are ours.

## The set and its limits

**What was read.** 100 scripts, each transcript read in full and tagged for the hook, who talks, when the product is named, what gets blamed, what proof is used and how it closes.
- 55 are the number one ad by impressions of 55 brands, still running after more than 120 days.
- 45 are top-spending ads from the author's earlier breakdowns in pet, skincare, supplements and song ads, including 6 of the author's own scaled pet ads.

**What it can't tell you.**
- The tags are read off transcripts, not spend or results. "Number one by impressions and live for 120 days" is a strong signal, but it is reach and survival, not ROAS.
- The set leans health, beauty, pets and food. B2B, fashion and gifting may behave differently.
- Survivorship: you see the winners, not the dead ads that used the same enemy.
- The enemy doesn't save a bad product.

Read every count below as "common among ads that survived", not "this causes results".

## 1. The enemy

84 of 100 attack something before they sell. Usually it is whatever the viewer uses now instead: the steroid cream, the cheap copy from a marketplace, the old pillow.

Why it works: nobody switches until the current thing looks like the problem. Naming it does two jobs. It tells the viewer "this is about you", and it gives a reason the last product failed that isn't their fault.

Specific beats vague. "Most 'clean' oat milks aren't clean" lands; "bad ingredients" doesn't. If you can't point at the thing, you don't have an enemy yet.

63 of 100 combine the enemy with a problem-first opening. That pairing is the closest thing to a default in the set.

The same idea runs through `yapper-ads.md`: the villain moment, and "new villain + same product = new angle". The villain there and the enemy here are often the same thing; the villain is who made them feel the pain, the enemy is what gets blamed.

## 2. Problem before product

67 of 100 open on the viewer's problem, in the viewer's words. In one script the product isn't named until 74 seconds into 87.

The other 33 open on the product, a result, an offer or a skit, and still work: for viewers who already know the category. On our ladder that is the difference between entering at 3 and entering at 7-9 (`awareness-script-ladder.md`).

The words come from customers, not from the brief: `customer-review-mining-method.md`, and `research-map` in the brain.

## 3. The why: one mechanism

67 of 100 explain why the product works with one mechanism, said simply. The shape of it: "Insoles sit on top of your foot. The problem is underneath."

The mechanism is the other half of the enemy. The old fix fails for a reason; the new one works for the opposite reason. In our words: the UMP and the UMS (`creative-vocabulary.md`). An enemy without a "because" gets nods and no purchase.

One mechanism, not three. If the script needs a list of reasons, pick the one that is the mirror of the enemy's failure.

## 4. Hook families

The first line, sorted into six families. Our `hook_family` tag uses the same six. The taxonomy in `hooks.md` is finer; this is where its types sit.

| Family (`hook_family`) | Count | What the first line does | Closest types in `hooks.md` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `callout` | 30 | Calls out the person or the symptom | #6 Demographic, #5 POV, #8 How do I know if, #14 Trigger word (when it names a symptom) |
| `story` | 23 | Opens a story | #18 Storytelling, #2 Investment, #22 Trigger-event |
| `show` | 20 | Shows something: a skit, an offer, a piece of news | #12 Reaction, #11 Viral product; skit and offer openers have no type of their own |
| `claim` | 13 | States a result or a claim | #9 Speed and transformation, #17 Comparison |
| `question` | 8 | Asks a question, or plays an overheard line | #20 Question, #10 Conversational, #1 Comment response |
| `secret` | 6 | Reveals a secret or leans on authority | #19 Authority, #15 Myth busting, #16 Educational, #13 Controversy, #3 Scam |

Not placed: #4 Give me time, #7 Multiple hook strategy (a way to stack hooks, not a family) and #21 Permission. Tag by what the first line does, not by the label in the brief.

The single most common opener is the symptom call out: 15 of 100. Results and claims open only 13, although briefs tend to default to them. The winners describe the viewer first.

| Voice | Count | `voice` tag |
|---|---|---|

| Expert (doctor, vet, trainer, groomer) | 17 | `expert` |
| Customer story | 12 | `customer` |
| Founder | 10 | `founder` |
| Voiceover | 10 | `vo` |
| A song | 10 | `song` |
| Skit | 6 | `skit` |
| Podcast, street interview, other | 6 | `other` (the format tag says `podcast` or `street`) |
| AI character | 3 | `ai` |

No voice owns the list. The same shape (enemy, problem, why) works across nine voices.

The delivery point: if every script in an account is a creator to camera, Meta may treat them as one ad in ten outfits and show them to the same people. How Meta groups similar ads: `andromeda-v2.md` (Entity ID). Change the voice, not only the words. An AI character speaking as a customer falls under `legal-ai-ugc.md`.

## 6. When the product shows up

76 of the 100 could be timed.
- The median script names the product at 28% of its runtime.
- 15 name it in the first 10%. 22 wait past halfway.
- Early naming goes with offers, reviews and demos for viewers who know the category. Late naming goes with scripts that teach first.
- In the 55 library winners, 18 name the product after 40% of the runtime. Those run longer: a median of 84 seconds against 54 for the rest.

The rule: product timing follows the job. A script that teaches needs about 80 seconds. Don't cut a teaching script to 30 seconds and expect the same result. The `product_at` tag records the percentage.

## 7. Proof

| Proof (`proof` tag) | Count |
|---|---|
| Personal experience (`personal`) | 26 |
| A number (`number`) | 24 |
| Authority (`authority`) | 16 |
| Demo (`demo`) | 6 |
| Timeline (`timeline`) | 2 |
| Taste reaction (`taste`) | 1 |
| None (`none`) | 25 |

Timeline as the main proof is rare, but 15 of 100 walk through a timeline somewhere ("week 1... week 2...").

A quarter use no proof at all: the enemy and the why carry the belief. A clear reason the old thing failed does more than a star rating. Every number, review count or rating in our ads comes from the proof inventory in `brand-lens.md` and nowhere else (`system/evidence-and-labels.md`).

## 8. The close

Counted in the 55 library winners only:
- "Link below" or "tap here": 18
- An offer (a discount, a gift, a bundle): 15
- A money-back guarantee, usually in the last 10 seconds: 11
- No call to action: 10

The offer rarely shapes the script. It sits at the end. Big brands can live with a high cost per sale, so test the close on your own numbers before copying theirs. Offers in Danish ads follow `offer-design.md` and the brand's compliance file.

## 9. The five shapes

Fill-in templates. The brackets are the slots; the words between them are ours to change. The `shape` tag uses the name in brackets after each heading. Where each shape enters the awareness ladder: `awareness-script-ladder.md`.

**1. Symptom call out (`symptom`)**
"If you [visible symptom], [surprising reframe]. Whatever you do, stop [what they do now]. [Why the old fix fails.] [What works, and why.] The one I [use / recommend] is [product]."

**2. Enemy first (`enemy`)**
"Don't buy [category] from [where they buy it]. I bought [number] of them. [What's wrong with them.] If you [symptom], you need [what the real one has]. [Product]: [how it's different]."

**3. Story with a cost (`story`)**
"I [low point, with a number]. I tried [fix 1], [fix 2], [fix 3]. Nothing worked. Then [who] told me [the real cause]. [Product] [what it does]. Week 1: [small change]. Week 2: [bigger change]. Week 3: [the moment]."

**4. Objection opener (`objection`)**
"We get asked this a lot: if [the doubt], what makes ours different? [What others do wrong.] [What we do.] [One number.] And if [it doesn't work for you], [guarantee]."

**5. Authority secret (`authority`)**
"I'm a [credible role], and here's the one thing we do to [outcome]. It isn't [obvious fix 1]. It isn't [obvious fix 2]. [The real cause.] [The old way that fails.] [Product] [does it right]."

The tag schema also has `offer` (a script built around the deal, for most aware viewers) and `other`. They are not among the five shapes in the study.

## 10. Shape first, voice second

Write the shape first. Then pick who says it: a creator, a vet, a customer, an AI character, a song. One shape becomes five ads that look nothing alike, which is what Meta needs to see different ads (section 5). Keep the enemy, the problem and the mechanism; change the speaker, the setting and the format.

## Pre-flight check

Run this on every script before it goes to the voice and grounding reviews. A "no" is not a ban; it is a question to answer in the brief.

1. **Enemy.** Does it name a specific enemy, something the viewer could point at? Write it in the `enemy` tag. "Bad ingredients" is not specific.
2. **Problem.** Is the problem said in the viewer's words, traced to a quote in the brain?
3. **Mechanism.** Is there one mechanism, and is it the opposite of why the old fix failed?
4. **Hook.** Does the first line describe the viewer (a symptom, a situation, a moment) rather than make a claim? If it is a claim, say why.
5. **Product timing.** Does it fit the job? Product early for category-aware viewers, late for teaching. Is a teaching script given about 80 seconds?
6. **Proof.** Was the proof chosen on purpose, from the proof inventory? Or is it none, because the enemy and the why carry it?
7. **Close.** Is the offer at the end, not in the middle?
8. **Shape and entry.** Is the shape named, and does the `ladder_start` fit the awareness level?
9. **Voice.** Was the voice picked after the shape, and is it different from the last batch for this concept?

Then the usual gates: `spoken-script-voice.md` for how it sounds, the grounding and voice reviews (`system/evidence-and-labels.md`), and `ad-policy-check` before launch.
