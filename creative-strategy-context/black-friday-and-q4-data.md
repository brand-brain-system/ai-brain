# Black Friday and Q4: what the cross-brand data says

Ours. Built from Parker's "The Q4 Ad Creative Report" and its methods file "How we built this report" (two PDFs, Q4 2025 data, pulled 12-15 Oct 2026, read 22 Oct 2026). No text is copied; the
numbers are theirs and credited here. This is the **one home** for these numbers. Other docs point here instead of repeating them.

How it fits with the other Q4 docs:
- `seasonality.md`: whether a season matters for this brand at all, and how buyer, angle and SKU shift
- `gifting-and-q4-creative.md`: the practitioner method for the gift season and the sale (buyer vs user, the phase model, the adaptation ladder)
- this doc: what the data across many brands shows, and where it confirms or challenges those two
- `.claude/skills/offer-ads/offer-copy.md`: the Danish price-marketing rules and the campaign phases for offer ads
- `.claude/skills/season-report/`: runs the same studies on one brand's own account

## How to read these numbers

- **Label: Stated.** One study, run by a vendor on its own customers' accounts. We haven't re-run it. A brand's own data beats every number here; when they disagree, follow the brand and note it in the
  brain's `running-notes/feedback.md`
- **Who is in it:** 432 brands that sell physical products, mostly US, each with at least $100,000 of Meta spend in Q4 2025 and 10+ ads that spent. $507.9M of spend, 586,948 ads. Biggest categories:
  supplements and health (103), beauty (81), other (69), apparel (56), home (42). The smallest size group is "under $250K in Q4", so most Danish brands are smaller than anyone in the study. Use the
  shapes and the ratios, not the dollar levels
- **What a winner is:** an ad in the top 10 % of its own brand's ads by spend in a window, with at least $2,500 spent there. Spend is Meta's choice of which ads to fund. ROAS, CPA and revenue are never
  compared across brands
- **Two checks:** every "did better" finding was checked with all brands added together and inside each brand. A pattern counts only when both agree. Several things that looked strong added together
  didn't hold inside brands (gifting is the clearest case), and those are marked below
- **Small groups:** under 30 brands are marked * and not used for advice
- **Black Friday week** in the study is Thu 20 Nov to Tue 2 Dec 2025
- **What it can't show:** who bought (only who Meta reached), sales or profit, cause (these are patterns in what Meta funded), and placements (video can run in more places than statics)

## The findings

Each finding: what happened, the number, what to do with it, and the caveat.

### 1. CPMs: up three weeks before Black Friday, cheapest right after Christmas

- Early October CPM about $17.67. Week of Nov 17: $21.91 (+24 %), before Black Friday had started. Week of Nov 24 (with Black Friday): $26.26 (+49 %). Week of Dec 29: $14.99, the cheapest week from
  July to January. Held when re-run on the same 283 brands every week and on each brand against its own early October
- By category, the median brand's CPM the week of Nov 24 was 46-54 % above its own early October in the five categories big enough to count: home +54 %, supplements and health +53 %, apparel +50 %, beauty and other +46 %
- **Use:** test new holiday ideas before the climb (in October and the first days of November), not in it. Plan the first week after Christmas as a cheap window to launch and test Q1 creative
- **Caveat:** CPM is the price of being seen, not of a sale

### 2. Volume and hit rate

- Median brand: 631 new ads in Q4 (about 48 a week; the average was 1,149, pulled up by giants). About 1 in 19 new ads ever reached $2,500 of spend, 1 in 91 reached $10,000. About 18 ads took half
  the median brand's Q4 spend; the top 10 took 38 %, the single top ad 8 %
- By size (median brand): under $250K of Q4 spend, 312 new ads and 1 in 31 reached $2,500; $250K-$1M, 539 and 1 in 20; $1M-$5M, 1,425 and 1 in 14
- A winner lasts: the median winner kept spending about 50 days, and 34 % were still spending in the second half of January
- **Use:** a cross-check for `creative-volume.md`. Expect most new ads to barely spend, and expect the season to rest on a small set of ads. Build sale creative that can keep running after the sale
- **Caveat:** every ad ID counts, so a copy of one ad in a new ad set counts as a new ad. Real creative volume is lower

### 3. When Black Friday ads went on

- Of 320 brands whose top ads named Black Friday: a quarter turned them on by Nov 11, half by Nov 18, 84 % by Monday Nov 24
- The ads that went on to win Black Friday week started earlier: a third were already running on Oct 31, two thirds by Nov 19. The busiest day for turning on a future winner was Friday Nov 21, a week
  before Black Friday
- Some started small: of the winners already running in October, about a third spent less than a quarter as much per day in October as in Black Friday week
- **Use:** have the Black Friday creative made and live before the peak, and let candidate winners run at low spend first. In Denmark, read "early" as the creative and the ads without a price; the
  price claim can only start when the offer does (see "The Danish rules change the calendar" below)
- **Caveat:** the study doesn't say whether the early winners already carried the offer

### 4. How fast winners showed

- Of Q4 winners launched in the season: 31 % were among their brand's top 10 % daily spenders on day 1, 60 % by day 3, 75 % by day 7, 86 % by day 14
- Of 245,498 new ads that ran at least a week: those that were top spenders within 7 days became winners 34 % of the time; those that weren't, 2 %
- **Use:** in a short sale window, judge a new ad's delivery after about a week, and make the sale creative early enough to get that week before the peak. The general rule lives in
  `ad-account-analysis.md` ("The day-7 read")
- **Caveat:** this is Meta's early pick, not proof that a slow ad is a bad ad. Slow-burners exist (`selecting-ads-to-iterate-on.md`): 14 % of winners took more than two weeks

### 5. New ideas or new takes

- 812 Q4 winners at 81 brands, each read against the brand's earlier ads: 18 % were brand-new ideas, 54 % new takes on an idea the brand already had (new hook, edit, format, offer), 27 % the same words
  as an older ad (mostly the same ad in a new ad set)
- And beyond the new ones: 31 % of all Q4 winners were made before October and kept running or came back
- **Use:** confirms "Do not reinvent for the sale" in `gifting-and-q4-creative.md` and the effort mix in `creative-volume.md`. Most of the sale is adapted winners; keep a few brand-new ideas in every batch

### 6. Which offers won

- Of the spend on ads with a specific deal: percent off 62 %, buy one get one 14 %, free gift 10 %, money off 9 %, bundle 5 %, free shipping 1 %*. The most common number was 50 % off; the median discount
  40 %
- Added together, money-off ads made their brand's top 10 % most often (61 % of the deal ads read), then buy one get one (51 %), percent off (46 %), free gift (40 %). Inside brands, no deal type clearly
  beat the others
- Deeper discounts did better added together (over 50 % off: 60 %; 21-25 % off: 35 %), but inside brands it split evenly (11 vs 11 at 25 brands), so it is not a finding
- Order size didn't shrink: the median brand's Meta-reported order size in Black Friday week was 3 % above October's
- **Use:** the deal type and depth are the brand's commercial call, not something the data settles. Don't push a deeper discount on this evidence. The Danish rules on price claims apply to every one of
  these (`offer-copy.md`)
- **Caveat:** order size is what Meta reports, and Meta tends to give itself credit

### 7. What won Black Friday week

Share of ads that made the brand's top 10 % in Black Friday week (all tagged ads: 9.3 %), and brands where it did better vs worse than the brand's other ads:

| Kind of ad | Made the top 10 % | Inside brands, better vs worse |
|---|---|---|
| Urgency | 14.6 % | 135 vs 74 |
| Offer ads (built around the deal) | 13.3 % | 142 vs 70 |
| Celebrity | 13.5 % | 14 vs 17 (held over the whole Nov 1-Dec 2 sale: 25 vs 16) |
| Founder | 11.0 % | 33 vs 29 (whole sale: 48 vs 27) |
| Hope-led | 9.0 % | 92 vs 125 |
| Plain headline statics | 9.0 % | 69 vs 114 |
| Single-creator UGC | 8.5 % | 44 vs 87 |
| Question headlines | 6.9 % | 8 vs 28 |
| Statics made to look like a social post | 6.6 % | 14 vs 36 |
| Benefit-list statics | 6.0 % | 29 vs 72 |

- Offer and urgency ads won on both checks. UGC, hope-led ads and plain statics lost inside brands mostly because offer ads beat them: with offer ads taken out, UGC and hope-led no longer clearly lost,
  plain statics still did
- Urgency held mostly on ads that also had a deal
- Offer ads beat the average in every size group big enough to count; at brands under $250K they nearly doubled it (6.1 % vs 3.2 %), while over the whole quarter their edge there was small (7.5 % vs 6.4 %)
- **Use:** in the sale week, put the deal on the creative. That doesn't mean new creative: put the deal on the proven winners (the adaptation ladder in `gifting-and-q4-creative.md`). Urgency only when the
  deadline is real (`offer-copy.md`)

### 8. Cyber Monday

- Of 320 brands running Black Friday ads, 56 put Cyber Monday in a top ad on Dec 1-2, and 10 made it the main message. Those days, ads that still said Black Friday took 28 % of the money on brands' top
  ads; ads that said Cyber Monday took 5 %
- **Use:** Cyber Monday is an optional phase, not a default second wave. In Denmark it matters even less; a stated Black Week period that runs through the Monday covers it

### 9. When brands stopped

- Main Black Friday ad off: 42 % by Dec 2, half by Dec 3, three quarters by about Dec 14; 17 % still spending in January. Median run 19 days (category medians 10-27 days)
- Deals didn't stop, they shrank: deal ads were 15 % of the median brand's spend in early October, 24 % in Nov 1-19, 37 % in Nov 20-30, and 22 % in Dec 3-20
- **Use:** have December ads (gift angle, shipping deadlines, the next offer) made and ready to swap in the day the sale ends. In Denmark a December discount is measured from the Black Friday price

### 10. Brands that usually reach men

- 85 brands whose summer spend mostly reached men (35 % or less to women): the median brand's women share was about 13 % in summer, rose from mid-October, reached 30 % the week of Dec 8, fell to 17 % the
  week of Dec 22 and 15 % in January
- Brands that usually reach women did not get a matching rush of men
- By category (all small groups, a hint only): beauty and grooming moved most (9 of 12 gained 10+ points), apparel 13 of 18, supplements 4 of 14
- **Use:** confirms the ICP flip in `seasonality.md` and "The buyer is not the user" in `gifting-and-q4-creative.md`: for a brand that usually reaches men, plan creative for women buying for men from
  mid-October to mid-December, and check the brand's own weekly gender split
- **Caveat:** it shows who Meta reached, not who bought

### 11. Gifting

- Brands running any gifting ad: 17-27 % a month from April to September, 50 % in December. Gifting's share of spend: about 2 % in July, 7 % in December
- Added together, gifting looked like a winner (15 % of gifting ads made the top 10 % in Q4, vs 11 % of other ads). Inside brands it didn't hold: over the whole quarter a tie (44 vs 44 brands); month by
  month, worse at 60 brands and better at 42. For the same 65 brands every month, the edge disappeared
- **Use:** test gifting ads against the brand's best ads, don't assume they win in Q4. This is the main correction to our practitioner docs (see below)

### 12. Statics vs video

- Statics took 41-45 % of spend every month from April to December. Across Q4, 3.7 % of statics made their brand's top 10 %, vs 5.3 % of videos, in every size group big enough to count
- A deal closed the gap: in Black Friday week, statics with a deal 13.4 %, videos with a deal 13.2 %. Without a deal, video 8.7 %, statics 7.1 %. Both held inside brands
- Statics also worked as late launches (34 % of static Black Friday winners went live during the sale, vs 27 % of video winners), and for 165 of 432 brands the biggest Q4 ad was a static
- **Use:** statics are a strong format for the offer itself, and for late additions during the sale (`offer-ads`). Outside the sale, video carries more
- **Caveat:** video can run in more placements, which may help its spend

### 13. How winners opened

- 4,696 Q4 winners. Video winners most often opened by teaching or revealing something (15 %), then stories, call-outs ("if you're a...") and the deal; no hook type owned video. Static winners: 28 % led
  with the deal, 20 % with the benefit (beauty: 35 % with the deal)
- **Use:** for sale statics, a deal-led or benefit-led headline is the common pattern among winners (see the open test in `offer-ads/feedback.md`)
- **Caveat:** there is no base rate (what all ads opened with), so this shows what winners did, not what makes an ad win

## Moving the calendar to this year

The study's dates are 2025. Black Friday is the Friday after the fourth Thursday of November, so it falls between 23 and 29 November. Move every date by the same number of days as Black Friday moved,
keeping the weekday. Then apply the Danish rules.

**2026, worked:** Black Friday is Fri 27 Nov 2026, one day earlier than 2025 (Fri 28 Nov).

| 2025 in the study | Moved to 2026 | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Early October baseline CPM | Early October | The cheap testing window |
| Week of Nov 17: CPM already +24 % | Week of Nov 16 | New ideas should be tested before this, ideally by the first week of November |
| Oct 31: a third of future winners already running | Oct 30 | Candidate sale creative live, at low spend, without a price claim |
| Nov 18: half of brands had Black Friday ads on | Nov 17 | |
| Fri Nov 21: busiest day for turning on a future winner | Fri Nov 20 | Also the usual start of a Danish Black Week |
| Week of Nov 24: peak CPM (+49 %) | Week of Nov 23 | Black Friday week; deal on the creative |
| Dec 1-2: Cyber Monday | Mon Nov 30 | Optional; Black Friday wording kept the money |
| Dec 3: half had switched off the main Black Friday ad | Dec 2 | December ads ready to swap in |
| Week of Dec 8: peak women share for brands that usually reach men | Week of Dec 7 | Gift buyers for men at their peak |
| Week of Dec 22: women share back down | Week of Dec 21 | Gift buying over; shipping cutoffs and gift cards |
| Week of Dec 29: cheapest CPM of the season | Week of Dec 28 | Launch and test Q1 creative |

### The Danish rules change the calendar

The rules are in `offer-copy.md` ("The Danish price-marketing rules"); this is only what they do to the dates above. Not legal advice.
- **The 10-day rule:** an offer may normally be marketed for 10 days, longer only if the full period is stated from the start (and within the other limits there). A price offer as long as the study's median Black Friday run of 19 days is only possible in
  Denmark with the period stated from day one, so "early" means the creative, teasers and ads without a price, not an early price claim
- **The 30-day rule:** a December discount is measured from the lowest price in the last 30 days, which is usually the Black Friday price. The "deals shrink but continue through December" pattern needs
  planning against it
- **Urgency must be true:** urgency ads won, but only a real deadline may be used

## Picking a seasonal swipe file from the Ad Library

The study's swipe-file rule fits our evidence rules (the Ad Library shows no spend), so we use it:
- Launched in the sale window (e.g. Nov 1 to Dec 2), mentions the sale, in the market's language, has an image
- Ended by the end of January, so evergreen ads still running a year later are left out
- One ad per brand
- Ranked by days running: how long a brand kept paying for an ad is the signal. Label the rank as a proxy (`brain-system/system/evidence-and-labels.md`), never as a winner

## Where the data agrees with our practitioner docs, and where it doesn't

**Confirmed, now with numbers:**
- Adapt, don't reinvent (finding 5) → `gifting-and-q4-creative.md`, "Do not reinvent for the sale"
- The ICP flip for brands that usually reach men (finding 10) → `seasonality.md`, `gifting-and-q4-creative.md`
- Build Q1 before Q4 ends (findings 1 and 9) → `gifting-and-q4-creative.md`, phase 7
- Keep proven winners running: 31 % of Q4 winners were made before October (finding 5) → `seasonality.md`, "Keep evergreen on"

**Challenged:**
- **Gifting content winning in the season.** `seasonality.md` and `gifting-and-q4-creative.md` carry the practitioner view that gift-giving and receiving content performs especially well. Inside brands,
  gifting ads did no better than the brand's other ads (finding 11). Treat it as a test, not a default
- **Evergreen beating sale ads in the season.** In Black Friday week, ads built around the deal beat the brand's other ads at twice as many brands as they lost at (finding 7). The two fit together:
  keep the evergreen winners, and put the deal on them for the sale week
- **Cyber Monday as a second wave** (finding 8) → now optional in `offer-copy.md`
- **The two-week recalibration heuristic** in `gifting-and-q4-creative.md`: across brands, Meta picked most winners within a week (finding 4). The heuristic is about a shift in who the ad is shown to;
  the delivery read itself shouldn't wait two weeks

**Open, to test on a brand:** whether a deal-led static headline beats a product headline with an offer bar (`offer-copy.md` says the season is a tag; finding 13 says static winners often led with the
deal). Logged in `.claude/skills/offer-ads/feedback.md`.

## Limits

- US brands, bigger than most Danish ones, in US dollars
- Spend is Meta's choice and can reinforce itself; an ad Meta funds early gets more chances to win
- Every ad ID counts as a new ad
- Format, feeling and deal come from the vendor's AI tags, with no accuracy figure given
- One season (Q4 2025). Re-check when a newer study or the brand's own season report exists. Re-check by October 2027
