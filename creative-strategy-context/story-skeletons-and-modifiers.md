# Story skeletons and communication modifiers

Two craft tools for scripts that don't sound like an ad. The skeleton comes from organic content outside the brand's niche; the modifier is the one storytelling mechanism stacked on top of a plain, factual
baseline. Used by `scriptwriting/processes/script-from-cross-niche-story.md`.

Source: a practitioner's public video on writing UGC scripts (Sarah Levinger, "Here's How to Never Write Another Boring Ad Script Again", YouTube). Her claims are *Stated* (one practitioner, no data shown).
The persuasion-knowledge argument she cites (Friestad and Wright, 1994) is published research; the "a tenth of a second" figure is hers and we have not checked it. The method below is our version.

## Why

A script that follows the usual attention, interest, desire, action order is recognised as an ad before the hook lands, and people then discount what it says. Two fixes: a story shape the viewer
knows from organic content, and a storytelling mechanism that gives the script emotion a fact sheet doesn't have.

## Single-task writing

One job per step, never the whole brief in one prompt:

1. Read the product page and say what you see. Facts only: what it is, who it's for, why it works.
2. Write a plain baseline script at the target length. It is meant to be boring. It is the fact layer.
3. Add one layer at a time (a skeleton, then a modifier, then the brand voice). Each layer is its own step so the result of the last one can be checked before the next goes on.

## Story skeleton (cross-niche)

A skeleton is what the video *does*, with every word of the original thrown away.

Take a high-engagement organic video from a niche **unrelated** to the brand's. Competitors' ads in the brand's own niche are the wrong source: copying them makes ads fatigue together with theirs.
(For a breakout in the brand's own niche, use `script-from-organic-niche` instead.)

Extract, per beat: what happens, how long it runs, what escalates or turns, and the emotion the viewer is in. Then name the pattern in one line (example from the source video: *serial escalation*, each
line tops the last and the pattern breaks about every three seconds). The skeleton is saved as beats and a pattern name, not as dialogue.

**Never carry over:** the original's wording, jokes, names, music, or a recognisable scene. Copy the story underneath, not the script. If a line from the original survives into the new script, the step failed.

## Communication modifiers

A modifier is one storytelling mechanism named to the model. One per script; stacking two blurs both.

| Modifier | What it does | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Forbidden knowledge | Walks the viewer through something they don't know but want to | Must be true. A "secret" that is a claim fails the claims check |
| Anti-hero positioning | The speaker is the flawed or unlikely voice, not the expert | Brand voice profile must allow it |
| Humor | Laughs carry the facts | Humor that needs the product name to land is an ad again |
| Absurdity | An exaggerated or ridiculous frame around a real fact | The fact must stay recognisable |

The list is open. Add a modifier here when a run uses one that held; log it in the skill's `feedback.md` first.

## The swap test (mandatory)

Read the finished script with the brand name replaced by a competitor's. If it still reads correctly, it is generic and goes back for another round. Fix by pulling more of the brand's own specifics
(customer verbatims, product detail, signature moves from the voice profile), not by adding adjectives. Run by a reviewer that did not write the script.

## Honesty and compliance

The modifier and the skeleton change the shape, never the facts. Claims, prices and health statements follow the brand lens and, for Denmark, the Danish rules in the skill. A skeleton that depended on a
trick or a misleading demo is rebuilt honestly, as in `script-from-organic-niche`.
