# Text-led: prompts

Rules for building the final prompt: `../production.md`. In this category the headline or the
copy does the work; the product supports it or isn't shown at all. Our own feedback backs it: the
"We're Sorry" letter was the best of the first test batch and the text meme worked first time, and
Alex Cooper's top course ad was plain text on white. The copy has to be strong enough to carry the
ad, so spend the approval time here.

## From the Adcrate playbook

### #1 · Headline
Suggested ratio: 4:5
Example image: `pb-01-headline.png`

Prompt: A static ad with a [BACKGROUND] background. Top third: large bold condensed sans-serif
headline "[HEADLINE, under 10 words]". Below in smaller [ACCENT COLOR] text: "[SUBHEAD, one
sentence]". Bottom half: [OUR PRODUCT] on [SURFACE] with [PROPS]. Shot at 50mm f/2.8 from slightly
above. Brand logo bottom right. Clean, authoritative.

Must be sourced: nothing numeric by default. Claims in the subhead come from the brand.

### #21 · Bold statement / reaction headline
Suggested ratio: 1:1 (the example is 4:5)
Example image: `pb-21-bold-statement.png`

Prompt: A vibrant [GRADIENT] background flowing diagonally from upper left to lower right. Upper
left: an oversized playful [FONT STYLE like rounded retro serif] white headline "[BOLD
STATEMENT]", loose, fun and expressive, not rigid or corporate. Right side: [PERSON DETAIL like a
hand reaching down from upper right] grabbing from [OUR PRODUCT or its contents]. Product center
right, slightly below the midline. Bottom left: brand logo with "[PRODUCT DESCRIPTOR]" in smaller
text below. No stats, no reviews, no badges: the gradient and the headline do the work.

Must be sourced: nothing numeric. The tone (e.g. censored swearing) has to fit `brand-lens.md`.

### #23 · Long-form manifesto / letter
Suggested ratio: 1:1 or 4:5
Example image: `pb-23-manifesto-letter.png`

Prompt: A copy-dominant ad on a clean white background; no background imagery. Top 15%: an
oversized bold black headline "[PROVOCATIVE HEADLINE like They're worth every dollar.]". Below:
left-aligned body copy in smaller regular black text, short punchy sentences with line breaks, not
paragraphs: "[BODY, 12-18 lines, verbatim]". The argument acknowledges the objection, lists what
would be lost by cutting corners, reframes it as a positive, and closes with a confident brand
statement. Bottom 20%: [OUR PRODUCT] on white, clean studio angle. No icons, no badges, no CTA
button. The writing is the ad.

Must be sourced: every price and fact in the body. A price comparison with other brands has to be
real and like-for-like.

### #39 · Curiosity gap / scroll-stopper hook
Suggested ratio: 1:1
Example image: `pb-39-curiosity-gap.png`
Reference use: visual tone only. No product, no logo, no branding.

Prompt: Top 35%: clean white background with large bold black sans-serif text, heavy weight,
tight leading: "[HOOK like Most [AUDIENCE] don't realize THIS is why [PROBLEM] but did you
know...]", followed by "more" in lighter gray, like a truncated Facebook caption. Bottom 65%: a
close-up, slightly uncomfortable photo of [PROBLEM VISUAL], real and editorial rather than stock,
shallow depth of field. No text on the photo, no product, no logo, no CTA.

Must be sourced: whatever the hook implies must be true and backed up on the landing page.
