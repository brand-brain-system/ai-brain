**Layout forensics.** A black ticker bar with small target-icon bullets runs the full width at the very top, scrolling all-caps sale copy that deliberately bleeds off both edges — a device borrowed from breaking-news graphics to signal "this is happening now" before the viewer reads anything else. Below it, on a cream-to-peach ground, the brand's icon-and-wordmark logo sits centered, then a bold black sentence-case headline beneath it with a loose hand-drawn underline squiggle — the biggest, highest-contrast text on the frame, so it's the first real read once the eye drops past the ticker. The bulk of the frame is a grid of animal photographs, each cropped into a rounded arch and paired with a matching beaded bracelet swatch along its bottom edge — eight different species across the grid, which is the money shot for a product whose whole appeal is variety and choice. Nested in the grid's center cell, breaking the photo pattern, sits a bright green circular price tag with a struck-through old price above a larger new price — the one color pop against an otherwise muted, naturalistic palette, which is what pulls it into third read despite being physically the smallest element.

**The story this ad tells.** This wants the viewer to believe there's a real, wide choice of animals to support and track, and that the deal is steep enough to be worth acting on today rather than browsing later. The grid does the congruency work for "track a real animal" — it shows the actual range of species and the matching bracelet for each, so the promise and the picture are the same thing. The ticker and the green price tag exist purely to inject urgency and a hard number into what would otherwise read as a calm, browsable collection page.

**Copy skeleton.** Ticker: names the sale event, repeating label mechanic, no fixed word count (scrolling loop). Logo: brand mark, no persuasive copy. Headline: states the core mechanic in plain language, command mechanic, 4 words. Photo grid: no copy, visual proof of variety. Price tag: anchors the discount with a before-and-after price, comparison mechanic, 3 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs several distinct SKUs or variants that can be shown as a grid, plus lifestyle or reference photography for each — it does not work for a single-SKU brand without either faking variety it doesn't have or dropping to a smaller grid. A brand without a defensible "track a real X" mechanic should replace the headline with whatever its own collection actually delivers, since the headline is a direct restatement of the mechanic, not a generic hook. The center price tag can carry percent off, a bundle price, or a value-stack callout with copy changes only; the ticker is the easiest zone to drop entirely for a brand without a live event to announce, since it adds urgency but carries no product information.
