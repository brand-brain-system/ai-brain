**Layout forensics.** Shot straight down onto grass, the frame reads as a found object rather than a designed ad. The product runs diagonally through the upper two-thirds of the frame, cuff folded over at the top to expose the brand label, with a tiny white logo mark tucked into the upper-right corner as the only polished touch. Below the sock, a white index card is pinned down with a small wooden clothespin and set at a slight tilt, carrying handwritten marker text that shifts from black to red mid-phrase. Overlapping its bottom-right corner, a smaller sky-blue sticky note carries a second handwritten line in blue marker, angled the opposite way from the card above it. The red ink against the white card is the highest-contrast, most saturated element in an otherwise green-and-grey frame, so it earns the first look; the blue sticky note's offer line follows since its color and shape distinguish it from the card behind it; the sock itself, despite occupying the most physical space, reads third because its muted grey blends with the grass; the small logo is last.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe this is a real customer's photo, not a studio shoot — the outdoor setting, the clothespin, the handwriting all argue for authenticity before a single sales word is read. The two-note structure (card, then sticky note layered on top) mimics how someone would actually jot down a sale and then add an afterthought, which makes the offer feel discovered rather than pitched.

**Copy skeleton.** Headline: names the sale event in handwritten form, three words, color-shift for emphasis on the event name. Offer: states the buy-more-get-more mechanic in full, six words, plain numeric command written as if jotted down quickly.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs one clean product shot that can be laid flat outdoors and a plausible handwritten-note aesthetic — any single-SKU product that photographs well on a natural surface can carry it. The buy-X-get-Y line swaps cleanly to a percent-off, dollar-off, or bundle claim without touching the card-and-sticky-note structure. What breaks the layout: a product that needs to be shown in use or in a lifestyle scene rather than laid flat, since the outdoor-grass staging only works for something with a flat presentable side; a review count or press mention would also clash with the handwritten authenticity conceit and should not be added here.
