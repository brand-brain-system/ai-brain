**Layout forensics.** A tall, dark teal-to-black gradient with a visible grain texture fills the whole frame, giving the ad a moody, almost velvet feel that suits an alternative-fashion brand. The wordmark sits near the top in a gothic display serif with a small star mark, centered and restrained. Below it, a small letter-spaced eyebrow line in plain caps names the sale occasion, set small and quiet compared to what follows. The centerpiece is a large three-line display treatment: a stacked mixed-weight composition where a script "up to" leads into a huge decorative serif percentage, which runs directly into a bold italic word closing the line, then a second huge line of decorative serif type below it spanning nearly the full width. This whole block dominates the middle of the frame through sheer scale and typographic flourish rather than color. Below that, a solid teal band spans the full width, holding two lines of italic-and-bold white serif text, the only hard color block on the page. Eye path: the giant decorative headline first (scale and the ornate mixed-weight serif treatment), the solid teal banner second (the only flat color block, positioned to catch the eye once the headline has been read), the small eyebrow and logo last, functioning as context rather than the sell.

**The story this ad tells.** This ad leans entirely on brand-voice typography to sell a sitewide discount, using the same gothic-romantic display type the brand uses everywhere else so the sale still feels like their aesthetic rather than a generic sale template. The teal banner strip at the bottom stacks a second, smaller discount on top of the first, which is a classic layered-offer move (headline discount, then a code for extra off) that rewards a reader who keeps scrolling past the big type. It works because the ornate type treatment is doing double duty as both brand identity and scroll-stop device, with no product or photo needed at all.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow — names the sale occasion, sets calendar context, 2 words. Headline — the core percent-off claim, styled as the brand's decorative display type, 5 words. Offer — the layered secondary discount plus its unlock code, 6 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs nothing but a strong display typeface and a solid-color band to work, so any brand with a distinct type identity can run it as-is; a brand without an ornate display face would need to simplify the headline treatment to a single weight, since the current three-line stacked composition depends on typographic flourish to earn its scale. The teal banner's layered discount-plus-code swaps cleanly to free shipping, a bundle, or a straight urgency line with a copy change only, since it's a flat color block with text, not a fixed device. This does not need a product shot, a face, or proof of any kind, which makes it easy to produce but entirely dependent on the brand's type system carrying the whole ad.
