**Layout forensics.** A moody black-to-red smoky studio backdrop fills the frame, with a faint, oversized outline of the word "SALE" repeated diagonally as a watermark behind everything else, visible but never competing for attention. At the top edge, a red ribbon banner appears taped down at both ends, angled slightly, carrying a white script line across it. Below, the product sits centred on a round black pedestal: the device stands upright with its small digital display glowing against the dark backdrop, its retail box positioned beside it, and two nozzle attachments plus a neatly coiled charging cable laid out in front on the pedestal surface. Dramatic red rim lighting traces the edges of the device and box, separating them from the dark background. First read is the product itself, since the glowing display and the red rim light create the highest local contrast in an otherwise dark frame. Second is the red ribbon banner at top, a bold color block that reads clearly even at a glance. Third is the SALE watermark, deliberately low-contrast and only noticed on a second look.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe this is a premium, tech-forward product caught in a rare, closing window of a discount — the dramatic lighting and glowing display do the premium-positioning work, while the taped ribbon banner and the watermark behind it both push the urgency without ever showing a countdown or a price. The pairing works for an offer because the product is allowed to look expensive and desirable first, so the "last call" framing reads as a shame to miss rather than a discount hunt.

**Copy skeleton.** Badge: name the sale and the deadline framing, mechanic is a short exclamation, 7 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a hero product that can be lit dramatically and paired with its own accessories staged in front of it; a product with no natural accessory set can still run the layout with just the device and its box. The ribbon-banner urgency device requires a real early-access or closing-window claim to stay honest — without one, swap the banner copy for a plain "Now Available" line and set urgency_device to false rather than keeping "Last Call" language with nothing behind it. The SALE watermark is a pure decorative texture and can be swapped for the brand's own wordmark repeated the same way, or dropped entirely, without affecting the rest of the layout.
