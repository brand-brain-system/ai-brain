**Layout forensics.** A small logo mark sits centred at the top, modest in scale, over a large two-line headline set in bold black serif that spans most of the frame width — a classic-newspaper feel rather than a modern sans treatment, which gives the whole ad a slightly more editorial, trustworthy weight. Directly below, an oversized green serif numeral breaks left-aligned across two lines, roughly matching the headline's visual weight but distinguished purely by color. Underneath that, three short blocks of body copy run down the left third of the frame in a loose vertical stack, each two lines, with one block using bold green for a key phrase inside otherwise-black text. On the right side, a small black circular badge sits tilted, ringed with white text reading like a seal of assurance, positioned just above and left of the product. The product itself — a neon-yellow-and-black cordless tool — is shot at a three-quarter angle taking up the right half and cutting into the lower half of the frame, angled so its nozzle sends a rendered blue air-stream and scattered leaf particles diagonally across the left side, physically tying the product to the copy block it points toward. The background is a soft pale-green-to-white gradient with faint vignetting, giving depth without introducing a real photographed scene. The eye lands on the black serif headline first, both for its top position and its size. Second is the green offer numeral, which is nearly as large but reads a half-beat later because it's positioned lower and shares the frame with the body copy blocks starting beside it. Third is the product itself, whose neon color and diagonal motion lines pull the eye across and down. Fourth is the small circular badge, noticed last as a confidence check rather than a hook.

**The story this ad tells.** This layout wants the viewer to believe a single, versatile tool is worth a large discount right now — headline and offer numeral do the emotional lift (sale is live, savings are big) while the three stacked body-copy lines do the rational selling one claim at a time, each one visually tied back to the product by the rendered air-stream crossing the frame. The guarantee badge is placed close to the product rather than near the price, which reads as "this tool itself is safe to buy" rather than "this discount is safe to trust." It works as an offer ad because the product's own visual effect (the air blast) becomes the connective tissue between copy and photo instead of a static hero shot sitting inert next to a list.

**Copy skeleton.**
- headline: announce the sale is live, statement, 5 words
- offer: name the discount, number, 4 words
- subhead 1: name a core benefit, short phrase, 6 words
- subhead 2: name a second benefit with one bolded phrase, short phrase, 5 words
- subhead 3: create urgency around the window, short phrase, 5 words
- badge: reassure on risk, seal text, 4 words

**Adaptation notes.** This layout depends on a product that can visually "do something" in-frame — a rendered air-stream, light, or motion effect that crosses toward the copy block. A product without that kind of effect can substitute a simple diagonal light-flare or motion-blur device in the same position and keep the connective read. The giant offer numeral and guarantee badge both survive a full mechanic swap: percent off, dollar off, a bundle price, or a buy-X-get-Y line can all sit in the same numeral slot. What breaks the layout: removing the guarantee badge without replacing it with any proof element leaves the product-trust question unanswered, since there's no review or press callout anywhere else in the frame to compensate.
