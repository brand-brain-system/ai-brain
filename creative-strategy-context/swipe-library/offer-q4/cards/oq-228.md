**Layout forensics.** The frame is a deep purple radial gradient, darker at the corners and lighter toward the centre, which does the job a spotlight would in a photographed scene without needing an actual studio backdrop. A short eyebrow line in small white caps sits centred near the top, functioning as a pre-headline claim rather than a hook on its own. Below it, a single huge white bold numeral-and-word pairing spans most of the frame width — this is the entire offer, rendered at a scale that makes it the largest single mark on the page by a wide margin. The product sits centred in the middle of the frame: a metallic charging case shown open at an angle with its two earbuds floating just above it in mid-assembly, one still nested in its slot, lit with a soft glow that plays off the metal finish. A short bold subhead sits below the product, then the logo anchors bottom-left and an outlined pill CTA anchors bottom-right, the two balancing each other at the base of the frame. Nothing in this layout uses a photograph in the traditional sense — gradient, product render, and type are all that's here, which keeps the whole thing feeling premium and controlled rather than busy. The eye lands on the giant white numeral first, both for scale and because it's the highest-contrast mark against the purple field. Second is the product, pulled toward the centre by the glow lighting and the floating-parts detail, which reads as a small moment of drama. Third is the subhead beneath it, and fourth the logo/CTA pairing at the base, functioning as a close rather than a hook.

**The story this ad tells.** This layout wants the viewer to believe the discount is the single most important fact before anything about the product itself registers — the numeral is scaled so large it reads before the eyebrow line above it does. The product's floating-parts presentation (case open, buds mid-lift) does quiet work suggesting quality and precision engineering without a single word of copy, which matters because the subhead is short and generic rather than a benefit list. It works as a pure offer-and-hero-shot ad because there's no competing proof element or urgency device pulling focus; the whole frame is built around one number and one beautifully lit object.

**Copy skeleton.**
- eyebrow: pre-frame the deal as significant, claim, 6 words
- offer: name the discount, number, 2 words
- subhead: name the core positioning, short phrase, 4 words
- cta: tell them what to do, command, 5 words

**Adaptation notes.** This layout is one of the most portable in the set: it needs only a single hero product that can be rendered or shot with a soft glow against a dark gradient, no lifestyle scene, no second SKU, no proof element. The giant numeral slot is currently a currency amount off rather than a percentage — worth flagging, since the row's tagged mechanic (percent off) doesn't actually match what's drawn here, so any brand reusing this layout should decide up front whether the giant numeral will read as a percent, a dollar amount, or a bundle price, since the type treatment differs slightly between a "%" and a "$" glyph at this scale. Free-gift or bundle callouts can slot into the subhead position with copy changes only. What doesn't survive: adding a second product or a busier background would compete with the deliberately spare, one-object-one-number composition this layout is built around.
