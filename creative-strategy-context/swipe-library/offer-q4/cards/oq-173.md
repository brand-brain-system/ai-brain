**Layout forensics.** Three stacked lines of grunge-texture, all-caps type carry the whole top half against a plain cream ground, each line a different color: green, then mustard-gold, then burnt terracotta. The gold middle line reads as the largest and brightest of the three, so it's the first thing the eye lands on even though all three sit at a similar scale — brightness is doing the work that size usually does. The terracotta line beneath resolves the gold word into a real claim by naming the discount. Below that, two glass jars sit side by side on the same cream ground: one shot from directly above with the lid off so the product texture is visible, one closed and facing camera so the label and logo read clearly — together they're the money shot, since a topical balm has to show its texture to make the "natural alternative" claim credible. A solid mustard-yellow bar spans the full width at the bottom, black bold type restating the sale with the promo code underlined so it's the one piece of type the viewer is meant to copy. Nothing else on the frame — no proof, no photography, no decoration — competes with these four blocks.

**The story this ad tells.** The ad wants the viewer to believe an unfamiliar natural product does the job of a well-known cosmetic procedure, and that the reason to act now is a live discount code. The gold headline borrows credibility from the comparison before the viewer has read anything about the actual product, the two jar angles prove the texture and packaging are real, and the yellow footer bar hands over the exact code to use. Congruency here rides entirely on the type-to-product handoff: the headline promises transformation, the jars deliver plain, tactile, natural-looking proof, so the gap between claim and image stays small.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow: opens the comparison, single word, label mechanic, 1 word. Headline: names the borrowed category, single word, label mechanic, 1 word. Offer (subhead): states the discount plainly, number mechanic, 4 words. Product: no copy, visual proof only. Footer offer: restates the sale and hands over the mechanic, code mechanic, 6 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a product that photographs well both from above (texture) and face-on (label), so it fits topical or jar-format goods better than a bottle or a box. A single-jar brand can drop to product_count 1 without breaking the frame — the layout survives on one hero shot, it just loses the texture-versus-label pairing. The borrowed-category headline ("Nature's X") is the riskiest swap: it only works where the comparison is defensible, and a brand without a clean comparison should replace it with a benefit headline instead of forcing an analogy. The code-box footer can carry percent off, dollar off, or a bundle offer with copy changes only; it cannot carry a review count or star rating without adding a zone the layout doesn't currently have.
