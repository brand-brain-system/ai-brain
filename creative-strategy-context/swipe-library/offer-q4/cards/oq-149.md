**Layout forensics.** Two identical diagonal gradient ribbons pin the frame at the top and bottom edges, each running magenta into blue with white star-bulleted text that reads as a repeating loop rather than a single sentence — a framing device more than a message. Between them, the center of the frame is dominated by a huge condensed red all-caps headline, by far the largest and most saturated text on the page, sitting directly under a smaller black subhead that names the sale before the headline delivers the urgency. To the left, a solid red circle badge stacks a three-line claim in white bold caps, its shape distinct enough to read as a second, separate offer from the headline above it. To the right of the badge, a woman mid-exercise on black equipment anchors the bottom half of the frame against the plain gradient studio floor. First read is the red headline (size and color both max out here), second is the red circle badge (same hot color, different shape, close enough to the headline to be read as its continuation), third is the product-in-use photo, fourth is the black subhead, which is deliberately quieter so it doesn't compete with the headline directly above it.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to feel the clock running out before anything else registers — the giant red "ends tonight" claim is built to be read from a full scroll away. The circular badge and the product photo then answer the two follow-up questions urgency creates: how much am I actually saving, and what is this for. The top-and-bottom banner ribbons function like caution tape, framing the whole offer as an event rather than a static promotion.

**Copy skeleton.** Badge (banner): restates the sale and the percent-off ceiling as a repeating loop, six words, list-style fragment. Subhead: names the sale plainly, three words, flat declarative tag. Headline: states the deadline in the most urgent terms possible, two words, command. Badge (circle): stacks the dollar-savings ceiling with a limited-time qualifier, seven words, number-led claim.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a genuine action photo of someone using the product — a static packshot in the product zone would flatten the energy the whole page is built around. The urgency headline and the percent/dollar badges swap cleanly to any equivalent claim, and either the badge or the banner can carry a buy-X-get-Y or bundle mechanic instead of a straight discount with no structural change. What breaks the layout: removing the deadline language from the headline zone, since "ends tonight" is the entire reason this template reads as urgent rather than as a generic sale banner; softening that line to a plain sale announcement collapses the hierarchy this card is built around.
