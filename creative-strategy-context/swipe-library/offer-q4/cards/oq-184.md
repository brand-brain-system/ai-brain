**Layout forensics.** The whole frame is a screenshot mockup of an AI chat app, and the format itself is the scroll-stop — it reads as a real interface before it reads as an ad. A thin header bar at top carries the app's own chrome: menu icon, app name, edit and profile icons, exactly where a real app would put them. Below a tiny gray disclaimer line, a rounded light-grey bubble holds the user's question, right-aligned the way a sent message sits in any chat app. The answer beneath has no bubble at all, sitting plain and left-aligned the way an AI response actually renders, with the brand name and the discount percentage set bold inside otherwise plain black text — that bold pair is the actual message of the ad, dressed up as an incidental detail inside a conversation. A row of small gray utility icons closes out the bottom, copied straight from the real app, which is doing more work for credibility than any headline could. First read is the bolded brand-and-percentage inside the answer, since bold-on-plain is the strongest local contrast on the frame; second is the question bubble that sets it up; third is the chrome that makes the whole thing believable.

**The story this ad tells.** This wants the viewer to believe an AI assistant independently recommended this brand, unprompted by advertising, and that the discount is just something it happened to mention. The format's congruency depends entirely on looking exactly like the real app — any deviation in icon placement, bubble shape, or type would break the illusion and the trust it's borrowing. There's no product shot, no offer badge, no decoration, because a designed element anywhere on this frame would immediately signal "ad" and undo the whole premise.

**Copy skeleton.** Disclaimer: incidental app chrome, no persuasive purpose. Quote (question): sets up the recommendation, question mechanic, 8 words. Headline (answer): delivers the brand recommendation and the discount inline, comparison-plus-number mechanic, 15 words. Chrome zones: no copy, purely format authenticity.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs no product photography and no logo at all — the entire asset is typeset UI chrome and copy, which makes it cheap to produce but entirely dependent on the AI-chat conceit staying current and plausible. A brand can swap the question to match its own category ("what's the best X for Y?") and the answer to name itself and its offer, but the discount has to be real and matched to what's live, since this format reads as neutral third-party endorsement and an unverifiable claim here is worse than in a normal ad. This does not adapt well to a brand whose product needs to be seen to be believed — anything visual or texture-dependent belongs in a different template, not this one.

**Prompt skeleton.** Create: a 9:16 static ad styled as a screenshot of a generic AI chat assistant app. Top: app header bar chrome with a menu icon, centered app name, and edit and profile icons. Below a tiny gray disclaimer line: a right-aligned rounded light-grey chat bubble containing "{{copy.question}}" in dark sans. Below, left-aligned with no bubble: "{{copy.answer}}" in plain black sans, with "{{brand.name}}" and "{{copy.discount}}" set bold. Bottom: a row of small gray utility icons (copy, speak, thumbs up, thumbs down, regenerate, share). Rendered as a flat, pixel-accurate app screenshot, no camera or lighting effects. Neutral, credible, incidental mood. 9:16 aspect ratio.
