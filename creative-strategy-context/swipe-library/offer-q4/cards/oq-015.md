**Layout forensics.** A warm, faintly paper-textured off-white backdrop holds a thin row of struck-through words at the very top, each one crossed out with a single clean line, naming things the product is positioned against. Beneath it, a large bold black sans headline runs two centred lines and is the single biggest, darkest type block on the page. A small bold caps subhead sits directly under the headline, with one numeral set noticeably larger than the words around it. The bottom of the frame is a row of three bottles, cropped tight at the frame edge, each with a different shade of green liquid dripping down over the label from the cap, giving the product itself a visceral, almost splattered texture. A tiny grey italic disclaimer runs vertically along the right edge, small enough to read as a legal footnote rather than a design element. First read is the black headline, since it carries the most ink and the most contrast against the light backdrop. Second is the dripping bottle row, whose saturated green and glossy liquid texture pulls the eye down even though it sits lower in the frame. Third is the small numeral-led subhead just above the bottles. Fourth is the struck-through comparison row at the top, thin and low-contrast enough to be skimmed past on a fast scroll.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe this product replaces several worse habits at once, not just one — the strike-through row does the dismissing, the bold headline does the promising, and the dripping bottle imagery makes the product itself look freshly made and almost edible rather than manufactured. The pairing works for an offer because the risk-free discount line sits quietly between the promise and the product, so it reads as a low-stakes way to test the bigger claim rather than the main event.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow: dismiss the alternatives being replaced, mechanic is a struck-through list, 4 words. Headline: name the benefit being promised, mechanic is a two-line declarative, 4 words. Subhead: pair the discount with a risk-reversal line, mechanic is a short claim with an embedded numeral, 5 words. Disclaimer: state the required regulatory line, mechanic is a standard legal statement, 14 words.

**Adaptation notes.** The struck-through comparison row needs real, specific alternatives the product is replacing to avoid reading as generic; a brand with a less pointed positioning can drop this row entirely and let the headline carry the promise alone, which still leaves three usable zones. The dripping-liquid product treatment only works for a product that is genuinely liquid or can be styled that way; a capsule, powder, or solid product should swap this for a clean packshot row instead, since the drip is a texture device tied specifically to this product form. The percent-off line is a plain-text callout with no distinct device shape, so it swaps freely to any other mechanic in mechanics_compatible with copy changes only.
