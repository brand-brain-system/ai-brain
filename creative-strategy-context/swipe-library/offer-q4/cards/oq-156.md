**Layout forensics.** The frame is a single lifestyle photo of cookware on a speckled countertop with two strips of blue paper tape layered over the top third, each carrying black marker hand-lettering. The first strip sits centered and slightly higher, the second directly beneath it offset a few degrees, so the two read as one deliberately imperfect, torn-edge message rather than a clean printed headline. Below the tape, the product sits lower-center-right, angled toward camera with its dark non-stick interior catching the light, while a second, out-of-focus pan leans against the matching speckled wall behind it, reinforcing the range without competing with the hero piece. First read is the top tape strip (cornflower blue against a warm pink counter is the sharpest color contrast in the frame, plus top position), second is the second tape strip directly under it, third is the product itself, which benefits from soft natural side light but is visually quieter than the tape above it.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to feel like they caught a handwritten note left on the counter, mid-use, rather than an ad — the tape, the torn edges, and the marker lettering all argue for a real kitchen rather than a set. The deadline language does the actual selling work; the product photo is there to remind the viewer what the sale applies to, not to make the sale itself.

**Copy skeleton.** Headline: names the sale plainly, three words, flat declarative tag. Subhead: states the deadline pressure, two words, short urgency fragment.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs one product that photographs well staged casually on a kitchen surface — cookware, kitchen tools, and similar countertop goods carry it easily; anything that can't be shot "in the kitchen" convincingly breaks the conceit. The deadline language swaps to a percent-off, dollar-off, or sitewide claim on a third tape strip with no structural change, since the tape-strip device can carry any short claim. What breaks the layout: adding a numeral or percent claim without a strip to hold it, since the two-tape structure is the whole visual system here — a bare price floating over the photo would look like an error rather than a design choice.
