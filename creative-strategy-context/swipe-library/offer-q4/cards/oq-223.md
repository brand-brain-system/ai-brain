**Layout forensics.** The frame is bookended top and bottom by identical full-width cobalt marquee bars carrying a repeating small-caps phrase, framing the whole ad like a ticker on a trading screen. Inside that frame, everything sits on a pale, almost-white ground: a small centred wordmark, then a giant two-word headline in bold cobalt sans set large enough to nearly span the frame width, then a medium two-line subhead with one clause bolded for emphasis, then a cobalt pill CTA button. Below the copy stack, a tilted cobalt circle badge sits to the left of centre with two lines of white bold text inside it — the classic sticker-badge device, rotated a few degrees off-axis to read as applied rather than printed. The product occupies the lower half: a single glossy red object, shot frontally with soft even shadow, rendered in full saturation against the pale ground and the cobalt type, making it the only warm-colored thing in the whole ad. A tiny disclaimer line sits bottom-left in the same cobalt as everything else, easy to miss on purpose. The eye lands on the giant headline first, both for its size and because cobalt-on-white is the highest-contrast pairing after the marquee bars. Second is the product, which wins on color alone — a single saturated red object against an otherwise blue-and-white palette pulls the eye immediately. Third is the badge, whose circular shape and rotation make it read as a discrete callout rather than body copy. The subhead and CTA follow, doing the explaining after the hook has landed.

**The story this ad tells.** This layout wants the viewer to believe the free-gift mechanic before they process anything about the underlying sale — "FREE GIFT" is set as large as a brand's biggest promise gets, and the actual candle earns its keep purely through color contrast rather than size. The badge exists to answer the one objection a free-gift claim invites (what's it actually worth), stamping a value figure right next to the product so the math is visible without reading the subhead. The marquee bars top and bottom do double duty as a seasonal timestamp and a visual frame that keeps the eye contained inside the ad. It works for a gift-with-purchase promotion because the whole hierarchy is built to answer, in order: what do I get free, what does it look like, what's it worth, and only then, what do I have to spend to get it.

**Copy skeleton.**
- ui_chrome (top and bottom marquee): timestamp the sale, repeating phrase, 3 words
- headline: announce the free-gift mechanic, two-word hook, 2 words
- subhead: name the underlying discount and the gift condition, sentence, 11 words
- cta: tell them what to do, command, 2 words
- badge: anchor the gift's value, number, 2 words
- disclaimer: state the qualifying condition, small print, 4 words

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs one hero product that photographs well as a single saturated object against a pale-and-blue palette — swapping in a lineup or a lifestyle scene would break the color-contrast trick the whole ad depends on. The free-gift mechanic can become a bundle discount or a buy-X-get-Y offer with copy changes only, since the headline and badge slots are both generic "big claim plus value anchor" zones. What doesn't survive: dropping the badge removes the only proof the gift has real value, so a brand without a defensible retail-value figure should replace the badge with a guarantee seal rather than leave the claim unsupported.
