**Layout forensics.** The whole frame is a single candid photo with three text callouts layered on top, no separate product or background zones. Top-left, a neon yellow-green speech-bubble sits over the photo carrying a short line in bold black type — its shape and color read as a text-message aesthetic rather than an ad. The photo itself is a two-person selfie-style shot, both in plain white tees, each holding up a tiny sample cup toward camera, shot at close range with visible depth in the room behind them. Centered lower-middle, a black rounded speech-bubble with bold white type sits directly between the two people's faces, the highest-contrast single element on the page. Along the bottom edge, a second neon banner in the same yellow-green repeats the callout style to carry the offer line. First read is the black-and-white centered bubble (contrast plus dead-center placement), second is the two faces themselves (a UGC photo always earns a look), third is the bottom neon offer banner, fourth is the top-left neon eyebrow, which the Z-path default would normally hit first but the photo's human subjects out-compete it here.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to read this as a message from a friend, not an ad — the speech-bubble chrome, the plain tees, the un-staged room all say "real people, real reaction." The offer sits at the bottom almost as an afterthought, which is the point: the photo and the callouts carry the relatability, and the discount is there for whoever reads all the way down.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow: teases something new before naming the sale, four words, casual fragment. Headline: announces the sale by name in first person, six words, plain declarative sentence. Offer: states a savings ceiling tied to the brand's own product line, seven words, number-led claim.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a genuine two-person (or one-person) UGC-style photo with visible faces and hands — it does not survive a swap to a studio packshot, since the whole design is built to look unposed. The speech-bubble chrome and neon banner are cosmetic and swap freely to any brand's accent color. The Buy-X-get-Y savings figure converts cleanly to a percent-off, dollar-off, or bundle claim with no structural change, since the offer band only ever carries a short savings line. What breaks the layout: any brand without willing on-camera talent, since there is no packshot fallback built into this template.
