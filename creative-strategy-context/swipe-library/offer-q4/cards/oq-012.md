**Layout forensics.** No discount, shipping, or price device is drawn anywhere on this crop; the only mechanic the frame actually carries is the implied value stack of three product variants shown together in the bottom row. A soft lavender-to-blush diagonal gradient with a faint grain texture fills the frame. Centred near the top, a two-word headline mixes a loose brown cursive script for the first word with a bold black sans for the second, so the two words read as a spoken question rather than a display headline. Below it, a single large amber-and-white capsule icon stands alone and isolated in open space — this is not the brand's own product, it is a stand-in prop representing the thing being compared against. A small cursive line with a thin curved arrow sits beneath the capsule, pointing down and to the right toward the actual product. The bottom of the visible content is a row of three bottles, cropped tight at the frame edge, each capped in a different color with a dripping liquid effect. Below that crop line, roughly the lower half of the 9:16 canvas is flat, unrendered cream space with no content at all, which reads as a partial or cover-frame crop of a video creative rather than a finished static. First read is the mixed-script headline, since it is the largest type and sits in the most open part of the frame. Second is the amber capsule, an isolated high-contrast color shape with nothing competing near it. Third is the small cursive arrow line, which functions as a connector rather than a destination. Fourth is the bottle row, which is partially cropped and therefore reads last despite being the actual product.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe they are being offered a gentler, more familiar-feeling alternative to something harsher they already know about — the cursive-and-sans mixed headline sounds like a friend asking a question, and the isolated capsule prop lets the comparison happen without the brand ever having to show or name a competitor's packaging. The arrow does the work of physically walking the eye from the thing being replaced to the thing replacing it, which matters for a comparison angle because it makes the substitution feel like a discovery rather than a pitch.

**Copy skeleton.** Headline: pose the comparison as a casual question, mechanic is a two-word question, 2 words. Cta: instruct the viewer toward the real product, mechanic is a short command, 3 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a comparison anchor that is safe to depict as a generic icon rather than a named or branded competitor product, and multiple variant SKUs to fill the bottle row convincingly; a single-SKU brand can run it with one bottle enlarged in place of the row. The comparison-prop device only works when the brand has a genuinely different, gentler mechanism to point to — for a brand whose main claim is disclosure rather than a milder alternative, this layout should be adapted to compare panels or ingredient counts instead of an abstract capsule icon, or dropped in favor of a straight product-lineup template. No offer mechanic is visible on this crop at all, so any percent-off, shipping, or bundle copy would need to be added as a new zone rather than assumed present.
