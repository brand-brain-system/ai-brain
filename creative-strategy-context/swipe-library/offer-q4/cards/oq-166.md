**Layout forensics.** The frame reads top-right to center-left against a nearly-white ground dusted with pale mint confetti shapes, low enough contrast that they never compete with the type sitting over them. Top right carries the loudest stack: a small black eyebrow line sits directly above one giant word set in deep forest green, tracked wide, easily the largest single element on the ad — that word alone is the scroll-stop. A black circular guarantee badge with curved white type overlaps the top of the product, small but high-contrast against the light ground. The product itself — a yellow-and-black cordless tool — sits large on the left third, angled three-quarter toward camera with a faint blue spray effect bleeding off the frame edge, which is the money shot: it shows the tool doing its job with no reading required. Below the giant word, a stacked numeral-led offer runs the same deep green, the numeral bigger than the percent sign and "OFF" beneath it, then a solid-green horizontal bar in white type. A dark-green footer bar spans the full width at the bottom, bookended by trophy emoji, carrying a ratings-style claim in white caps. First read: the giant green word (size and color contrast against the pale ground). Second: the numeral-led offer, which repeats the same green so the eye treats it as part of the same message. Third: the product itself, large but photographic rather than typographic, so it reads after the two type blocks resolve. The guarantee badge and footer bar are proof beats that close the scan.

**The story this ad tells.** This wants the viewer to believe a serious discount just opened on a tool they already understand the category for, and that the discount is safe to act on because it's backed and rated. The giant sale word does the emotional lift, the numeral makes the size of the deal legible in a glance, and the product shot proves the thing is real and does what it looks like it does. The guarantee badge and the footer claim exist purely to remove hesitation right where the eye lands last, so the ad closes on reassurance rather than on the offer alone.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow: announces the sale event, single word or short phrase, command/label mechanic, 2 words. Headline: names the sale itself, one giant word, label mechanic, 1 word. Badge: removes risk, states the guarantee, label mechanic, 4 words. Offer (numeral): anchors the size of the discount, number mechanic, 4 words. Offer (banner): names a second concrete perk, list/label mechanic, 2 words. Footer badge: proves credibility with a ranking claim, stat mechanic, 6 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a hero product shot that can carry a demonstration effect (spray, glow, motion blur) and a brand that can support a guarantee claim and a ranking-style proof line. A single-SKU brand runs this as-is. A brand without a "#1 rated" claim has to drop the footer bar or swap it for a plain guarantee restatement — inventing the ranking breaks the layout's credibility rather than helping it. The guarantee badge can become a subscription or free-gift badge with copy changes only; the numeral offer can carry percent off, dollar off, or a bundle count without touching the frame. Losing the demonstration effect on the product shot costs the money shot and should be treated as a real downgrade, not a like-for-like swap.
