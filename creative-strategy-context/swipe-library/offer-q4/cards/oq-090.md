**Layout forensics.** A pastel holographic gradient washes the whole field in soft blue, pink, and lavender, immediately softer than the stark-white sibling group this brand also runs. A deep-purple marquee ticker still opens the frame at the top in neon-yellow and white caps, carrying the same persistent urgency signature. Below it, a medium black eyebrow line sits above the frame's real anchor: a white rounded card holding four words in huge bold black caps, the strongest contrast pairing on the page because it's the only pure white-on-color shape in the frame. To the left, three benefit claims sit stacked as black-outlined pill chips, each a short spec claim rather than a persuasive line. To the right, a solid red circle badge carries a two-word scarcity claim in white bold caps — small but the most saturated color on the frame, which pulls the eye almost as fast as the white card does. The product, a black gym bench shot at an angle, sits centered beneath all of it, blending slightly into the pastel background since nothing forces contrast between the two. A plain black bar closes the bottom with no visible text. Eye order: the white card first, the red badge second (color contrast wins over the black-and-white list), the pill list third, the eyebrow fourth, the product filling the remaining space.

**The story this ad tells.** This ad is built to convert three things fast: this deal is live right now (ticker plus eyebrow), the offer itself is urgent (the white card's command), and the product won't be around much longer (the red badge). The benefit chips are almost an afterthought relative to the urgency stack above them — they exist to answer "what does it actually do" for a reader who has already been hooked by the scarcity framing, not to lead the argument.

**Copy skeleton.**
- offer (ticker) — persistent urgency announcement, list/repeat mechanic, 10 words
- eyebrow — names the event, sentence-style, 6 words
- headline — urgency command inside a card, no number, 4 words
- list — three short spec claims, one per chip, 11 words total
- badge — scarcity claim, no number, 2 words

**Adaptation notes.** The card-behind-headline device and the pill-chip benefit list are the two structural pieces worth keeping regardless of brand — the card gives the urgency line a contrast boost the pastel background can't provide alone, and the chips let three unrelated specs sit at equal weight without a bullet list looking busy. The scarcity badge needs a real basis (actual low stock, a real closing window) or it should be swapped for a plain value badge instead, since "almost gone" is a claim that needs to be true. The pastel gradient is a mood choice, not structural — it swaps freely for any brand-appropriate gradient or even a flat color without disturbing hierarchy, since the white card and red badge are what carry contrast, not the background.
