**Layout forensics.** A dark teal-to-black diagonal gradient fills the frame, giving the product real dimensionality against a moody backdrop. The brand wordmark sits small and bold in the top-left corner, out of the way of the main composition. Center frame carries the hero shot: a gift-wrapped retail box with a glossy red satin ribbon and bow, paired with the device itself unboxed and angled beside it, so the reader sees both the gift-ready packaging and the actual product shape in one glance. Below the product, two white rounded pill-shaped banners stack directly on top of each other, both in bold black sans, high-contrast against the dark gradient behind them; the first states the discount, the second names the sale occasion with a small trailing emoji. A slim code line sits at the very bottom, small and quiet compared to the pill banners above it. Eye path: the gift box and device first, since it's the brightest, most detailed object against the dark field; the two white pill banners second, since their rounded white shape and bold black text is the highest-contrast element after the product; the code line last, read only once the reader is already committed to the offer.

**The story this ad tells.** The gift-wrap treatment reframes a self-care device as a present, worth giving or worth treating yourself to, which is a specific seasonal move for a Black Friday window that overlaps with gift-buying season. Showing the product both wrapped and unwrapped answers two questions at once: this is a real gift-shaped object, and here's what you're actually getting inside it. The stacked pill banners keep the discount and the promo code legible without needing a separate badge or strip graphic, which lets the dark gradient and the product itself carry the visual drama.

**Copy skeleton.** Offer — the percent-off claim, the primary discount hook, 4 words. Badge — names the sale occasion, reinforces the offer's timing, 3 words. Code — the redeemable promo code, the mechanical unlock for the discount, 2 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a packshot that can be shown both boxed and unboxed, or at minimum a box that photographs well with a ribbon treatment; a single-SKU brand without a giftable box could still run this by wrapping just the box and dropping the unboxed unit, losing some product clarity but keeping the gift framing intact. The two pill banners and the code line swap cleanly to a dollar-off, bundle, or free-gift claim with a straight copy change, since they're just rounded text blocks, not fixed graphic devices. This layout has no room for a face, a quote, or a review count without adding an entirely new zone, since its whole visual budget is spent on product plus offer stack.
