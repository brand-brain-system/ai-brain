**Layout forensics.** The frame splits into a dark charcoal upper-left field and a cream tabletop lower band. Top-left carries the eyebrow, boxed between two hairline white rules like a ticket stub, in tight small-caps sans. Directly below, the headline explodes in huge, heavy, condensed white sans, the single biggest mass of type on the page, filling nearly the full width of the left two-thirds. A second, smaller bold line sits right under it, same white sans but a fraction of the headline's point size, closing out the numeral claim. Below that, three short lines of lighter-weight white sans sit in the remaining dark space, ragged-left, reading like a quiet aside rather than a shout. The product occupies the right half and lower-centre: a stand-up pouch printed with a two-tone face-silhouette illustration, angled slightly toward camera, with a whole pineapple looming large behind it and two stray gummies dropped on the tabletop in front. The pineapple's texture and warm gold tone is the only color note against the mostly monochrome type block, and it's large enough to compete with the headline for attention. A small serif wordmark anchors the bottom right on the cream surface. Eye path: the giant numeral claim first (size and contrast against the dark field), the pouch-and-pineapple still life second (color, texture, and sheer physical presence), then the quiet supporting line third, read almost as a caption once the offer and product have registered.

**The story this ad tells.** This is a straightforward big-type sale announcement built to be read from a scroll in under a second: event name, discount, scope, done. The product isn't hidden behind the offer, it's staged right next to it as proof this is a real thing you can buy, and the pineapple prop does double duty as a flavor cue and a way to fill the visual weight on the right side of the frame so the type doesn't feel like it's floating alone on black. The layout works for an offer because it never makes the reader hunt: numeral first, scope word second, product third, done.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow — names the sale event, sets the calendar context, 4 words. Headline — the discount number itself, the single loudest thing on the page, 2 words. Subhead — the scope qualifier that completes the discount claim, 1 word. Offer support line — softens the numeral into a reason-to-care sentence, sets tone, 8 words. Logo — brand mark only, no persuasive copy.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a packshot that can stand upright and read clearly at an angle, plus one lifestyle prop (the pineapple here) that supplies color and scale without a face or hands. A brand without a strong hero packshot could swap the prop for a lineup of two or three SKUs instead of one pouch plus fruit, but the single-pouch-plus-prop balance is what keeps the right side from feeling cluttered, so a multi-SKU swap needs careful sizing to not out-compete the numeral. The three-line support copy under the headline could carry almost any secondary claim (free shipping, sitewide scope, urgency) with a straight copy swap since it's plain sans on a plain dark field. This layout does not support a face, quote, or review count without breaking its clean two-zone (dark type field / cream product shelf) structure.
