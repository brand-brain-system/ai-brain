**Layout forensics.** The frame splits diagonally into two solid fields — black filling the upper-left, a white wedge cutting in from the lower-right — with no photo or gradient anywhere, just the hard color break itself acting as the composition's spine. At the top, on the black field, an offer line runs in bold white caps with the percent figure set larger than the words around it, the classic giant-number treatment but folded into a sentence rather than standing alone. A staggered row of six orange deodorant sticks sits right on the diagonal seam, receding in perspective so the row itself echoes the diagonal cut. Below the seam, on the white wedge, two words in solid black caps run enormous — the single largest text on the page, planted where the Z-pattern's natural bottom-left stop would land. A rounded blue pill CTA closes the frame at the very bottom in white bold caps. Eye order: the white-on-black offer line first because it opens the frame in the highest-contrast pairing available, the giant black headline second because of sheer scale, the orange product row third since color carries it even at smaller size, the CTA last as the button that resolves everything above it.

**The story this ad tells.** This ad uses the diagonal split as the argument's structure, not just its decoration — the product physically sits on the seam between the discount claim above and the event name below, so the eye reads "here's the deal, here's what's on sale, here's the day it's for" as one continuous diagonal sweep instead of three stacked blocks. The blue CTA breaks the black-and-white-and-orange palette on purpose, which is exactly why it reads as clickable.

**Copy skeleton.**
- offer — states the mechanic and the bonus, number-led, 8 words
- headline — names the event, 2 words, label mechanic
- cta — directs the click, command mechanic, 2 words

**Adaptation notes.** The diagonal split-field structure only works when the product can be staged along the seam itself — a single hero product can still ride the diagonal, but a brand with no multi-variant lineup loses some of the "row echoes the cut" effect and should consider angling one product across the seam instead. The percent-off-plus-gift offer line swaps for any number-led mechanic since it's a single sentence with one anchor figure. The blue CTA pill is a strong, portable device on its own — any brand can drop a contrasting-color pill button at the base of a bold two-tone layout and get the same "this is where you click" signal, independent of the rest of the copy.
