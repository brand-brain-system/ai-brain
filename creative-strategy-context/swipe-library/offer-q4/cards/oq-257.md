**Layout forensics.** A red banner runs full-width at the very top carrying a short all-caps sale name, but it isn't the biggest thing on the page — directly beneath it, a pair of huge black numerals joined by a plain black arrow outsizes the banner itself, making the price the true headline of this layout rather than the sale name above it. Below the price pair, the product sits center-frame as a gift-wrapped box with a red satin bow next to the bare handheld device tilted toward camera, both on a plain white ground with a soft, realistic shadow — the wrapping is doing the "holiday" work the layout needs, since there's no seasonal decor anywhere else on the page. Two small cta blocks anchor the bottom corners: a two-line risk-reversal note lower-left, a code callout lower-right, both kept deliberately small so they don't compete with the price above. The eye lands first on the giant price pair because it's both the largest type and centered high in the frame; second on the red banner just above it, since the eye backtracks up to read what the price is attached to; third on the ribboned box, which is large in area but quiet in contrast against the white ground.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe the price drop alone is the gift — wrapping the box in a bow visually equates "buying this" with "receiving a present," while the price pair does the actual selling. Image and words are congruent in a narrow, effective way: the product is dressed as a gift specifically because the headline claim is a holiday sale, so the one photographic choice (the ribbon) is carrying the entire seasonal frame that the copy never has to spell out.

**Copy skeleton.** Offer: names the sale event in the fewest words inside the top banner, announcement mechanic, 2 words. Price: anchors old against new as a plain large numeral pair with no strike device, before/after mechanic, 2 words. Cta (left): removes purchase risk with a short guarantee line, risk-reversal mechanic, 5 words. Cta (right): states the code as a direct instruction, instruction-plus-code mechanic, 3 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs only a packshot and a way to wrap it — no face, no lifestyle scene, no lineup of multiple SKUs — which makes it easy to run for a single-hero-product brand. What breaks it: a brand whose product doesn't photograph well gift-wrapped (loose or unboxed items lose the visual cue entirely), in which case the ribbon device should drop and the box needs another way to signal "holiday," such as background decor instead. The guarantee line lower-left is a legal swap for any real risk-reversal claim the brand can back (a different day count, a different promise); a review count could not sit here without being fabricated, so the guarantee stays the safer proof element for this corner. The banner and price mechanics both swap freely across percent-off, dollar-off, free gift, bundle, and promo-code framings without touching the photo.
