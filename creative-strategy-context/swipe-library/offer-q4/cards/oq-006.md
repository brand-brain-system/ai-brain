**Layout forensics.** A saturated red gradient fills the whole canvas, radial and slightly brighter at centre than at the corners, which pushes all the focus toward the middle of the frame. Centred at top is a medium-size bold white sans-serif line, then directly beneath it in a much larger acid-yellow bold sans caps is the two-word headline, easily the biggest and highest-contrast type on the page. Below that sits a bright red plastic shopping basket seen straight-on, holding two identical product pouch boxes tilted against each other so both front labels are readable. At the very bottom, a white pill-shaped badge carries a red bullseye retail-store icon next to a short bold red caps line. There is no price, no strikethrough, and no countdown anywhere in the frame — the offer is carried entirely by the giant headline text and the retail badge below it. First read is the yellow headline, since it is both the largest type and the only warm-on-warm high-contrast color pairing against the red field. Second is the basket of product, which reads as a clean colorful shape against the flat red. Third is the smaller white eyebrow line above the headline. Fourth, and easy to miss on a fast scroll, is the small retail badge at the very bottom.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe the deal is simple and already real: two boxes are already sitting in a shopping basket, so the purchase decision is basically made before the viewer even reads the fine print. The giant type and flat saturated color do the shouting, the basket does the "just go grab it" visual logic, and the retail badge closes the loop by proving the product is sitting on a shelf somewhere nearby right now — which matters for an offer ad because it removes the "is this even in stock near me" hesitation before it can form.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow: name the offer mechanic plainly, mechanic is a short declarative, 5 words. Headline: name the product category in the largest possible type, mechanic is a bare noun phrase, 2 words. Badge: state where and that it is available now, mechanic is a location callout, 4 words.

**Adaptation notes.** The layout needs two units of one SKU (or a look-alike duo) to fill the basket convincingly; a single-unit product can still run this template by swapping the basket for one box held or set inside it, but the "two boxes stacked" visual is what sells the buy-one-get-one read at a glance, so a single-SKU brand running a different mechanic (percent off, bundle) should keep the basket prop but drop the doubled-product framing. The retail badge is the one element that cannot be faked: it only works for a brand that genuinely sells through that retailer, so a DTC-only brand should swap it for a generic "Now Available" ribbon or drop the zone rather than invent a retail claim.
