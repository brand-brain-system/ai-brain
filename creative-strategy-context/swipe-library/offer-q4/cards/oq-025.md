**Layout forensics.** This is a pure text letter on a near-black field, no product, no logo mark visible, no color anywhere. A short bold all-caps headline sits at the top, centered, the only line set in caps and the only line at that weight, which makes it the clear entry point. Below it, four separate paragraphs of regular-weight centered white body copy run down the frame in sequence, each a self-contained beat of the story: the incident, the emotional reaction plus the ask, the specific request for help, and the thank-you close. None of the body paragraphs are visually differentiated from each other beyond spacing, so the read is entirely linear, top to bottom, like a letter rather than a poster. A short dash-led signature line closes the piece at the very bottom, set apart from the paragraphs above it by the extra whitespace around it. Eye path: the bold caps headline first (weight and case contrast against the thin body copy below it), then the paragraphs read in strict top-to-bottom order since nothing else on the page competes for attention, ending on the signature line as the visual and narrative close.

**The story this ad tells.** This ad tells a crisis story rather than making an offer at all: a robbery, an appeal for help, and a note of gratitude to the community, timed right before a sale window ("right before Black Friday") without actually announcing one, which is the only urgency cue drawn on the image. It works as a scroll-stopper because it reads like a personal update from a founder or team rather than an ad, and the total absence of product or discount graphics is what sells that illusion. The lesson for an offer-static library is structural, not promotional: this is the bare bones of a long-form text post, and the same stacked-paragraph rhythm on a plain dark field can carry an actual sale announcement instead of a crisis story with a straight copy swap.

**Copy skeleton.** Headline — the attention-grabbing lead-in, sets an emotional register before any specifics, 5 words. Subhead 1 — the incident itself, told plainly, 26 words. Subhead 2 — the emotional reaction plus the reason for posting, 20 words. Subhead 3 — the specific ask of the reader, 13 words. Subhead 4 — the gratitude close, wraps the emotional arc, 21 words. Attribution — signs off as the team, not an individual, 3 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs nothing but a dark solid background and a paragraph of copy, so any brand can run it as-is with zero product or photo assets; the four-paragraph rhythm swaps cleanly to a sale announcement (occasion, discount, scope, close) without changing a single structural element. Because there's no offer device, no proof element, and no product on this template at all, it's the most flexible and cheapest card in the set to adapt, but it also does the least visual selling on its own; a brand leaning on this format needs its copy voice to carry the whole ad, since there is nothing else here to lean on.
