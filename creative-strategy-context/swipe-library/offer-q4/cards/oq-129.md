**Layout forensics.** The frame reads top to bottom in four clean bands on a single flat cream field, no photographic background at all. The top band is the product itself, shot large and centered as a clean hero packshot — the jar fills most of the width, with a loose chew set beside it as a small styling touch. Directly under the product, a medium two-tone italic serif line splits its color mid-phrase, red bleeding into black, which reads as a soft transition line rather than a shout. Beneath that sits the loudest element on the page: a huge black all-caps numeral claim set in a heavy sans, wide enough to span almost the full frame width, with none of the italic's ornament. The bottom band holds three thin-bordered rounded boxes, each carrying a green checkmark and a short claim, giving the ad its proof close. First read is the product (size and top position), second is the black numeral (weight and contrast beat the italic line above it), third is the italic eyebrow, fourth is the checklist row.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to recognize the product first, register the size of the discount second, and only then pick up the reassurance details — vet-formulated, no fillers, money back — that remove any last hesitation. The clean cream field keeps every band legible on its own, so the eye can move down the page in a straight line without a background photo competing for attention.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow: names the sale event, two-tone for a soft visual break, three words, declarative tag. Offer: states the discount size as the single biggest claim on the page, three words, number-led command. List: three short trust claims in checklist form, each answering a different objection (ingredients, formulation, risk), ten words total, short fragment style per line.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs one clean packshot and nothing else — it survives a swap to any single-SKU supplement, food, or consumer product with a jar, bottle, or box shape. The percent-off numeral swaps cleanly to a dollar-off or bundle price with no structural change. The checklist is the flexible zone: any three short trust or benefit claims can rotate through it, including a review count or star rating if the brand has one to show, though a fabricated proof line should never fill that slot. What breaks the layout: a product that can't be isolated cleanly against a flat background, since the whole ad depends on the packshot reading instantly with nothing else in frame to help it.
