**Layout forensics.** A quoted first-person line runs enormous across the top of the frame, easily the largest type on the page, with a small attribution line directly beneath it naming who supposedly said it — the classic testimonial-as-headline move, where the "hook" isn't a brand claim but a borrowed voice. Below that, the product sits centered on a plain white ground, a red cylindrical bottle with two loose capsules resting beside it, lifted visually by a soft lavender wave shape curving up behind it — the one piece of color-as-backdrop in an otherwise white frame. Underneath the product, a black angled ribbon banner names the sale event, and directly below it a second black bar carries the discount figure in a bright yellow-green that jumps off the black the way the red bottle jumps off the white above it. A tiny two-line grey disclaimer runs along the very bottom, small enough to be legally present without competing for attention. The eye lands first on the giant quote because nothing else on the page approaches its size or contrast; second on the product, which sits in the exact vertical center of the frame with the lavender wave lifting it off the white ground; third on the black offer stack, whose bright accent color pulls the eye down and closes the read.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe a real customer had a dramatic personal result, and it leads with that claim rather than the product, betting that the emotional jolt of the quote earns the scroll-stop the product photo alone wouldn't. Image and words are only loosely congruent: the quote does the persuading, the product photo confirms what's being sold, and the offer stack closes the loop, so the three elements function in sequence rather than reinforcing each other visually.

**Copy skeleton.** Quote: states a dramatic first-person outcome in quotation marks, testimonial mechanic, 6 words. Attribution: names the source in shorthand, name-plus-initial mechanic, 2 words. Badge: names the sale event on an angled ribbon, announcement mechanic, 3 words. Offer: states the discount tier stacked under a small lead-in label, number-as-hero mechanic, 3 words. Disclaimer: qualifies the testimonial and the review basis in small legal type, hedge-language mechanic, 22 words.

**Adaptation notes.** The quote at the top of this layout must be a real, sourced customer quote — it is the entire hook, and there is no legal swap for an invented one; a brand without a strong enough verbatim quote on file should not run this template and should choose a hero-packshot or offer-led layout instead. What swaps cleanly: the offer stack's mechanic, which moves freely between percent-off, dollar-off, free shipping, promo code, and bundle framings without touching the quote or the product. The lavender wave behind the product is a pure color-and-shape device and can shift to any brand color without changing the layout's function. The disclaimer block should stay proportioned to whatever qualifying language the brand's own quote actually needs, and should never be padded with claims the quote doesn't support.
