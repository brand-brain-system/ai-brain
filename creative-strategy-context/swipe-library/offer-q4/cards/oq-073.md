**Layout forensics.** The frame opens on a warm coral gradient, lighter at the crown and deepening toward the base, with a small white script wordmark centered at the very top. Under it sits a dark maroon two-word eyebrow in bold sans caps, then the frame's real scroll-stop: a single word set enormous in solid white block type, wide enough to nearly touch both edges. That word is set at roughly double the point size of anything else on the frame, which is what earns it the first look. Below that, a mixed-scale offer line runs the percent figure oversized against smaller flanking words in the same bold white caps, so the discount reads as its own beat even inside one line. A hot-pink circle sticker sits at the shoulder of the product cluster, carrying a three-line white claim in bold caps — the second-brightest thing in the frame after the headline, because saturated pink against warm coral is the sharpest color-contrast pairing on the page. The product itself is a fanned row of five sticks, the tallest centered and the others stepping down and out to each side, all standing on a two-tier stone ledge that reads as a store shelf corner. Eye order: the giant white word first, the offer line second (same white, smaller but still bold), the pink badge third, the product last as the payoff the type has been promising.

**The story this ad tells.** This is a single, unambiguous message: a seasonal event is on, the discount is steep, and there's a bonus attached. The giant type does the work a headline usually does on its own — no supporting sentence is needed because the word itself is the whole argument — and the badge exists purely to add a second reason to believe ("biggest deal") without competing for the same visual slot. The product row grounds the promise in something real: this is what actually ships, in five scent variants, so the discount reads as applying to the whole catalog rather than one SKU.

**Copy skeleton.**
- eyebrow — names the event, 2 words, command/label mechanic
- headline — announces the sale itself, 1 word, single-word command
- offer — states the mechanic and the bonus, number-led, 7 words
- badge — anchors superlative claim ("biggest deal"), 6 words, claim mechanic

**Adaptation notes.** The layout survives cleanly on any brand with a multi-variant lineup — swap five sticks for five flavors, five colors, five sizes, whatever the catalog actually has, and keep the fanned staging. A single-SKU brand breaks the "lineup" read; drop to one hero unit and the giant-word-plus-badge structure still holds, since neither depends on product count. The percent-off number and the free-gift line are both easy swaps for dollar-off, bundle, or subscription language — the value_list zone just needs one anchor figure and one bonus clause. The badge claim ("biggest deal of the year") is a superlative, not a review count, so it never needs faked social proof; it can drop entirely if the brand has no comparable claim to make, and the layout still resolves on the giant word alone.
