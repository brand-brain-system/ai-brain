**Layout forensics.** Two giant elements compete for the top of the frame and both win a piece of the read: a red all-caps urgency headline runs the full width at the very top, and immediately beside the product a giant black numeral runs nearly as large just below it — the ad has two scroll-stop devices instead of the usual one, and it can afford that because they're stacked rather than side by side at equal weight. The product itself sits lower-left, a box and a handheld device angled together against a soft warm gradient, sized modestly compared to the type around it — it's present but not the hero here, the numbers are. To the numeral's right, a strikethrough price sits inside a red pill, an arrow points to a green pill carrying the new price, and below all of it a three-line black cta block spells out the mechanics with the code picked out in red on its own line. A single pine sprig anchors the bottom-left corner as the only seasonal decor on the page. The eye lands first on the red headline because it's both the top-most element and the loudest color; second on the giant black "90" because its sheer scale rivals the headline even though it's black-on-cream rather than colored; third on the product, which the eye only really registers once the numbers have been read, since it's small relative to everything stacked around it.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe the deadline is real and the discount is enormous, in that order — "FINAL HOURS" primes urgency before the eye ever reaches the number, so the 90% reads as a closing-out price rather than an everyday one. Image and words cooperate loosely rather than tightly here: the product photo is doing modest work, and almost the entire persuasion load sits in the type treatment of the numbers themselves, which is the layout's whole bet — that a big enough numeral doesn't need a photo to back it up.

**Copy skeleton.** Headline: states the deadline at maximum size and urgency, alarm mechanic, 2 words. Badge: names the discount tier and states it as a giant standalone numeral, number-as-hero mechanic, 4 words. Price: anchors old against new with a visible strike and an arrow, before/after mechanic, 2 words. Cta: repeats the urgency, states the code, and names the outcome across three short lines, instruction-plus-code mechanic, 9 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout only needs a packshot to run — no face, no lifestyle scene, no before/after required — which makes it one of the most portable templates for any brand with a boxed product and a percentage to lead with. What breaks it: a brand that can't credibly claim a deadline, since the giant numeral only works alongside a headline that makes the number feel time-boxed rather than evergreen; drop the "FINAL HOURS" framing and the giant number reads as a permanent markdown, which undersells it. The pine-sprig decor is a pure seasonal skin and drops cleanly for any non-holiday window. The percentage mechanic swaps freely to dollar-off, promo code alone, or a bundle callout since the giant numeral is just a container for whichever figure the brand wants to feature.
