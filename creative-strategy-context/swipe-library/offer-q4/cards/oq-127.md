**Layout forensics.** The frame splits into two solid color blocks, roughly 45/55 black-to-grey, and the split itself does the sorting work. In the black upper block, a small letter-spaced coral eyebrow sits centered near the top, and directly beneath it a huge white all-caps headline fills most of the block's width — the highest-contrast element on the page and the first thing the eye lands on. The six product boxes are arranged as a fanned lineup, each tilted a few degrees off vertical, overlapping in two staggered rows that straddle the black-to-grey seam, so the product visually bridges the two halves rather than sitting inside either one. Below the seam, on the light-grey lower block, a giant red all-caps offer numeral spans nearly the full width, and a smaller black all-caps line sits directly under it, tight against its baseline. The bottom quarter of the frame is empty grey space, acting as a landing pad that keeps the offer from feeling cramped. First read is the white headline (size plus maximum contrast against black), second is the red offer numeral (size plus the only saturated color on the grey half), third is the box lineup (color and product recognition), fourth is the small coral eyebrow.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to register "the big sale everyone's talking about is finally live" in the first half-second, then let the product lineup answer "for this brand, specifically" before the numeral closes with the reason to act now. The black-to-grey split works like a scene change — event announcement first, offer mechanics second — and the fanned lineup in between does double duty as a money shot (the whole flavor range at once) and as the visual hinge connecting the two halves.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow: announces the sale event by name, two words, direct statement. Headline: confirms it has started, two words, short declarative punch. Offer: names the percent-off mechanic and anchors the ceiling of the discount, four words, number-led claim. Subhead: stacks a second incentive on top of the discount, two words, additive list-style tag.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs multiple SKU packshots to fill the fan — a single-SKU brand can swap the six-box fan for a tighter three-pack or a single hero bottle shot larger in the same zone, and the layout survives because the black/grey split and numeral do the heavy lifting regardless of what's in the product zone. The percent-off numeral converts cleanly to a dollar-off or buy-X-get-Y figure with no structural change. The "+ FREE GIFT" line drops cleanly if there's no gift offer; a subscription callout could sit in that same slot instead. What breaks the layout: any brand without at least one clean product image, since the lineup zone is the only visual proof the eye gets — pairing this shell with a talking-head or lifestyle photo instead voids the reason the black/grey split exists.
