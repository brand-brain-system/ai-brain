**Layout forensics.** A scrolling marquee ticker bookends the frame top and bottom — a deep-purple band with alternating neon-yellow and white bold caps, the visual signature of a limited-time drop rather than a static banner. Between the two ticker bands, the field goes to plain white. A black bold subhead sits upper-frame naming the dollar figure at a moderate size, and directly beneath it the frame's actual scroll-stop: two words in huge red bold caps, sized well beyond anything else on the page, floating in white space with nothing competing around it. The product — a black gym machine shot at a working three-quarter angle — sits centered below, small relative to the type stacked above it, which flips the usual "product as money shot" order: here the urgency language is the money shot, and the hardware is proof it exists. Eye order: the red headline first (size and color both work against a white field), the black subhead second, the ticker bands as a persistent frame third, the product last.

**The story this ad tells.** This ad isn't selling the discount amount so much as selling scarcity of time. The ticker never stops announcing "live now," the giant red type escalates that into a command, and the black subhead is the only place the actual dollar figure lives — small, almost secondary to the urgency itself. The product photo exists mostly to confirm this is a real, physical thing worth acting fast on, not to sell the machine on its features.

**Copy skeleton.**
- offer (ticker) — persistent urgency announcement, list/repeat mechanic, 10 words
- subhead — states the discount mechanic and figure, number-led, 4 words
- headline — urgency command, no number, 2 words

**Adaptation notes.** This layout is asset-light — it needs one clean packshot and nothing else, so it travels to almost any product category as-is. The dollar-off figure in the subhead swaps cleanly for percent-off, bundle, or free-shipping language since it's just one clause. The ticker banner is the one element that can't be faked with a stock claim: if the brand has no live "early access" window running, drop "live now" language and keep it to a plain countdown-style deadline instead, or the urgency reads false. No proof element is present or needed; adding a review count here would compete with the ticker for the same "trust me, act now" register and should stay out.
