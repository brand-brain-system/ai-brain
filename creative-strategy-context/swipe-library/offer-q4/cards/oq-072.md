**Layout forensics.** A deep navy textured wall fills the background, with visible mottled brushstroke texture giving it depth rather than a flat color field. The top of the frame carries the brand wordmark centered in clean white type, followed by a two-line headline stack: a shorter light-blue bold line naming the sale event, directly above a single massive solid-white word set at a scale that spans nearly the full width of the frame — the largest text element by far. Below that, a bold white line states the discount and gift claim in plain sans type, numeral-led on both halves. Overlapping the right edge of the product row, a solid orange circle badge breaks the navy palette with the only warm, saturated color in the frame, holding a short stacked superlative claim in bold white type. The bottom of the frame holds the product itself: five identical stick containers standing upright on a light gray cement plinth with angled, faceted cut edges, each labeled with a different scent, lit evenly from the front. The eye lands first on the giant white headline word (largest scale, highest contrast against navy, top-weighted), second on the orange circle badge (the only warm color in an otherwise cool palette, medium size), third on the product row (grounded at the bottom, detailed but smaller relative to the type above it).

**The story this ad tells.** This wants the viewer to believe they've hit the single best moment to buy a real, functional product line, with the orange badge acting like a physical shelf-talker sticker slapped onto the display. The cool navy-and-ice-blue palette keeps the tone restrained and premium even while the headline word is shouting, and the orange badge's warmth against that cool field does the job of pulling the eye toward the specific claim without needing a second loud color anywhere else. The layout works for an offer because the product row at the bottom keeps the ad grounded in something real and buyable, even after two rounds of oversized sale typography above it.

**Copy skeleton.** Logo — brand wordmark only, no persuasive copy. Eyebrow — name the sale event, 2 words. Headline — one massive word declaring the sale type, 1 word. Offer — a percent-off number plus a free-gift count, numeral-led on both halves, 7 words. Badge — a superlative claim about the deal's scale, stacked two lines, 6 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout survives for any brand with a multi-SKU lineup that stands well upright in a row (sticks, bottles, tubes) and a genuine percent-off-plus-gift mechanic to state plainly — swap the five sticks for the brand's own SKU row on a plinth or shelf surface, and the orange badge's claim for whatever superlative the brand can honestly make. The badge is mechanic-flexible and can carry early access, a bundle price, or a value-stack claim instead of a straight discount with no structural change. It breaks if the brand has only one SKU, since the bottom third is built around a row of standing variants; a single product would need to be re-blocked as a hero shot, which would leave the badge with nothing to overlap.
