**Layout forensics.** The top third of the frame is a single white, rounded-corner caption card that reads like a screenshotted social post rather than a designed ad. A small red siren emoji leads the block (the eyebrow), then the card runs straight into a dense, bold black all-caps paragraph that fills the rest of the card with no line breaks between sentences. Below the card, the frame cuts to a candid store photo: a hand reaches up from off-frame and holds one pouch box out toward the camera at a slight upward angle, plucked from a shelf; several identical boxes sit in a wire retail shelf beneath, and a generic warehouse-style aisle with harsh overhead fluorescent panels fills the background. There is no separate offer badge, banner, or price device anywhere in the frame — the "ending soon" urgency and the BOGO mechanic are carried entirely inside the caption-card text. First read is the caption card: it is the highest-contrast element (pure black text on white, large point size, top-loaded) and the eye has nowhere else to go before the product. Second is the product itself, popping in saturated magenta/purple/yellow against the neutral grey shelving. Third is the eyebrow siren, which is small and easy to skip on a first pass even though it sits above the headline text.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe they stumbled on a real customer's unscripted PSA, not an ad — the screenshotted-caption device and the store-shelf hand photo both borrow the visual grammar of organic social posts. The words do the announcing (retail launch, BOGO, deadline) while the photo does the proving (this is a real product on a real shelf, not a render), and the pairing works for an offer because the retail presence itself is the credibility device: the offer only feels urgent and real because you can see the hand grabbing it off an actual shelf.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow: alert flag, mechanic is a single exclamation cue, 1 word. Headline: announce the retail launch, name the BOGO mechanic, and land the deadline in one run-on multi-clause statement, mechanic is a stacked announcement, 22 words. Product zone carries no copy of its own; the shelf photo is the proof.

**Adaptation notes.** Caption alignment (Alex, 2026-09-16, board feedback on Honeylove #5): the caption text and its white rounded background are both centred, as on the reference; never mix centred and left-aligned lines in the caption. Centred is preferred; fully left-aligned also works. This layout needs a genuine retail placement to shoot the hand-and-shelf photo honestly — without one, the "PSA" device reads as fabricated and the whole layout breaks, since the retail-legitimacy angle is the entire reason the caption card is believable. If a brand has no retail presence, the same caption-card-over-photo structure can survive with a DTC swap (a hand holding the box against a doorstep or unboxing scene) but loses the specific "just found it at the store" hook the copy is built around. The BOGO mechanic swaps cleanly to any other mechanic in mechanics_compatible with copy changes only, since the caption card is plain text with no shape locked to "get 1 free." The "ending soon" clause requires a real deadline; drop it (urgency_device becomes false) rather than inventing one.
