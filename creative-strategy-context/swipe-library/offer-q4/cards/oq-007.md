**Layout forensics.** A flat soft-pink backdrop holds a small red rectangular badge at the very top, bold white caps inside it, sitting above a large bold purple all-caps headline that is the biggest single line of type on the page. A smaller purple sentence-case subhead sits directly beneath the headline. The left column then carries the offer stack: a large hot-pink bold numeral line, a smaller bold purple line beneath it, and under both a four-item checklist where each line leads with a small heart-emoji bullet and the word "FREE" set in hot-pink bold against the rest of the line in plain purple. On the right, the hero pouch stands upright on a round white cylindrical podium, angled slightly so both the front panel and a hint of the side gusset are visible, with a soft contact shadow grounding it. At the very bottom, a centred line of five outlined star icons sits beside a short guarantee statement. First read is the purple headline, since it is the largest, boldest, most central type block. Second is the hot-pink offer numeral on the left, which is the highest-saturation color on the page and pulls the eye down and left. Third is the product itself, anchored by its own podium and shadow. Fourth is the free-gift checklist beneath the offer, which reads as supporting detail once the main numbers have landed.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe this is the last chance to get a stacked deal that already includes several free extras, not just a discount. The badge and headline set the clock, the pink numeral makes the discount impossible to miss, and the checklist quietly multiplies the perceived value beneath it — the pairing works for an offer because the eye is walked from urgency, to size of discount, to a growing pile of "also free" line items, before it ever has to think about the price it will actually pay.

**Copy skeleton.** Badge: flag the deadline, mechanic is a two-word alarm, 2 words. Headline: name the product/kit, mechanic is a bare noun phrase, 3 words. Subhead: state the problem the kit solves, mechanic is a short claim, 7 words. Offer: state the discount size and the gift count, mechanic is a stacked numeral pair, 5 words. List: name each free item, mechanic is a bulleted list, 13 words across four lines. Disclaimer: state the guarantee window, mechanic is a numeral-led claim, 5 words.

**Adaptation notes.** The layout needs one hero packshot that can stand upright on a podium and a genuine percent-off number to run as-is. The four-item free-gift checklist is the one part that cannot be faked for a brand without real bundled extras — it can shrink to one or two real items, or the "FREE" gift line can become a "FREE shipping" or subscription-perk line instead, without breaking the layout. The five-star row and guarantee line are generic decorative trust marks here, not a review count; a brand with no verified rating should keep the guarantee text but drop the star icons, or replace them with an actual guarantee seal graphic, since an invented star rating is not something to carry into new copy.
