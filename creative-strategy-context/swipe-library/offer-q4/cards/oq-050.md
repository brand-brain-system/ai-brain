**Layout forensics.** A light gray radial gradient fills the frame, brightest at center-top, giving the whole ad a studio-lightbox feel with no hard edges. The top 20% is dominated by an oversized eyebrow line in yellow fill with a heavy black outline and drop shadow, stacked directly above a second line of solid black all-caps type naming the product — together these two lines run edge to edge and set the register before anything else is read. Below that, the frame splits roughly 55/45: the left side carries a cropped body shot, shot from behind and slightly below, one leg lifted mid-stride so the garment's seam lines and fit are visible on the glute and thigh; the right side stacks three short benefit pills, black-on-yellow and yellow-on-black alternating, each pill a rounded rectangle sitting on its own line with light spacing between. In the lower-left corner, overlapping the body shot's hem, sits a black scalloped starburst sticker — the classic "sale sticker" shape — holding a struck-through original price above a much larger sale price, both in yellow. Lower right holds three rolled swatches of the same fabric in different colorways, stacked diagonally to read almost like a stack of towels, showing the product exists beyond the black colorway shown on the body. The eye lands first on the huge yellow-outlined eyebrow line (size and the highest-contrast color in the frame), second on the body photo (large scale, skin tone against the neutral gray), third on the starburst price sticker (isolated black shape, bottom-left anchor point).

**The story this ad tells.** This wants the viewer to believe a specific, already-flattering product is now half price for one seasonal window. The body photo does the proof-of-fit work while the yellow badge system does the shouting — together they read "the product looks like this on a real body, and it's currently 50% off," with the color-option swatches quietly answering "and yes, it comes in the shade you want." The layout works for an offer because it never makes the viewer choose between believing the product and noticing the deal; both are large, both are unmissable, and the eye can bounce between them without losing either thread.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow — announce the sale window, one punchy noun phrase, 2 words. Headline — name the product plainly, no benefit language, 4 words. List — three parallel benefit lines, each a short noun-phrase claim, command-adjacent tone, 11 words total across the three. Offer — a before/after price pair, numeral-led, 2 words (the two price numerals). No copy needed for the two product zones beyond what's visually shown.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout survives almost any brand with a single hero SKU that comes in more than one color or size and a body-worn or hand-held use case: swap the body photo for the brand's own product-in-use shot and the three swatches for that brand's actual variant options, and the sticker's before/after price drops in cleanly for any percent-off or dollar-off mechanic. It breaks if the brand has no in-use or on-body photography — a flatlay product alone in that left zone loses the "proof of fit" read the whole ad depends on. The three-pill benefit list is mechanic-agnostic and can carry any short claims the brand is allowed to make. If the brand lacks color variants, the swatch zone can drop entirely without damaging the hierarchy, since it's a tertiary read.
