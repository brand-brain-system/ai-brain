**Layout forensics.** The frame is a straight-on selfie-style photo: a sage-green interior wall fills the background, softly out of focus, with a woman's face and shoulders taking up the center and right two-thirds of the image, looking directly at the lens with a warm, knowing half-smile. She holds the product bottle vertically at chest height, cropped so the label faces the camera clearly. Two stacked text cards sit top-left, overlapping the top of her hair: a black rectangle with small bold white type, directly under it a white rectangle with bold black type — the two cards read almost like a headline broken into a setup line and a punch line. Lower left, breaking out of any card and sitting directly on the photo, is the offer: a slim white label line above a huge outlined-and-filled white numeral with a percent glyph, "OFF" tucked small beneath it so the numeral itself reads as the loudest single element after the face. The eye lands first on the face (direct gaze, center-weighted, warmest color temperature in the frame), second on the giant white percent numeral (largest single text element, high contrast against the green wall), third on the black-and-white headline cards at top (smaller scale, upper corner, read last as context).

**The story this ad tells.** This wants the viewer to believe a real, relatable person just solved an annoying, recurring problem with one simple product — and that the same product is now half price. The face-to-camera format borrows the trust of a friend's recommendation rather than a studio ad, while the giant numeral does the commercial lifting so the testimonial-style photo doesn't have to carry the offer alone. It works because the two jobs never fight: the face earns belief, the numeral earns urgency, and the product bottle sits physically between them as the literal bridge.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow — set up the claim as a superlative, question-adjacent framing, 5 words. Headline — deliver the specific promise the product makes, a direct command or benefit statement, 5 words. Offer — name the sale window in a small label line, then the discount as a giant numeral, 5 words total across the label and numeral. No copy needed for the product zone beyond what's visually shown.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout survives for any brand with a genuine face-to-camera asset (founder, customer, or a hired talent shot in a home setting) holding a single hero product at chest height — swap the wall color and the bottle and the two-card headline drops in cleanly for any problem-solution claim the brand can make. The giant-numeral offer zone is mechanic-flexible: percent off, dollar off, or a bundle price all read the same way at that scale. It breaks without a usable face asset — a hands-only or product-only substitute loses the "relatable person" trust the whole format is built on, since the headline cards are written as if a person is speaking them directly to camera.
