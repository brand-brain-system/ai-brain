**Layout forensics.** Identical scaffolding to its sibling — deep-purple marquee ticker top and bottom in neon-yellow and white caps, plain white field between — but here the giant red headline carries an actual numeral rather than a bare command, and that numeral is rendered at the largest scale on the whole page, larger even than the sibling's urgency phrase. A black bold subhead sits above it warning that the pricing window is closing, smaller and more matter-of-fact than the red claim beneath it. The product sits centered and small below the type stack, same gym machine at the same three-quarter angle. Because the numeral itself is the oversized element, this variant reads as a straight discount-anchor ad first and an urgency ad second — the opposite emphasis of its sibling, which leads with the urgency phrase and leaves the number smaller.

**The story this ad tells.** This version wants the discount percentage to be the thing that stops the scroll — 26 percent is the argument, and the subhead's "won't last" line is there to convert that number into action before the reader looks away. The ticker keeps reinforcing the same time pressure in the background the whole time the eye is on the number.

**Copy skeleton.**
- offer (ticker) — persistent urgency announcement, list/repeat mechanic, 10 words
- subhead — states the deadline pressure, no number, 5 words
- headline — discount anchor with command, number-led, 3 words

**Adaptation notes.** Same low asset requirement as its sibling — one clean packshot, no lifestyle or face needed. The percent figure in the headline swaps directly for a dollar figure or a "buy one get one" framing since the giant-numeral zone just needs one number or short numeric claim to anchor on. Running this and its sibling as true A/B variants (urgency-led headline vs. discount-led headline) is the intended use of the pair — that's the actual axis this template family is testing, so keep everything else, including the ticker copy, identical when adapting both.
