**Layout forensics.** This is the quietest layout in the set: no banner, no numeral, no badge, just a small centered wordmark, two lines of restrained sans-serif copy, and a tall vertical product shot beneath it. The wordmark sits smallest at the very top; below it a slightly larger, softer-weight headline states the mechanic in two words; below that a thin, smaller subhead spells out the specific terms across two lines. The product group takes up the majority of the vertical frame — three tubes of different heights and colors standing upright shoulder to shoulder, cropped tight at the bottom so the group reads as a still-life rather than a catalog shot. A deliberate stylistic choice runs through the product labels: they're rendered upside-down, which reads as an intentional design flourish rather than an error, and it's the one thing in the frame doing any visual personality work. The eye lands first on the product group because it occupies the largest area and carries the only saturated color in an otherwise neutral, low-contrast frame; second on the headline above it, since the type block reads top-down once the eye has registered the product; third on the subhead, which resolves the specific numbers the headline only gestured at.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe this is less an ad and more a product-page screenshot — restrained type, no urgency device, no sale banner, just a clean subscription pitch sitting quietly above a well-composed lineup shot. Image and words are congruent in a soft way: nothing about the copy oversells, and the still-life photography matches that same understated register, so the whole ad reads as a brand simply presenting its subscription option rather than pushing a discount.

**Copy skeleton.** Logo: identifies the brand at minimum size, wordmark mechanic, 2 words. Headline: names the program in two words at the top of the type stack, label mechanic, 3 words. Subhead: states the discount and the shipping term in one plain sentence, plain-statement mechanic, 11 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a real multi-SKU lineup to fill the frame — a single product would leave the composition looking sparse, since the still-life's visual interest comes from the varied heights and colors of three or more tubes standing together. What breaks it: a brand with only one hero SKU, which would need to either shoot a bundle of that one product in multiple sizes or swap this template out entirely for a hero-packshot layout instead. The subscription mechanic swaps cleanly to a straight percent-off or dollar-off sale without touching the photo, since the offer language sits in plain sentence form rather than a styled device. The upside-down label treatment is a pure style choice and can run right-side-up with no loss to the layout's function.
