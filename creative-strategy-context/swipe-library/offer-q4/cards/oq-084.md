**Layout forensics.** The frame runs a flat burnt-terracotta field top to bottom, with the product itself appearing twice as cropped fragments — a lid at the very top edge, a full pan at the very bottom — so the copy stack reads as sandwiched between two glimpses of the same product rather than sitting beside a single hero shot. A small cream serif wordmark centers the top of that stack, then a three-word headline in bold cream sans caps where the middle word is set visibly larger than its neighbors, escalating mid-phrase rather than all at once. A dark-brown banner strip spans the full width beneath it, naming the specific SKU in small caps — a clean color-contrast break from the terracotta field. Below that, the actual price mechanic: an old figure struck through beside the new one, both in bold cream serif, sitting at a smaller scale than the headline but larger than the SKU banner. An arch-shaped brown badge floats to the side carrying a short cream italic urgency line. Eye order: the escalating headline first, the strikethrough price second (numbers draw fast), the arch badge third, the SKU banner and cropped product shots last as supporting context.

**The story this ad tells.** This ad wants the reader to register three things in order: there's a sale, here's exactly what's on sale, and here's the actual math of what changes for them. The strikethrough price does the persuasive heavy lifting — it's the only element that requires the reader to compare two numbers, which is a small cognitive tax that pays off in credibility a flat percentage doesn't buy. The urgency badge is deliberately small and understated relative to the price, so it nudges rather than shouts.

**Copy skeleton.**
- headline — announces the sale, escalating emphasis, 3 words
- subhead (SKU banner) — names the specific product on sale, 4 words
- price — anchors old vs. new figure, number-led, 2 words
- badge — soft urgency nudge, no number, 5 words

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a single hero SKU with a real price to strike through — it does not survive on a brand that can't show two comparable numbers, since the strikethrough device is the whole persuasive engine. A brand without an "old price" can swap the price zone for a plain value or percent-off callout, but that trades away the ad's sharpest device. The SKU banner is a one-line swap for any product name. The arch badge's urgency line can drop entirely without breaking the layout, since the strikethrough price already carries the persuasive weight; it would take a genuine deadline claim to replace it credibly, not an invented one.
