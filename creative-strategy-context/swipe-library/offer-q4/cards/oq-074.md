**Layout forensics.** Same skeleton as its sibling, restaged in a darker register: the backdrop is a mottled rust-brown texture rather than a gradient sky, which pulls the whole frame moodier and makes the white type pop harder by contrast. A small white script wordmark sits top-center, then a two-word orange eyebrow in bold caps, then the frame's anchor — a single word in solid white block type at roughly double the scale of any other element, spanning nearly the full width. Beneath it, an offer line mixes scales within one row, the percent figure set larger than the words around it. An orange circle sticker carries a three-line white superlative claim at the shoulder of the product cluster; orange-on-brown is a lower-contrast pairing than the coral sibling's pink-on-coral, so the badge reads a beat quieter here, but it's still the second stop after the giant word. The product is the same fanned five-stick row, now standing on a warm wood tabletop instead of stone — a domestic surface rather than a retail-shelf cue. Eye order holds: giant word, offer line, badge, product.

**The story this ad tells.** This is the same argument as its sibling in a different key — the message doesn't change, the mood does. Where the coral version reads bright and retail, this one reads warmer and more intimate, closer to a home-counter shot than a store display. That's useful evidence on its own: the brand is testing tonal variants of one structure rather than testing structure itself, which means the layout is doing its job regardless of palette.

**Copy skeleton.**
- eyebrow — names the event, 2 words, command/label mechanic
- headline — announces the sale itself, 1 word, single-word command
- offer — states the mechanic and the bonus, number-led, 7 words
- badge — anchors superlative claim ("biggest deal"), 6 words, claim mechanic

**Adaptation notes.** Identical adaptation logic to the coral sibling: any multi-variant catalog can restage the fanned lineup, and a single-SKU brand drops to one hero unit without losing the giant-word-plus-badge spine. The wood-tabletop surface is the one asset-specific choice here — a brand without a lifestyle surface to shoot on can substitute a plain studio backdrop and lose nothing structural, since the surface is texture, not argument. Percent-off and free-gift swap cleanly for dollar-off, bundle, or subscription copy.
