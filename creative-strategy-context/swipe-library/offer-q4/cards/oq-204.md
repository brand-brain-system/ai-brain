**Layout forensics.** A flat mustard-gold field is the only background element, chosen for maximum contrast against pure black type rather than for any brand-lifestyle reason. A small circular tri-color logo icon sits top-center, understated relative to what follows immediately below it: a two-column block where a giant black numeral-and-percent-sign anchors the left side and a stacked, bold caps sale name anchors the right, with a smaller product-name subhead tucked underneath the sale name. The numeral is by far the largest single element on the frame, bigger than the product itself, which makes the discount the frame's real subject rather than the club — an unusual choice for a hero-packshot template, since normally the product would dominate. Center-frame, the clubhead sits in a top-down three-quarter angle that shows off its face plate and crown markings clearly, red and silver against the gold field, with the shaft running straight down to the bottom edge and slightly interrupting the CTA button beneath it. That CTA is a rounded yellow pill, the frame's second color note besides the gold background itself, sitting dead-center at the base with a command in bold black caps. Eye order: the giant numeral first (raw size wins outright), the sale-name block second (proximity and matching weight tie it to the numeral), the clubhead third (color and product recognition, but smaller than the type above it), the yellow CTA button last, though its color contrast against gold does give it a fighting chance to be seen before the eye leaves the frame.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe the discount is remarkable enough to be the headline in its own right, with the product serving as proof that the deal is real and not too-good-to-be-true — a hybrid club replacing two others is a credible, specific claim that backs up an otherwise generic seasonal discount. Image and words are only loosely congruent here: the type block does all the persuasive work while the product shot is decorative confirmation rather than a money shot, which is a real risk on a layout this type-heavy, since the club itself never gets to sell its own benefit visually. It works as an offer ad because the numeral-first hierarchy is unambiguous — nobody scrolling past has to read a sentence to know there's a big discount here.

**Copy skeleton.** Logo: brand mark, icon only, treated as 1 word for count. Offer: the discount, giant numeral plus unit, number-led, 2 words. Headline: the sale name, event naming, 2 words. Subhead: the specific product on sale, naming, 4 words. Product: no copy, proof shot. CTA: a two-part command with an aspirational payoff, command plus benefit, 6 words.

**Adaptation notes.** The two-column offer-plus-headline block is the most portable piece here — any brand can drop a percent, a dollar figure, or a "buy X get Y" phrase into that giant-numeral slot without touching the rest of the layout. It needs a product that reads clearly from a top-down or three-quarter angle with visible branding on the surface itself; products that only read well front-on (bottles, boxes) would need a different angle treatment to earn their space next to type this large. The CTA button is optional dressing — dropping it costs little since the numeral and headline already carry the whole message — but the layout does depend on having a product asset with legible on-package branding, since there's no separate zone here for a logo callout on the product itself.
