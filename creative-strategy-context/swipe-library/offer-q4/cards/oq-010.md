**Layout forensics.** A pale cream-to-champagne gradient, lighter toward the centre, fills the whole canvas. In the top-left, a large bold maroon script-adjacent headline runs two lines and is by far the darkest, boldest type on the page. The brand's pink brush-script wordmark sits alone in the top-right corner, small and unaccompanied by any other type. Below both, a five-cell grid fills the bottom two-thirds of the frame: each cell holds a small glossy 3D-rendered icon (a keychain pill case, the product pouch, a ribboned gift box, a glass storage jar, a shipping box) with a caption beneath it where the word "FREE" is set in hot-pink bold and the rest of the label sits in plain black. The product itself is rendered at the same icon scale as the other four items, given no special lighting, podium, or size advantage over the surrounding gift icons. First read is the maroon headline, since it is the largest and highest-contrast type on the page, sitting alone in open space. Second is the five-icon grid as a whole, since its repetition and color-blocked "FREE" labels pull the eye across it in a scanning pattern. Third is the product icon specifically, which only registers once the eye has already scanned the grid, since nothing visually elevates it above its neighbors.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe the barrier to trying the product is basically zero, because the product itself is presented as just one line item in a pile of free things rather than the star of the ad. The headline sets the expectation ("free starter kit"), and the grid delivers on it visually by making every item, including the actual product, look equally free and equally easy to receive — which works for an offer because it reframes the ask from "buy this" to "claim your kit," lowering the perceived commitment before price ever enters the conversation.

**Copy skeleton.** Headline: announce the free kit, mechanic is a short imperative, 5 words. List: name each included item with a repeated "FREE" lead-in, mechanic is a five-item bulleted list, 21 words across five lines.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs enough real bundled extras to fill four or five grid cells convincingly; a brand with only one or two genuine free-gift items should shrink the grid rather than pad it with invented items, since a fabricated free-gift count breaks the same trust a fabricated review count would. The generic prop icons (pill case, gift box, jar, shipping box) are not brand-specific and can be swapped for whatever a given brand's real bundle actually includes. If a brand has no bundle to offer at all, this layout does not survive a mechanic swap cleanly — it is built specifically around the "here is everything in the kit" read, so a percent-off or subscription offer is better served by a different template.
