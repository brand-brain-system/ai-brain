**Layout forensics.** A soft pink solid wash fills the frame with a small magenta rounded pill badge centred at the very top. Directly beneath it is the price block: the old price in plain black with a single diagonal strike through it, sitting beside a much larger hot-pink bold new price with a small "per bag" suffix tucked to its side — the size jump between the two numbers is the loudest single move on the page. The product, one flexible pouch, lies diagonally across the centre of the frame with no podium or prop, resting directly on the flat backdrop so its own splashy label art carries the visual weight. A small maroon starburst sticker overlaps the pouch's top-right edge with a short bold white line inside it. In the lower-left corner sits a two-line pink bold subhead followed by a three-line benefit list, each line led by a plus-in-circle icon. First read is the price block, since the size contrast between the struck old number and the huge new number is built specifically to be read in under a second. Second is the product itself, filling the centre of the frame at a dynamic diagonal angle. Third is the starburst gift badge, small but high-contrast against the pink pouch panel it sits on. Fourth is the benefit list, tucked low and left, read only once the offer has landed.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe they are catching a steep, already-marked-down price rather than a starting price — the strike-through does the anchoring, and the diagonal product placement keeps the layout feeling energetic rather than like a plain price tag. The pairing works for an offer because the eye is forced through the discount math before it ever reaches the benefit copy, so the value read happens first and the product's actual case gets made only after the deal already feels good.

**Copy skeleton.** Badge: name the sale, mechanic is a short label, 4 words. Price: anchor the old price against the new one, mechanic is a numeral pair, 3 words. Badge (starburst): name the gift count, mechanic is a short numeral claim, 3 words. Subhead: frame the daily habit and the payoff window, mechanic is a short claim, 6 words. List: name three benefits, mechanic is a bulleted list, 7 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a genuine "was" price to anchor against — without one, the strikethrough device cannot run honestly and should be replaced with a plain percent-off or dollar-off badge instead. The diagonal-pouch placement works for any single flexible-pouch product; a rigid bottle or box can still run the layout by tilting on its base rather than lying flat. The starburst gift sticker and benefit list are optional value-adds that can shrink, grow, or swap mechanics (subscription perk, bundle item) without breaking the layout, since neither is load-bearing for the price-anchor read.
