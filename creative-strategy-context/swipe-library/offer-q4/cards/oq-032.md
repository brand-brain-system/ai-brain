**Layout forensics.** The top half is a three-way photo composite: the same leggings shown from front, side, and back on a cropped torso-to-leg model shot, all set against a plain white backdrop so the pattern and fit read clearly from every angle. The bottom half stacks four solid-color text blocks like a ticker: a coral-red full-width banner in bold white caps, a yellow full-width banner in bold black text just below it, a dark maroon full-width banner in bold white caps beneath that, and finally the price treatment at the very bottom, where a small struck-through black price sits beside a much larger price rendered in bold red with a heavy black outline, with a small label trailing it. Each colored block reads as its own discrete strip, stacked directly on top of the next with no gap, so the lower half feels like a stack of stickers rather than one designed panel. Eye path: the giant outlined price at the very bottom pulls first (size, color, and the struck-through companion price that signals "before/after"), the red banner second (the boldest, most saturated block, positioned to be read as the headline of the offer stack), the three-angle photo third (it's the largest area of the frame but the lowest-contrast, softest part of the composition).

**The story this ad tells.** This ad proves fit and shows a steep price drop in the same glance: three angles of the same product answer "does this fit my body type" while the stacked banners answer "how much does it cost right now." The price-anchor device (small struck price next to a giant new price) does the emotional work of making the discount feel dramatic, and stacking it dead last means the reader has already looked at the product before they hit the number, which reads as evaluate-then-convince rather than lead-with-the-deal.

**Copy skeleton.** Badge — the sale name, the loudest single claim, 2 words. Subhead — the time-bound urgency qualifier, 3 words. Offer — the specific product claim being sold at this price, 5 words. Price — the was/now price pair plus a small unit label, 4 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a product that photographs well from three angles on a body or a flat surface; a brand without on-model photography could swap the three-panel composite for three angles of a packshot instead, since the banners and price stack don't require a human at all. The strikethrough price pair swaps to a percent off, a bundle, or a buy-X-get-Y line with a straight copy and graphic change, since each colored block is just solid-fill text, not a fixed device. The time-bound "24-hour" language in the yellow block is a genuine deadline claim drawn on the image, so a brand without a real time-boxed offer should drop or soften that block rather than invent one, since invented urgency is a hard no under any brand's claims discipline.
