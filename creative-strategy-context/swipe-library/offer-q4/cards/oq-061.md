**Layout forensics.** The frame is a tall vertical charcoal-gray backdrop with a slightly lighter gray shelf surface below. The top third carries a two-line headline: a shorter line in light-blue bold caps naming the sale event, directly above a single massive word set in white hollow outline only — no fill, just stroke — so it reads almost like a stencil and takes up nearly the full width of the frame. Below that, five identical stick-format containers stand shoulder to shoulder across the shelf, each labeled with a different scent name, flanked by seasonal styling props: pine cones and a curl of cinnamon bark on the left, a halved lime and another cinnamon curl at center, and a spray of eucalyptus leaves on the right — the props do double duty as fall-holiday set dressing and as visual cues for the scent lineup. Overlapping the right edge of the product row, breaking out of the rectangular frame, sits a solid dark-blue circle badge carrying the discount and gift claim in bold white type, stacked so the percentage reads largest. The brand wordmark sits centered at the very bottom in clean white type, closing the frame. The eye lands first on the giant hollow-outline word (largest single shape, unmissable negative-space contrast against the dark backdrop), second on the product lineup with its seasonal props (visually rich, centrally placed, full width), third on the blue circle badge (smaller, but the only saturated color block in an otherwise gray-and-white frame).

**The story this ad tells.** This wants the viewer to believe they're getting ahead of the crowd on a real, tangible product line, styled here almost like a seasonal gift display rather than a generic promo graphic. The seasonal props around the products do quiet work making a fairly plain lineup feel like an autumn gift-guide moment, which softens the loud sale typography above it. The layout works for an offer because the giant hollow word earns the scroll-stop on pure scale, then hands off calmly to a real product shelf and a contained, specific badge — nothing above the fold is asking the viewer to read fine print to understand the deal.

**Copy skeleton.** Eyebrow — name the sale timing as a pre-emptive claim, 3 words. Headline — one massive word declaring the event type, 1 word. Offer — a percent-off number plus a free-gift count, numeral-led on both halves, 7 words total. Logo — brand wordmark only, no persuasive copy.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout survives for any brand with a multi-SKU lineup that reads well shoulder to shoulder (scents, flavors, colors) and a genuine seasonal or gifting angle to lean on with props — swap the five sticks for the brand's own SKU row and the seasonal decor for props that fit the brand's category and season. The circle badge is mechanic-flexible and can carry percent off, dollar off, or a bundle price with no structural change. It breaks if the brand has only one SKU, since the whole centre zone is built around a row of variants; a single product would need to be re-blocked as a hero shot rather than a lineup, which changes the composition's balance. The seasonal prop styling is optional dressing and can drop for a cleaner product-only shelf without hurting the hierarchy.
