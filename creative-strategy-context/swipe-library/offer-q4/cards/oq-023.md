**Layout forensics.** The whole ad is one tight UGC-style crop: a woman's bare midsection fills the top half of the frame, both hands gripping and pulling the leggings' waistband outward in a classic "look how stretchy this is" gesture. The offer isn't a graphic device at all, it's handwritten in bold black marker strokes directly on her skin, two centered lines stacked in the exact middle of the frame where the eye would land anyway. Below that, the leggings themselves take up the entire lower half in a busy diagonal-stripe-and-dot knit pattern that reads as activewear-specific texture, not plain fabric. A flat black bar runs across the very bottom edge, the kind of crop artifact that signals this was pulled from a video and gives the still a native, unpolished feel rather than a designed one. Eye path: the marker-written offer first, since it's centered, high-contrast black-on-skin, and sits right at the frame's natural center of gravity; the leggings' pattern and the pulling gesture second, since it's the largest single mass of texture and motion in the shot; the plain white backdrop and black letterbox bar last, functioning only as framing.

**The story this ad tells.** This ad borrows the visual honesty of organic content, handwriting the offer straight onto skin the way a friend might scrawl a note, rather than dressing it up in brand graphics. The hands-pulling-the-waistband gesture does the product demonstration work a spec sheet would otherwise need to do, showing stretch and fit in one frame. It works as an offer format because the deal feels like a personal tip being passed along rather than an ad unit, which matters most for a product category (shapewear/leggings) where trust in fit claims is the real barrier.

**Copy skeleton.** Offer — the sale name plus the buy-more mechanic, written as if scrawled by hand, names the deal directly, 4 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This layout needs a real UGC-style body or hands photo and a product that can be worn and demonstrated with a simple gesture; a brand without on-model UGC assets could still run this with a hand-holding-product close-up substituting for the torso shot, since the marker-on-skin text works on any bare skin surface, not just a midsection. The buy-X-get-Y mechanic swaps cleanly to a percent off, dollar off, or free-gift line since it's just two lines of handwritten text; a review count or star rating cannot be added here without breaking the intimate, unbranded feel that makes the format work, so proof elements should stay off this template rather than get bolted on. A brand without any UGC or influencer content simply cannot run this layout as-is.

**Prompt skeleton.** Use the attached images as brand reference. Create: a 1:1 UGC-style ad with a plain white studio backdrop. Bottom edge: a thin black letterbox bar for a native video-still look. Shot on a phone camera, flat even studio light, candid and unpolished. 1:1 aspect ratio.
