## Body

**Layout forensics.**

A soft gradient fills the top of the frame behind an oversized two-line white headline in bold sans-serif all-caps, framing the offer as a complete system. Directly below, a solid banner bar spans the full width, divided into five equal segments by thin vertical rules, each holding a two-word white benefit label naming a different function. Below the bar, three distinct product containers stand side by side at a slight angle, each with its own label and cap color, photographed in bright even studio light against the pale background. In the lower-left corner, entering from the bottom edge, a hand holds a small product detail up toward the camera. A small brand icon sits above a wordmark in the bottom-left corner. First thing the eye lands on: the oversized white headline, the most contrast and largest scale on the page. Second: the benefit bar, since its five-way segmentation reads as a list before any word is parsed. Third: the row of distinct products, where the headline's "system" claim becomes literal.

**The story this ad tells.**

This ad tells the viewer "this isn't one product, it's a complete routine," making that case by naming several distinct functional benefits in a single scannable bar and backing each one with a visually distinct product standing in for it. The headline sets the ambition, the benefit bar breaks it into concrete, individually named jobs, and the product row plus the hand detail prove there's real, physical substance behind the claim rather than one item wearing several labels. This layout carries multi-product, multi-benefit offers best — it needs enough real product range to fill each benefit slot honestly rather than one SKU covering several claims.

**Copy skeleton.**

- headline: hook (frames the offer as one system, not separate products), statement, 4 words
- list: prove (distinct, named functional benefits, one per product), list, 10 words / 5 items

**Adaptation notes.**

Needs a real multi-SKU lineup where each product genuinely does a different job — the benefit labels only work if there's a real function behind each one; forcing one product to cover multiple labels breaks the "system" claim. Swaps that keep the layout intact: the number of benefit segments (match it to how many real SKUs/benefits the brand actually has, rather than padding to a fixed count), the gradient color, the detail shown in the hand. Swaps that break it: labeling a benefit that isn't substantiated by the product's actual formulation (this reads as an efficacy claim and needs sign-off), or showing products in the bar that don't correspond to anything actually sold. This reference image does not show the products nested inside any bundle packaging or gift box, despite the template's name — they stand independently on the gradient background, so the "bundle" idea here is carried by the benefit bar and headline, not by a physical box; and the lower-frame prop in this reference is a close-up of loose product rather than a lifestyle object, so treat that slot as swappable to whatever detail shot suits the brand. The hand-in-frame device needs a real hands asset; flag for content-filter review since a cropped hand close-up can trigger likeness/generation checks.

**Fit note.** multi-SKU systems/kits/bundles | BOF | real bundle + 5 benefit labels | energetic tone. Exclude for single-SKU.
