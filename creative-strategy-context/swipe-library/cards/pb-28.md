## Layout forensics

A warm cream textured background carries the whole frame. At the top, a slim italic serif line sits directly above a massive bold black headline set in two words — the headline is by far the heaviest single mark on the page and dominates the top third. Below it, a hand holds the product tin at a natural, slightly downward angle dead-centre. Four short callout lines are arranged radially around the product rather than stacked — one upper-left, one upper-right, one lower-left, one lower-right — each connected to the product by a curved, deliberately hand-drawn (not ruler-straight) arrow in dark brown, so the eye follows arrow-to-claim-to-arrow-to-claim around the product like a clock face. A full-width dark-brown banner closes the bottom edge with a short offer line in light type. First landing is the giant black headline (scale wins); second the product the arrows all point at; third the four callouts, read in whatever order the arrows suggest rather than strict top-to-bottom.

## The story this ad tells

It wants the viewer to believe the product does several distinct things at once, and that those things are worth annotating like a diagram rather than listing like a spec sheet. The hand-drawn arrows do the persuasive work here — they turn a product photo into an explainer, implying a person walked up and pointed out each feature live. This layout carries multi-benefit, editorial-feeling product intros best, especially where the offer (a sale or bundle) is secondary to the feature story rather than the headline itself.

## Copy skeleton

Subhead (hook) — sets up the category reframe, statement, 6 words.
Headline (statement) — the category-defining claim, 2 words.
List (explain) — four short benefit callouts, arrow-linked, 17 words total across 4 items.
Offer (direct) — banner CTA/promo line, command, 5 words.

## Adaptation notes

Needs four genuinely distinct product features or ingredients that can each be reduced to a 2–4 word callout — vague or overlapping claims make the radial-arrow device look padded. The offer banner needs a real, currently-running promotion; if there is none, the honest swap is a plain CTA line ('Shop now') in the same banner position rather than inventing a sale. The product needs to be photographable held naturally in one hand at a slight angle — a product that can't be handheld (large format, fragile, multi-piece) breaks the central device and needs a tabletop hero shot instead. A hand is shown but no face, so content-filter risk is low; keep the hand naturally posed, not stylised, to avoid an uncanny result.
