## Layout forensics

A single casual overhead photo fills the frame — a hand holding the product jar over a lived-in desk scene (succulent, notebook, water glass, a couple of loose capsules), shot in natural daylight the way a phone camera would, not a studio. Scattered across it are five rounded text bubbles built to look like a phone's built-in story-text tool, each with its own solid highlight colour so no two adjacent bubbles blend together. The topic bubble sits near the top, short and bold with an emoji, and is the boldest single mark on the page. Below it a longer educational bubble states a surprising body fact, then a dark bubble explains the product's specific mechanism at length, then near the bottom a short personal-result bubble and a short endorsement bubble close the story. Placement is deliberately uneven — hand-placed, not gridded — which is core to the native read. First landing is the overall photo and the topic bubble together (the boldest, highest-contrast bubble); second the longer explanatory bubbles; third the closing personal-result and endorsement bubbles.

## The story this ad tells

It wants the viewer to believe they're looking at a real person's own story slides, not paid media — the uneven bubble placement, the casual desk setting, and the first-person, typo-tolerant voice all work together to read as authentic documentation rather than a pitch. This layout carries an educate-then-personally-validate arc best: a surprising category fact, then the product's specific answer to it, then a real early-use update and a one-line endorsement. It needs a product with a genuine mechanism worth explaining, not just a flavour or a look.

## Copy skeleton

Quote (hook) — topic name plus emoji, 2 words.
Body (educate) — surprising stat or mechanism about the category, statement, 16 words.
Body (explain) — specific product mechanism, excited informal tone, 23 words.
Quote (prove) — personal early-experience result, first-person, 15 words.
Quote (endorse) — one-line approval, 5 words.

## Adaptation notes

2026-09-16 (For Wellness engine run): a product zone was added to the frontmatter so the prompt builder attaches the packshot as Image 2; the frontmatter already said product: in_hand but the zone list had only the photo.

Needs a real, checkable category stat (the kind of fact a knowledgeable user would actually know) and a genuine product mechanism to explain — an invented statistic here is exactly the claim-sensitive risk this layout carries, since the education bubble reads as fact rather than opinion. The personal-result bubble needs to be a real early-use account, not a fabricated testimonial; if no real user account exists, the honest swap is to soften it to a general expectation-setting line rather than a first-person result claim. A hand is shown but no face, so content-filter risk stays low; keep the desk scene genuinely lived-in rather than overly styled to protect the native read.
