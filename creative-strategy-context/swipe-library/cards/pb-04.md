## Body

**Layout forensics.** A bold mauve/pink serif headline spans two centered lines at the top of an all-white frame — the clear first read. Below it, four separate product shots (a squeeze tube, two open compacts, a lipstick) are arranged in a loose row at roughly the vertical middle, each product small and evenly studio-lit — this reads second, as a unit, before the eye starts working the individual callouts. Four callout boxes surround the row, one above-left, one above-right, one below-left, one below-right, each built the same way: a small filled pink dot connected by a thin pink line to its product, a bold black all-caps product-name label, and a line or two of regular-weight black descriptive copy beneath it. A centered website URL sits in plain black text near the bottom, and the brand's serif wordmark closes the frame bottom right, third and fourth reads respectively.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe a bundled kit delivers real, specific value across several distinct needs at once — each callout makes one concrete, differentiated claim (a formula detail, a wear-time claim, a skin-feel claim, a closure detail) tied visually by a leader line to the exact product it describes, so nothing reads as generic filler. This is a diagram, not a story: it's built to be scanned in four quick beats, one per product, and works best when a brand has a genuine multi-item kit with one crisp fact per item rather than a single hero product with four vague adjectives.

**Copy skeleton.** headline — hook — statement — 6 words. list (callout 1) — explain — label + statement — 6 words. list (callout 2) — explain — label + statement — 10 words. list (callout 3) — explain — label + statement — 9 words. list (callout 4) — explain — label + statement — 5 words. body (url) — tell them what to do — statement — 1 word.

**Adaptation notes.** This reference is built around a genuine four-product kit, not four benefits of a single product — the source prompt describes one centered product with four benefit callouts pointing at it, but the actual image shows four different SKUs, each with its own callout. Only route a brand here if it has a real multi-item bundle; a single-SKU brand should use the stat-surround or bullet-point templates instead, since collapsing this layout to one product breaks the leader-line device. Each callout needs one specific, substantiated claim (an ingredient, a formula detail, a wear-time or texture fact) — generic adjectives ("amazing," "the best") will read as filler against this diagram's precise, clinical tone. No people in frame, so no content-filter risk.
