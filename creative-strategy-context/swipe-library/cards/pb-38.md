## Body

**Layout forensics.** A clean vertical split. The left ~55% is a candid, warm-toned lifestyle photo of a person mid-sip, drink in hand, natural café light, genuinely relaxed expression — no visible branding in this half at all, it's pure mood and context. The right ~45% is a flat, saturated solid-color panel carrying everything transactional: small sparkle accents scattered top-right, the product can floating center-right at a slight angle under clean studio lighting, and below it a white rounded review card stacking five filled stars over a short italic-style quote set in the brand color. The wordmark logo sits centered at the very bottom of the color panel with matching sparkle accents flanking it. Eye path: the human face and gesture on the left pulls focus first (faces always do), then the product floating against the saturated color reads second, then the review card closes the loop with a specific, short quote.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe this is a moment a real customer actually lived — the split composition keeps "real life" and "the pitch" physically separate but adjacent, so the lifestyle photo earns trust before the branded half asks for anything. Image and words divide labor cleanly: the photo sells the feeling, the quote sells the specific outcome. This layout carries a short, punchy first-person quote best — one line, not a paragraph, since the card has to read in under a second next to a photo doing the emotional heavy lifting.

**Copy skeleton.** quote — prove: land one short, specific first-person outcome; quote; 9 words. logo — hook: close on brand recognition; wordmark; 1 word.

**Adaptation notes.** Needs a real, short customer quote — this format can't carry a long review, so pick the single sharpest line rather than truncating a longer one awkwardly. Needs a genuine lifestyle photo of the product in actual use in a plausible setting; a generic stock photo with no visible connection to the product's use-case weakens the split's whole premise. Legal swap that keeps the layout intact: change the solid panel color and the lifestyle scene to match the brand's own palette and use-case. Swap that breaks it: using a quote that isn't a real customer's words, since the star rating right above it implies verified review provenance.
