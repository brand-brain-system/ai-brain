## Layout forensics

The frame is a hard vertical colour split. Left half sits on the example brand's warm tan; right half sits on flat pale grey. Each half opens with a header — a white wordmark logo top-left, a plain dark-grey category label ('Other chocolate bars') top-right — then a straight-on product photo, then a stacked benefit list. A comic-book 'VS' burst in dark brown, drawn with radiating spike lines, sits dead-centre on the seam and physically overlaps both product photos, which is what pulls the eye there first. The example brand's bar is shown with a corner square snapped off and angled forward for texture; the rival bar is a generic foil-wrapped block, unwrapped flat with no visible branding. Below each product, four rows of bold uppercase claims run in a tight vertical stack — green circle-checks on the left, red circle-X's on the right — each icon locked to the left edge of its line so the eye can scan down the icon column without reading every word. First landing is the VS burst (highest contrast, centre of frame); second is the two products it interrupts; third is the checklist columns, read top-to-bottom as a pair.

## The story this ad tells

It wants the viewer to believe the two products are being judged on the same scale and the example brand wins on every line. The colour split does the framing — warm/energetic versus flat/generic — before a single word is read, and the VS burst turns a side-by-side into a contest rather than a menu. This layout carries head-to-head spec or ingredient comparisons best: it needs four genuinely opposing facts, not just four features, because the whole device collapses if the rival column reads as a strawman rather than a real (if unbranded) competitor.

## Copy skeleton

Headline (compare) — names the rival category, states fact, 3 words.
List, left (prove) — four ingredient/spec wins, one line each, checkmark format, 13 words total across 4 items.
List, right (compare) — four matching rival weaknesses, same structure, 14 words total across 4 items.
Decor 'VS' — no copy, graphic device only.

## Adaptation notes

Needs four real, checkable contrasts against a genuine competitor category (ingredient specs, certifications, formulation facts) — soft or invented claims break the device instantly since the whole layout reads as a factual scoreboard. The rival product must stay generic/unbranded to avoid a direct callout; if the brand only has two or three real contrasts, drop to a 2–3 item list rather than padding to four with filler. No people shown, so no content-filter risk from faces or bodies. If claims touch health outcomes rather than plain specs (sugar content, ingredients, certifications), route through claim review before running.
