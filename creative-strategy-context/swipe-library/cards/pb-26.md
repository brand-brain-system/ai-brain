## Layout forensics

A clean 50/50 horizontal split. The top half is a warm, soft-focus lifestyle photo — two hands preparing the product with the packaged box resting beside it, shot in warm natural light so it reads as intimate rather than clinical. The bottom half opens on a thin dark-to-transparent gradient that darkens toward the base; a centred wordmark logo sits just below the seam, flanked by two thin horizontal rules that act as a formal divider between photo and claim. Below that, four lines of bold white uppercase text run centre-aligned, with the two key result phrases picked out in a soft pink highlight block so they read as a distinct visual unit inside the sentence. The stat itself carries the whole bottom half — there is no separate subhead. Eye lands on the photo first (largest, warmest area), then drops straight to the highlighted percentage phrases (highest contrast against the dark gradient), then the logo anchors it as the brand doing the claiming.

## The story this ad tells

It wants the viewer to believe a real usage study backs the product, and that the brand is confident enough to make the data the headline rather than burying it under a tagline. The soft lifestyle photo builds warmth and normalcy before the claim lands, so the stat reads as credible testimony rather than an ad boast. This layout carries verified-percentage, study-backed claims best — it is built for brands with a real number to put in bold type, not for general benefit statements dressed up as data.

## Copy skeleton

Stat (prove) — one sentence, two embedded percentages, highlighted phrases, 19 words total.

## Adaptation notes

Requires a genuinely verified usage statistic or study result — this is the exact layout that needs human claim review before running, since the percentage figures are the entire ad. Without real data, the honest swap is to drop the percentages and run a plain benefit statement in the same position, though that weakens the layout's core mechanic (the stat IS the headline). The lifestyle photo needs real hands and real packaging, not a stock swap, to keep the testimony feel intact. No faces shown, so no body/face content-filter risk.
