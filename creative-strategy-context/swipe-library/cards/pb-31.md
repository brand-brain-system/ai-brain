## Layout forensics

A literal table on plain white, ruled with thin black lines like a spreadsheet or a meme format. The top row is split 50/50: the example brand's bag on the left, photographed clean with product spilling out for texture; the rival bag on the right, shot flat and plain. Below that, three full-width rows each split by a thin black vertical line into a left/right pair of short bold black serif statements — no icons, no colour, no checkmarks, just typeset text doing the contrast. Each row pairs one plain-stated advantage against one plain-stated (sometimes blunt or dismissive) rival weakness. The total absence of colour or iconography is the point — it borrows the visual language of a deadpan internet meme rather than a designed ad. First landing is the two product photos at top (the only imagery on the page); second the grid rows, read row by row rather than column by column since each row is a single joke/contrast unit.

## The story this ad tells

It wants the viewer to believe the comparison is so obviously one-sided it doesn't need design to sell it — the plainness itself is the confidence move. Photo and copy split cleanly: the top row shows what's being compared, the grid below states the verdict without ornament. This layout carries blunt, almost comedic ingredient or quality contrasts best, for brands willing to be a little combative and where the copy alone (not styling) has to land the joke.

## Copy skeleton

List, left (compare) — three short brand-advantage statements, 10 words total across 3 items.
List, right (compare) — three matching rival-weakness statements, 9 words total across 3 items.

## Adaptation notes

Needs three real, specific, checkable contrasts against a genuine rival product — the deadpan tone only works if the claims are true and blunt, not exaggerated, since there's no visual device to soften an overreach. The rival product should stay identifiably a real competitor category rather than a strawman, or the meme format reads as unfair rather than funny. This is a copy-only device — no icons, no colour — so weak or generic one-liners have nowhere to hide; each row needs an actual laugh or gut-punch, not a bland feature note. No people shown, so no content-filter risk.
