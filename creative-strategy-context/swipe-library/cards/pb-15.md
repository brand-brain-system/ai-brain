## Body

**Layout forensics.**

On a plain white background, an oversized bold black headline spans the top of the frame in a single line, trailing off into an emoji that signals tone before any further text is read — written to name and address a specific audience segment directly. Beneath it, a light-gray rounded-rectangle comment card sits centered, styled exactly like a screenshotted social comment: a small circular profile photo in the top-left corner, a bold name beside it, and several sentences of conversational, specific, slightly messy review copy beneath — the kind of detail that reads as unmistakably real rather than written for advertising. A small gray timestamp sits just below the card, reinforcing that this is a captured moment rather than brand copy. Below that, the product is centered on the same plain white background, shot in soft, simple studio light with none of the polish of the comment card above it. There's no logo and no star rating anywhere on the page. First thing the eye lands on: the oversized black headline, the largest, highest-contrast element, written to feel like it's addressing the viewer directly. Second: the comment card, since its gray box reads as "someone else's words" before any sentence is parsed. Third: the product, which answers the question the headline and comment raised together.

**The story this ad tells.**

This ad tells the viewer "someone exactly like you already found the answer," using a headline that names a specific audience segment, a comment that reads as an unmistakably real, slightly messy screenshot, and a plain, undecorated product shot that deliberately avoids looking like an ad. Image and words split the job almost entirely by design: nothing here is polished on purpose — the whole point is that it should look like a screenshot someone took and dropped a product photo under, not like a brand made it. This layout carries raw, specific, first-person testimonials best, especially for categories where a very targeted audience segment needs to see themselves named before they'll keep reading.

**Copy skeleton.**

- headline: hook (names the exact audience segment, addresses them directly), statement, 4 words
- attribution: prove (a real first name plus last initial, grounds the comment as a real person), statement, 2 words
- body: prove (a specific, conversational, slightly-messy first-person account), statement, 42 words
- stat: prove (a timestamp, reinforces this was a real, recent comment), stat, 1 word

**Adaptation notes.**

Needs a real comment or review with specific, concrete, slightly imperfect detail — a smooth, ad-sounding paragraph undercuts the entire "this looks like a real screenshot" premise, so this only works with genuinely messy customer language. Needs a plain, undecorated product shot deliberately styled to look like an afterthought rather than hero photography. Swaps that keep the layout intact: the audience-segment headline, the reviewer's name and timestamp, the product shown beneath. Swaps that break it: polishing the comment copy into ad-voice, or adding a logo/star rating (both undercut the "raw, unbranded screenshot" premise this template depends on). No people are shown beyond a small profile photo, so content-filter risk is low, but that profile photo should be a real or clearly licensed image rather than a generated face used to imply a specific real reviewer.

**Fit note.** universal | TOF | REAL comment/review | raw organic tone.
