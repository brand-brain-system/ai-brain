## Body

**Layout forensics.** A slightly off-center, slightly grainy phone photo of a product sitting on a real kitchen counter, background softly blurred with warm daylight, a naturally curved product overflowing its bowl and a few loose pieces scattered around its base for casual realism. Stuck directly to the front of the product is a crooked yellow post-it note — genuinely trapezoid from the angle it was pressed on, a visible fold crease, held at the top by a small strip of tape rather than its own adhesive — carrying four lines of thick, uneven black-marker handwriting that reads like a real note someone left, not ad copy. Below the photo, on a plain off-white strip, sits a small, plainly-typed caption in lowercase — the kind of line someone types under an organic post, not a headline. A small wordmark sits bottom-right of that strip. No border, no logo overlay inside the photo itself — the brand identity in-frame comes entirely from the product's own packaging. Eye path: the product itself first (large, centered, appetizing), then the handwritten note stuck to it (the novel, curiosity-driving element), then the small caption and wordmark closing the frame.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe they've found a real photo with a real post-it note left by a real person — every technical imperfection (the crooked note, the grain, the off-center crop, the tape instead of a sticker) is a deliberate authenticity cue, not a mistake. Image and words split the job: the photo supplies the "found, not composed" premise, and the handwritten line supplies the hook or joke that gives the post a reason to exist. This layout carries a short, punchy, personality-forward line best — three or four lines of casual voice, not a marketing claim, since the entire format collapses the moment the copy sounds written by a brand.

**Copy skeleton.** body — hook: land a short, funny or blunt hand-written line; statement; 13 words across 4 lines. body — hook: reinforce with a casually-typed caption underneath; statement; 7 words.
