## Body

**Layout forensics.**

Full-bleed photographic background shows three of the brand's product pouches side by side, softly defocused in warm beige studio light — ordinary product photography until the review card lands on top of it. Centered in the upper-middle third, a white rounded-rectangle card with a soft drop shadow carries the whole device: a gray circular default-avatar placeholder, a bold username, a one-star-of-five gold rating, and an orange "Verified Purchase" pill — standard review-platform chrome. Directly under the stars sits a short bold black headline that reads as a complaint at a glance, followed by four lines of regular-weight body copy that flip it into a rave once actually read. The card runs edge to edge horizontally and takes up roughly the center 45% of the frame vertically, so it dominates the composition before a single word is read. Below the card, a full-width black bar carries an oversized bold white punchline, with a small circular brand monogram tucked into its lower-right corner. First thing the eye lands on: the single gold star sitting inside an otherwise all-white card — the visual anomaly that stops the scroll. Second: the bold black headline underneath it, which reads as a warning. Third: the black punchline bar at the bottom, the reveal that recontextualizes everything above it.

**The story this ad tells.**

This ad tells the viewer "don't trust the star rating, read the words" — it weaponizes the pattern-recognition of a one-star rating (universally read as a red flag) to force a stop and a re-read, then rewards that re-read with a specific, genuine rave. The image (blurred product lineup, real review UI) supplies category context and credibility while the copy carries the entire joke and the entire proof; the closing bar exists purely to make sure nobody scrolls past without landing the twist. This layout carries curiosity-driven, humor-forward hooks best — it needs a real customer review that genuinely reads negative on first parse and clearly positive on second, which is a narrow, specific kind of review to source.

**Copy skeleton.**

- attribution: prove (a handle behind the "reviewer"), statement, 1 word
- headline: hook (the bait line — reads negative, forces a second look), statement, 5 words
- body: prove (the actual rave, delivers the twist and the specific before/after), statement, 37 words
- badge: land the twist (reframes everything above as positive), statement, 4 words

**Adaptation notes.**

Requires a real, verbatim customer review that genuinely sounds negative in its opening line but is unambiguously positive once read in full — this can't be written from a blank page without reading as fake, and a review that's only mildly ambiguous will fail the "wait, what?" beat. Needs blurred product photography of the brand's actual lineup in the background; a single hero packshot works fine if there's only one SKU. The one-of-five star rating is load-bearing — don't swap to two or more stars, the hook depends on the worst possible score. Swaps that keep the layout intact: the review-platform styling, the handle name, the closing punchline line. Swaps that break it: dropping the star rating, or using a review that's actually negative (this device only works as bait-and-switch, never as genuine negative proof). No people are shown, so there is no content-filter/likeness risk.

**Fit note.** humor-tolerant brands | TOF | a REAL review that sounds negative but is praise | playful tone.
