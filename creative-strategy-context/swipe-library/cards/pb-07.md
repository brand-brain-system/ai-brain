## Body

**Layout forensics.** The frame splits vertically down the middle: a muted charcoal-gray panel on the left, a black panel on the right with a warm orange radial glow concentrated behind the right-hand product. A white circle carrying a bold orange "VS" sits centered at the seam, top — this is the first thing the eye catches, since it's the only pure-white shape on the page and sits dead center. From there the eye splits: left header in bold gray caps names the generic category, right header is the brand's own wordmark logo rendered in white with an orange accent dot — second read, establishing who's who. Below each header, a single product sits centered in its half: a plain unlabeled white bottle on the left (a deliberately generic stand-in), a dark, glowing branded tube on the right — third read, since the orange glow pulls extra attention to the brand's own product. The bottom half of each panel carries a five-line vertical list, red X icons on the left (weaknesses), orange checkmarks on the right (strengths) — fourth read, working top to bottom in parallel. A small version of the brand wordmark repeats bottom right, closing the frame.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe the entire category has the same set of flaws and this one brand alone has fixed every one of them — the two headers frame it as brand versus category rather than brand versus a named competitor (which sidesteps any legal risk from naming a rival), the mirrored five-item lists let the eye read each weakness against its direct strength counterpart, and the glow on the brand's product visually signals "this one is the answer" without saying it. This layout carries five honest, specific, one-to-one contrasts best — it collapses if the strengths are vague restatements of "we're better" rather than concrete, checkable facts.

**Copy skeleton.** eyebrow (left header) — compare — statement — 2 words. badge (VS) — compare — statement — 1 word. list (left, weaknesses) — compare — five-item list — 15 words. list (right, strengths) — compare — five-item list — 24 words.

**Adaptation notes.** Needs five honest, specific strengths that map one-to-one against five category weaknesses — the "competitor" side can stay generic and unbranded (no real rival product or logo required, which keeps legal risk low), but every claim on the brand's own side must be substantiated with real facts (ingredient counts, testing claims, guarantee terms), not marketing adjectives. This instance carries an efficacy claim ("builds natural [X] production over 90 days") that needs legal/compliance review before running, and any brand adaptation that keeps a numeric or time-bound efficacy claim on the strengths list should get the same review. No people in frame, so no content-filter risk.
