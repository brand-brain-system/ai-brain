## Body

**Layout forensics.** The frame is built to be mistaken for a phone screenshot of a news site, not an ad. The top quarter is plain white and carries a large bold serif masthead centered at the very top, with a small-caps regional line directly under it — the example brand used a "Daily Mail Australia" style wordmark. A thin gray rule separates the masthead from the body. Below it, small gray "Latest Headlines" eyebrow text sits left-aligned, doing nothing but reinforcing "this is a real feed." The headline dominates the upper-middle third: bold black serif, full width, opening on a curly-quoted first-person line before rolling into the descriptive rest of the sentence — price, review count and use-case all folded into one run-on. The bottom 60% is two square-cropped selfie photos side by side, divided by a thin white gutter, each person holding the product tube up beside her face in a natural, unstaged pose — one in cool daylight with a phone visible in a mirror, one in warm lamp light. No logo, no CTA, no button anywhere. The eye goes: masthead first (it reads "legitimate publication" before anything else registers), then the headline (the actual claim, in quote form), then the two faces confirming real people said it.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe they've stumbled on editorial coverage of a product, not an ad for it — the masthead borrows a real news outlet's authority, the headline is written exactly like a clickbait consumer-journalism hook ("'It's my holy grail product': The $X category with over N five-star reviews that also does Y"), and the two ordinary-looking selfies read as reader-submitted proof rather than brand photography. Image and words do the same job from two directions: the copy supplies the specific, quotable claim (price, review count, secondary use case), while the photos supply the human plausibility that a review count alone can't. This layout carries a strong quote plus a real number best — it collapses "customer says," "press says" and "here's the number" into one glance, which makes it the highest-authority-per-second reviews template in the set, provided the review count is real and the masthead isn't lifted from an actual outlet.

**Copy skeleton.** logo — hook: establish setting-as-news-source; statement; 3 words. eyebrow — hook: reinforce feed authenticity; statement; 2 words. headline — prove: fold a real quote, a real price and a real review count into one press-style claim; quote + stat; 19 words.

**Adaptation notes.** Needs a real customer quote that reads as a natural soundbite ("it's my holy grail product"), a real price, and a real review count — none of these can be invented. Two real or convincingly real customer photos holding the product are required; stock or studio photography breaks the native illusion immediately. Legal swap that keeps the layout intact: use a generic, unbranded masthead style ("Daily Insider," "Today's Edge") rather than an actual publication's name and logotype unless the brand genuinely secured that feature — using a real outlet's wordmark without a real feature is a compliance risk, not a style choice. Swap that breaks the layout: replacing either selfie with an obviously staged studio shot, which collapses the "this is organic" premise the whole template runs on.
