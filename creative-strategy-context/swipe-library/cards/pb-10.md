## Body

**Layout forensics.**

On an off-white, lightly textured background with generous margins, the top eighth carries a small, wide-tracked eyebrow line in the brand's accent color, centered. Directly beneath it, five grayscale publication logos run left to right in a single row, evenly spaced and equal in visual weight — no single outlet is favored over another. The middle third is the ad's real center of gravity: a three-line italic serif pull-quote in the same accent color, large enough to read at a scroll-speed glance, with a shorter attribution line sitting quietly below it in smaller, regular-weight dark sans. The lower third drops to a single product shot on a raw wood surface with a small natural prop beside it, soft directional side light giving it a tactile, editorial feel rather than a flat studio one. The brand's stacked wordmark sits small in the bottom-left corner — the only branding on the page besides the product's own label. First thing the eye lands on: the publication-logo row, since a named credibility line plus five recognizable marks is the fastest trust signal on the page. Second: the italic pull-quote, the largest text block. Third: the product itself, which closes the loop the quote opened.

**The story this ad tells.**

This ad tells the viewer "don't take our word for it — take theirs," borrowing the authority of several real outlets to pre-validate a single, specific quote before the product ever appears. Image and words split cleanly: the logos and quote do all the persuading, and the product photo simply confirms what's being discussed, styled with enough polish that it reads as belonging in the same spread as the press it's citing. This layout carries premium, credibility-first messaging best — it works only when there's a real, attributable press mention to run, and it undersells anything urgent or promotional since nothing on the page pushes toward a call to action.

**Copy skeleton.**

- eyebrow: hook (signals third-party validation before any claim is read), statement, 3 words
- logo: prove (recognizable outlet marks carry credibility without a word of copy), list, 5 items
- quote: prove (the actual press endorsement, does the persuading), quote, 20 words
- attribution: prove (source, publication and year — makes the quote checkable), statement, 6 words

**Adaptation notes.**

Needs a genuine press mention — a real quote from a real publication with a real attribution — this is the one template that can't be faked with a written-to-sound-like-press line; without real press, this card shouldn't be matched. The five-logo row assumes five real outlets; brands with fewer should run as many as they actually have rather than padding with unrelated names. Swaps that keep the layout intact: which publications appear, the quote itself, the product's styling. Swaps that break it: inventing a quote, or attributing a real quote to the wrong outlet (both a trust risk and a legal one). No people are shown, so there is no content-filter risk.

**Fit note.** brands with REAL press | TOF/BOF | real press quote + real publication logos | premium tone. Exclude if no press.
