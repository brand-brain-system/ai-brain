## Body

**Layout forensics.**

A solid color block fills the entire frame. In the upper third, a bold italic serif quote in white with curly quotation marks sits centered, large enough to read at scroll speed — pulled straight from the review below it. Directly beneath, five filled gold stars run in a single horizontal row, reinforcing the rating without adding words. The lower half is dominated by a white rounded-corner review card, anchored bottom-left and overlapping the color block: a gray circular default-avatar icon sits beside a bold name with a small flag emoji, a verified-checkmark line beneath it, then several lines of regular-weight review copy long enough to require truncation — it cuts off mid-sentence into a bold, brand-colored "read more" prompt, and below that a small gray helpfulness line with a thumbs-up icon and a count. Bottom-right, overlapping both the card and the color block, a small cluster of products sits angled toward the viewer, crisp and sharply lit against the softer background. First thing the eye lands on: the large white italic quote, the highest-contrast text on the page. Second: the review card as a shape, since its white rectangle reads as social-proof chrome before any word is parsed. Third: the product cluster bottom-right, which closes the loop between the review and what it's a review of.

**The story this ad tells.**

This ad tells the viewer "a real person just like you was skeptical and got converted" — the pull-quote does the emotional hook, the review card supplies the credibility (a verified name, a flag, a helpfulness count), and the deliberately truncated body copy creates an open loop that keeps the eye moving instead of bouncing away. Image and words split cleanly: the color block and product photography carry the brand feel, while the review card does all of the actual persuading, styled to look exactly like a screenshot of a real review. This layout carries long-form, emotionally specific testimonials best — it needs a genuine review with a real narrative arc, not a one-liner, since the truncation device only works if there's enough real copy to cut off.

**Copy skeleton.**

- quote: hook (the review's most emotional clause, pulled out and enlarged), quote, 8 words
- attribution: prove (a real name plus a market-matching flag builds specificity), statement, 2 words
- body: prove (the full review narrative, skepticism to conversion), statement, 52 words
- cta: hook (the truncation itself, creates a "read more" curiosity gap), command, 2 words
- stat: prove (a helpfulness count signals other shoppers already validated it), stat, 5 words

**Adaptation notes.**

Requires one real, long-form customer review with an actual narrative arc — short reviews can't sustain the truncation device, and the pulled quote at the top must be a genuine phrase lifted from that same review, not written separately. Needs product photography for whatever the brand sells; a single hero packshot works fine, a multi-product lineup isn't required. Swaps that keep the layout intact: the color-block hue, the reviewer's name/flag/count, the product styling. Swaps that break it: fabricating or lightly editing the review copy (the device depends on this reading as unmistakably real), or dropping the star rating. No people are shown, so there is no content-filter risk.

**Fit note.** universal | TOF | one long emotional real review | trust tone. "...Read more" open loop.
