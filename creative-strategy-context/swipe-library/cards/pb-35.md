## Body

**Layout forensics.** A warm solid tan/gold field with three horizontal bands. The top band carries a bold black uppercase two-line headline stating a superlative claim, centered; directly beneath it sits a small white pill CTA button in bold uppercase brand-color text. The middle band is the hero: the product package angled diagonally, hero-lit like a studio shot, sitting inside a radial explosion of broken chocolate chunks, whole nuts, and cocoa dust scattered outward from the center — the mess is deliberate, built to convey indulgence and appetite appeal rather than clinical cleanliness. The bottom band is a single white pill-shaped stat bar spanning the width, split into three columns by thin vertical rules, each with an oversized bold number-plus-unit over a smaller uppercase label. Eye path: the bold headline claim lands first on the flat gold field, then the product itself pulls focus as the largest, most textured element in the frame, then the stat bar closes the case with hard numbers.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe the product delivers an indulgent, best-in-category eating experience without the usual nutritional trade-off — the superlative headline makes the emotional promise, the scattered ingredients around the product sell the sensory experience, and the stat bar backs it up with the numbers that make "healthiest" or "best-tasting" a claim you can check rather than just take on faith. This layout carries a strong superlative plus a short, punchy stat trio best — it's built for categories where taste and macros are both selling points, and it wants exactly three numbers, no more, kept oversized and scannable at a glance.

**Copy skeleton.** headline — hook: state the superlative claim; statement; 5 words. cta — tell them what to do: invite the click; command; 2 words. stat — prove: land three real nutrition numbers as the closing argument; stat ×3; 9 words across 3 items.

**Adaptation notes.** Needs a real superlative the brand can defend (best-tasting, world's healthiest) and three real, verifiable nutrition or usage numbers — grams of protein, sugar, carbs, whatever the category's proof points are. If the brand only has two solid stats, cut to two columns rather than padding a third with something soft; the bar's power is in each number being unambiguous. Legal swap that keeps the layout intact: swap in a different indulgent-food scattering (nuts, fruit, spice) that matches the actual product's flavor story. Swap that breaks it: using a superlative the brand can't stand behind, since the stat bar right underneath it invites exactly the scrutiny that would expose it.
