**Layout forensics.** Clean white background, no imagery except the product. The top ~20% carries an oversized bold black sans-serif headline set across two lines, dominating the frame on scale alone. Below it, left-aligned body copy in a smaller regular weight fills the middle 55% of the frame — thirteen short punchy lines broken by two paragraph gaps rather than run as prose paragraphs, giving it the rhythm of spoken argument. The pair of white leather sneakers sits bottom-center-left, angled three-quarter view with a soft contact shadow, product-only against the white ground, cropped tight at the very bottom edge. Eye order: the huge black headline first (pure scale against white), then the body copy block (it occupies the largest single area of the frame), then the product resolving the argument at the bottom.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe the price is fair because the brand refuses to cut the corners that would make it cheaper — the headline states the conclusion up front, then the body walks backward through the reasoning (acknowledge the objection, list what corner-cutting would cost, reframe as a positive, close on a confident brand statement). Image and words are almost entirely words: the product appears only at the very end, as the payoff after the argument has already been won, not as the thing selling itself. This layout carries a genuine price or value objection best — it needs a real tension the brand can defend point by point, and it breaks on vague or unverifiable claims.

**Copy skeleton.**
- headline — hook (conclusion-first statement) — short declarative claim — 4 words
- body — explain/remove risk (objection → reasoning → reframe → close) — short-line argument, 13 lines — 82 words

**Adaptation notes.** Needs a real price gap the brand can defend line by line (an actual competitor price point, actual named materials or sourcing details, an actual manufacturing claim) — every specific in the body copy needs to be true and defensible, since the whole format is built on specificity as proof. Swap-safe: the exact price figures, competitor price, material/sourcing details, and the brand name inside the copy. Swap-breaking: compressing the body into a single paragraph kills the "spoken argument" rhythm the short lines are built for, and removing the headline's direct address to the price objection turns this into a generic brand statement rather than an answer to a specific doubt.
