**Layout forensics.** Solid navy-blue background fills the frame. The top third carries a large bold white serif pull-quote in curly quotation marks, stacked across two centered lines, taking roughly 25% of frame height. Directly below it, five large gold five-point stars sit in a single centered row. Below the stars, a white rounded-rectangle review card with a soft drop shadow occupies the center-to-lower half of the frame: a small gray circular avatar placeholder sits top-left of the card, next to a bold dark name with a flag emoji and, on the same line, a blue checkmark with "Verified Reviewer" in dark text. Below that sits a five-sentence review paragraph in regular-weight dark gray/black sans, then a blue "…Read more" link, then a "Was this review helpful?" line with a thumbs-up emoji and a number. The product — a jar with its box behind it, plus two loose capsules — sits bottom-right, overlapping the card's right edge at a slight angle with a soft cast shadow. Eye order: the oversized white quote first (scale and contrast against navy), then the dense white card with its long review body (mass and centrality), then the product resolving the story bottom-right.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe a specific, ordinary person had the exact problem they have and found real relief — the pull-quote sells the emotional payoff up front, the stars establish instant credibility, and the full review card supplies the proof a skeptical scroller needs before they'll trust a headline claim. Image and words split the job cleanly: the quote is the ad's hook, the card is its evidence, and the product is the quiet resolution sitting at the bottom of the trust stack. This layout carries a strong, specific voice-of-customer review best — it needs a real testimonial with a concrete before/after arc, not a generic compliment.

**Copy skeleton.**
- quote — hook — short first-person exclamation — 6 words
- attribution — prove (identity) — name + locale cue — 2 words
- badge — prove (platform trust) — status label — 2 words
- body — prove (specific transformation) — first-person narrative, 5 sentences — 42 words
- cta — explain (signal more exists) — link label — 2 words
- stat — prove (social engagement) — question + count — 5 words

**Adaptation notes.** Needs one real, specific customer review with a clear before/after arc (sleep, mood, energy — something concrete) and a real reviewer first name; a fabricated quote is a compliance risk on a "verified reviewer" layout. The helpfulness count and flag emoji should reflect real, plausible numbers, not invented precision. Swap-safe: reviewer name, flag/locale, the count, and the packshot — all interchangeable without breaking the layout. Swap-breaking: shortening the review body below three sentences flattens the "verified, detailed reviewer" credibility the card is built to sell, and dropping the star row removes the fastest trust signal in the frame. No human body or face is rendered here (avatar is a generic icon), so there's no content-filter risk on that front.
