## Layout forensics

A 45/55 split, product photo left and copy stack right. The left panel is an overhead shot of a white bowl divided into four wedge sections by thin curved white lines, each section hand-labelled in curved white type running along its own divider — the variety names double as the only copy in that half of the frame, so the food itself is doing double duty as proof and as label. The right panel, on cream, opens with a tight proof stack: five gold stars over a bold small-caps review count, then a rounded coral badge holding the wordmark logo. Below that a two-line serif headline in the brand's dark green, then a short subhead flanked by thin rules with a small heart icon, then three benefit rows each keyed to a filled dark-green checkmark. A large rounded pill CTA anchors the bottom-right corner. First landing is the colourful overhead bowl (largest, most saturated area of the frame); second the serif headline (heaviest single line of type on the right); third the checkmark list, read top to bottom.

## The story this ad tells

It wants the viewer to believe the product is both varied and trusted — the wedge-divided bowl proves range in one glance, the star/review-count pairing proves social validation before a single benefit is read, and the checklist closes on concrete, specific claims rather than vague adjectives. Photo and copy work as a proof pair: the left half shows, the right half tells and backs it with numbers. This layout carries variety-plus-trust messages best, for products that can show several SKUs or flavours in one frame and back them with a real review count.

## Copy skeleton

Stat (prove) — star rating plus review count, 3 words.
Headline (hook) — short benefit statement, 5 words.
Subhead (connect) — transition line into the list, 2 words.
List (prove) — three checkmark benefit rows, 15 words total across 3 items.
CTA (direct) — command, 3 words.

## Adaptation notes

Needs a real, verifiable review count and star rating — an invented number here is a fast way to lose trust on the exact layout built to earn it. Also needs genuine product variety (multiple flavours/SKUs) to fill the overhead wedge shot honestly; a single-SKU brand should swap the divided bowl for a single hero flatlay and drop to fewer wedge labels rather than fabricating variety. The three benefit lines need to be specific and checkable (ingredients, format, sourcing), not generic adjectives, or the checkmark device stops carrying weight. No people shown, so no face/body content-filter risk.
