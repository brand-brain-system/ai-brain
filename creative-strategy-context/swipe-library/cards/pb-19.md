**Layout forensics.** Clean off-white/cream background. Top-left: a circular customer headshot photo, a real smiling woman in her fifties. Immediately to its right, a bold dark-gray name sits on one line with a small blue verified checkmark beside it. Below spans a large black serif quote set across five lines at roughly two-thirds of the frame width — inside that paragraph, one short phrase is set on a solid orange rectangular highlight, breaking the reading rhythm mid-sentence. Directly under the paragraph, a second and more emphatic pull-line sits on its own full-width orange highlight bar across two lines, visually separated from the paragraph above it and reading as the ad's real takeaway. Bottom-right: a circular tan badge with curved text sits just left of the product, a dark-capped cream-label bottle angled and cropped by the bottom edge. Bottom-left: a small brand wordmark in a color-accented serif/sans mix. Eye order: the orange highlight blocks first (the only saturated color on the page, breaking a mostly black-on-cream palette), then the headshot and name establishing who's speaking, then the product resolving the pitch.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe a real, credible customer had a specific and relatable problem and found relief — the headshot and verified badge establish this is a real person, the long-form quote gives the whole arc (problem, timeline, result), and the highlight treatment does the skimmer's work for them by surfacing the two lines worth reading even if nothing else gets read. Image and words split responsibility cleanly: the photo sells authenticity, the highlighted copy sells the outcome. This layout carries a detailed, multi-beat testimonial best — one with a clear timeframe and a strong closing line, since the highlight treatment needs two genuinely quotable moments, not one.

**Copy skeleton.**
- attribution — prove (identity) — name + verified mark — 2 words
- quote — hook/prove (problem → timeline → result) — first-person narrative — 30 words
- quote — hook (emphatic pull-line, highlighted) — first-person superlative statement — 8 words
- badge — prove (authority/credential) — seal label — 6 words

**Adaptation notes.** Needs one long, real customer quote with a genuine timeframe ("after X weeks") and a natural closing superlative line the highlight treatment can isolate — writing a review to fit this template backwards is a fabrication risk. Needs a real customer headshot (or a licensed stock photo standing in for one, clearly not implying a specific unconfirmed person) and a real seal — doctor-developed, award, or certification — not an invented credential. Swap-safe: name, headshot, highlight color, badge text, and packshot. Swap-breaking: shortening the quote below three sentences removes the room the two-tier highlight treatment needs to work, and dropping either highlight block flattens the whole point of the layout. A real face is rendered here — flag for content-filter review before shipping, and never use a real person's photo without their consent even when the quote is genuine.
