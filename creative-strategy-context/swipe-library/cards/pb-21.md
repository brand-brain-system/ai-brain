**Layout forensics.** A diagonal gradient runs dark-brown (upper-left) to warm tan (lower-right), splitting the frame roughly corner to corner. Upper-left: a bold, rounded, retro-serif headline in cream/off-white stacks across three left-aligned lines, taking up almost half the frame height on its own — loose, playful letterforms, not a rigid grid. Upper-right: a hand enters the frame from off-screen, fingers pinching a single chocolate-filled cone piece mid-pick. Center-right, below the hand: a glass bowl is packed to overflowing with the same product, filling a large chunk of the lower-right of the frame; a few loose pieces and crumbs sit scattered on the tan surface in front of it. Bottom-left: the product pouch sits at a slight angle, its top edge finished with a drip-style graphic, bold wordmark, a script sub-line, a small cartoon mascot, and a flavor callout at the base. Eye order: the oversized cream headline first (maximum contrast against the dark gradient), then the full bowl of product (scale and texture), then the hand action tying the two together.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe this snack is so good it provokes an involuntary, unfiltered reaction — the headline does all the persuasive work with tone alone (no stats, no reviews needed), and the hand caught mid-grab plus the overflowing bowl make the craving physical and immediate. Image and words work as one gesture: loud, loose type paired with a candid snacking moment, selling personality over proof. This layout carries a single punchy, personality-forward line best — it needs a brand voice confident enough to be blunt or profane, and breaks on anything that reads corporate or hedged.

**Copy skeleton.**
- headline — hook (provocative reaction statement) — command-adjacent first-person-brand exclamation — 6 words

**Adaptation notes.** Needs a brand voice that can credibly say something loud and unfiltered without feeling try-hard — this only works for brands whose existing tone supports it. Needs a genuinely appetizing "bowl full of product" hero shot and a real hand-grab moment; both are swappable for any snack format without breaking the layout. Swap-safe: gradient colors, headline wording (within the same register), flavor/pack callout, mascot. Swap-breaking: adding stats, badges, or a review turns this into a different template — the whole point here is that the gradient and the headline carry the ad alone. A real hand is rendered here — flag for content-filter review on hand/finger anatomy before shipping.
