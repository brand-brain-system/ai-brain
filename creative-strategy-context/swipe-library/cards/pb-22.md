**Layout forensics.** The full frame is a close-up food photograph: glistening salted-caramel cubes drizzled with dark chocolate sauce and dusted with flaky sea salt, shot on dark slate with shallow depth of field. Over the top third, a bold white sans headline runs three centered lines directly on the photo, one word set in italic for emphasis mid-sentence. The product bar sits laid into the scene at bottom-right, angled roughly 15 degrees as if just set down among the caramel pieces. Along the very bottom, a dark semi-transparent rounded bar holds three stat columns — bold white numerals over small all-caps labels, separated by thin vertical dividers — with a final small all-caps claim line centered beneath it. Eye order: the glossy caramel photography first (it fills the frame and reads as appetite before anything else), then the bold white headline cutting across it, then the product resolving what's being described.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe the product genuinely tastes like the indulgent food shown, not like a compromise — the food photography does the sensory selling, the headline names the specific flavor comparison, and the stat bar quietly reassures that the indulgence comes with real nutrition numbers attached. Image and words split cleanly: the photo sells craving, the copy sells permission. This layout carries a specific, appetizing flavor comparison best — it needs a real dessert or dish the flavor genuinely resembles, and breaks on generic or vague flavor names.

**Copy skeleton.**
- headline — hook (flavor comparison claim) — statement with one emphasized word — 10 words
- stat (×3) — prove (nutrition facts) — numeral + label, column format — 6 words across 3 items
- disclaimer — prove (clean-label claim) — short all-caps tag — 2 words

**Adaptation notes.** Needs a real, appetizing food comparison that the flavor genuinely delivers on, real nutrition numbers for the stat bar, and one true clean-label or ingredient claim for the bottom line — this only works for food and beverage products with an actual flavor story to tell. Swap-safe: the food scene, the specific flavor name, all three stat values, and the bottom claim. Swap-breaking: removing the food photography collapses the entire premise (the product alone can't carry a "tastes like X" claim), and dropping to two stats or fewer weakens the nutrition-facts credibility the bar is built to convey.
