## Body

**Layout forensics.**

On a plain white background, a single large opening quotation mark in the brand's accent color sits centered at the very top, signaling that everything below is a direct quote. Beneath it, a short italic semi-bold setup line leads into two lines of enormous, heavy black bold all-caps — the bait phrase — set at a scale roughly triple the setup line's, built to stop the scroll before the sentence resolves. A smaller sentence-case line follows, delivering the reveal and the brand name, with the closing quotation mark rendered small and inline at the end of that line rather than as a matching large graphic mark. A right-aligned attribution sits below in light gray. The bottom third splits into two halves: left, a single product bottle at a slight angle with a couple of loose tablets beside it, paired with a circular trust-seal badge to its left; right, five filled stars stacked above a bold two-line proof claim with a small icon. A full-width disclaimer line runs along the very bottom edge in small gray text. First thing the eye lands on: the two lines of enormous bait-phrase text, since their scale dwarfs everything else on the page. Second: the setup line above it, once the eye backs up to find the sentence's start. Third: the reveal line, where the brand name and the resolution both land.

**The story this ad tells.**

This ad tells the viewer "wait, is this what I think it is?" before immediately answering its own question — the bait phrase is written to read as alarming or intimate out of context, and the smaller reveal line directly beneath it reframes the whole thing as a specific, relatable symptom with a named solution. Image and words split the job almost entirely into words: there's no photography doing persuasive work here, just typography scaled for maximum tension-then-release, with the product and proof elements held in reserve at the bottom to close the sale once the hook has landed. This layout carries provocative, real-quote-driven hooks best — it needs an actual customer quote with a genuinely surprising opening clause, since a bland or clearly-branded bait phrase won't create the pause the whole layout depends on.

**Copy skeleton.**

- headline (setup): hook (the quote's opening clause, sets up the misdirection), quote, 4 words
- headline (bait): hook (the provocative phrase, built to be read out of context), quote, 6 words
- subhead (reveal): explain (resolves the misdirection into the real, specific symptom and brand), statement, 10 words
- attribution: prove (a real name, grounds the quote as a genuine customer), statement, 2 words
- badge: prove (a trust seal claim), statement, 2 words
- stat: prove (a proof-of-authority number paired with a claim), stat, 4 words
- disclaimer: remove risk (regulatory disclosure required alongside a health-adjacent claim), statement, 16 words

**Adaptation notes.**

Needs one real, verbatim customer quote with a genuinely provocative or double-take opening clause that resolves into something specific and relatable — a written-to-sound-provocative line reads as try-hard rather than authentic. Needs a real trust badge and a real, source-checked proof stat for the right-hand corner — in this reference the proof stat is a professional-endorsement count rather than a review count, so match that slot to whatever the brand actually has (reviews, doctors, members) instead of assuming a review count. Swaps that keep the layout intact: the accent color, the specific badge and stat claims, the product shown. Swaps that break it: writing a bait phrase that isn't drawn from a real quote, or running the badge/stat without being able to substantiate them. Flag for human review: this card pairs a health-adjacent quote with a disclaimer, so any implied efficacy claim in the reveal line or the stat needs sign-off before running; and the closing quotation mark in this reference is small and inline rather than a large matching mark, so don't assume a second big graphic mark is needed to match this layout.

**Fit note.** humor/edge-tolerant | TOF | a REAL provocative quote with a reveal | bold tone.
