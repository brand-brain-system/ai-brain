## Body

**Layout forensics.**

A bright outdoor lifestyle photo of a person mid-action in the product, on a palm-lined course in natural daylight, fills the left two-thirds of the square frame. Centered at the top, the brand's wordmark and icon mark sit small and bold. Directly below the logo, a two-line headline in enormous bold white sans-serif with a dark outline is set inside curly quotation marks like an unattributed endorsement, large enough to read over the busy background. Bottom-right, three product variants are fanned and overlapping in the foreground, each a different colorway, shot crisp and studio-lit against the softer lifestyle background so they pop forward as the "real" product even though the scene around them is a photograph. First thing the eye lands on: the oversized white headline, the highest-contrast element against both sky and foliage. Second: the figure mid-motion, since motion and a human body pull the eye next. Third: the fanned colorway array bottom-right, where the ad resolves into "here's what to buy."​

**The story this ad tells.**

This ad tells the viewer "this is what serious people in this category wear," using an unattributed, tagline-style superlative claim over an authentic action shot to sell both the use-case and the range in one frame. The words carry the confidence and the aspiration; the lifestyle photo proves the product performs in the exact moment it's designed for; the colorway array at the bottom turns aspiration into a concrete choice. This layout carries confident, unattributed brand-voice claims best for multi-colorway physical products — it needs a real action photo of someone actually using the product, not a studio shot, and at least three real colorways to fan out credibly.

**Copy skeleton.**

- headline: hook (a bold, unattributed superlative claim framed like an endorsement), statement, 5 words

**Adaptation notes.**

Needs a genuine outdoor action photograph of someone using the product in context — this only works for apparel or gear with a clear use-case, and doesn't adapt to single-SKU or non-wearable products. Needs at least three real colorways to fan in the foreground; fewer than three loses the point of the "array" half of the template, and this shouldn't be forced onto a single-SKU brand. Swaps that keep the layout intact: the sport or setting, the specific claim, which three colorways appear. Swaps that break it: substituting a studio or stock photo for a real action shot, or fanning fewer than three genuinely distinct colorways. The layout puts a full human body front and center mid-motion — flag for content-filter review, since a full-body figure in athletic motion can trigger likeness/generation checks; a real licensed photo (not a generated one) avoids this entirely.

**Fit note.** multi-colorway physical products (apparel, gear) | TOF/BOF | 3+ colorways + lifestyle use case | aspirational tone. Exclude for single-SKU.
