## Layout forensics

The whole frame is a single selfie: a young man mid-task, brushing his teeth, shot on a front camera in ordinary bathroom light with visible grain and no styling. There is no product, no logo, no CTA anywhere on the image — the ad format itself is the disguise. Overlaid on the lower half is a convincing screenshot of a social-forum post card: platform chrome (subreddit icon, name, timestamp, username, a blue follow button, an overflow menu) sits above a bold two-line post title, then three short paragraphs of regular-weight body text. The overlay is positioned low and centred, as if the person paused mid-task to react to something they just read. First landing is the face and the mundane action (it reads as a real story before anything else); second the bold post title, which carries the actual hook; third the body copy, which resolves the claim.

## The story this ad tells

It wants the viewer to believe they've stumbled on a real person's genuine reaction to a real post, not an ad. The mundane action — brushing teeth — is doing deliberate work: nobody stages a personal-care moment for a brand, so it reads as unplanned and therefore trustworthy. This layout carries pure curiosity/opinion hooks best, for categories where the problem itself (not a specific product) is the interesting thing — it explicitly has no room for a product shot, logo, or CTA, so it only works as a top-of-funnel scroll-stopper feeding a separate landing experience.

## Copy skeleton

Headline (hook) — provocative opinion stated as fact, question or command, 12 words.
Body (explain) — first-person expansion in 2-3 short paragraphs, statement, 48 words.

## Adaptation notes

Needs a genuinely provocative, real-feeling opinion in the product's problem space — a bland or obviously-marketing headline breaks the native illusion immediately. No product or brand element appears anywhere, so this only works as a cold-open hook with the actual offer living downstream (comments, landing page, or a follow-up ad in sequence); it cannot carry a CTA without breaking its own premise. The selfie needs a real, unstaged-looking person doing an unrelated mundane task — this is the layout with the highest content-filter risk since a face and body are front and centre and central to the whole illusion; a generic or clearly-stock face undermines the native feel and may also trip similarity/likeness review depending on the source.
