## Body

**Layout forensics.** On a flat warm off-white background, a bold dark-navy headline spans two centered lines at the very top — the biggest, boldest text on the page and the clear first read. Directly below it, a row of five gold stars sits centered, followed by a smaller gray rating line — together these form the second read, a quick trust stat before the eye moves down. The middle of the frame holds a phone mockup shown at a slight scale, its screen displaying real in-app UI (a circular progress ring, streak badges, a blue action button) — third read, since it's the largest single graphic element. A frosted white review card overlaps the bottom of the phone, itself carrying a small star row, a bold one-line headline, and several lines of italic quoted review text ending in an inline name attribution — fourth read, and the densest text block on the page. Beneath the card, a small letter-spaced "as featured in" line introduces a row of five press logos, and the brand's wordmark closes the frame bottom right.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe this app is both popular and independently validated — the headline leads with scale (a member count), the star row and rating line supply a platform-verified score, the phone mockup proves the product actually exists and does something concrete, the review card supplies a specific, credible first-person result, and the press row closes with third-party authority. This is a trust-stacking layout, not a single-proof layout: it works because every element is a different kind of proof (scale, rating, testimonial, press), and it needs all of them to feel substantiated, not just the loudest one.

**Copy skeleton.** headline — hook — number/stat statement — 6 words. subhead (rating line) — prove — stat statement — 9 words. subhead (review title) — hook — statement — 6 words. quote (review + attribution) — prove — first-person quote — 36 words. eyebrow — prove — statement — 3 words.

**Adaptation notes.** This is an app/software layout, not a physical-product layout — the reference's "product" zone is a phone mockup with an in-app screenshot, so it needs real (or closely approximated) product UI to render inside the frame, not a packshot; a physical-goods brand should route to a different template rather than forcing a device mockup. Requires a real member count, a real store rating, one real review, and real press logos — drop or omit any single proof element the brand can't verify (a brand with no press, for instance, can lose the press row and logo strip and keep the rest of the layout intact); inventing any of these breaks trust with the platform and with the viewer. No people in frame, so no content-filter risk.
