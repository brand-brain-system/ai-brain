## Body

**Layout forensics.** The frame splits vertically at roughly 45/55. The left portion is a photographed lifestyle scene — a light wood tabletop holding a hero product pouch front and center in sharp focus, with a second and third pouch cropped at the left and right edges behind it, plus a scoop of powder and loose coffee beans scattered in the foreground for texture. This reads first, since it's the only photographic, colorful element on the page. The right portion is a flat off-white panel carrying a vertical stack of five short bullet lines, each led by a small filled teal circle, generous line spacing between them — this reads second, working top to bottom as a checklist. A small circular brand mark sits quietly bottom right of the list panel, last read.

**The story this ad tells.** It wants the viewer to believe one product line (or in this reference, one product family) delivers several distinct, credible benefits at once — the photographed hero pouch supplies appetite appeal and product truth, while the bullet list does the persuasive heavy lifting with short, punchy, single-claim lines that are easy to scan without committing to a full paragraph. This layout carries five real, distinct benefits best; it reads as a fact sheet, not a story, so it needs each line to be a genuinely different claim rather than five phrasings of the same idea.

**Copy skeleton.** list — explain — five-item bulleted list — 28 words across 5 items.

**Adaptation notes.** The reference shows a three-product lineup (one hero pouch centered, two others cropped at the edges) rather than the single product the source prompt describes — a single-SKU brand should drop the two flanking pouches and center the hero alone on the wood surface, which keeps the layout intact. Needs five real, distinct benefit lines; cut any claim (here including a blue-light-protection line and an anti-inflammatory line) the brand can't honestly make rather than padding to reach five. No people in frame, so no content-filter risk.
