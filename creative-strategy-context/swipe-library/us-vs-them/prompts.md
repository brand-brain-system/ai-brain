# Us vs. Them: prompts

Rules for building the final prompt: `../production.md`. In this category, comparisons have to
be fair and checkable; use a generic, unbranded competitor product unless a real competitor is
named deliberately.

---

## From our templates

### 1 · us-vs-them-01.webp: split background, VS badge, check/X lists
Suggested ratio: 1:1 or 4:5

Prompt: A comparison ad split vertically into two equal halves. Left half: [PRIMARY BRAND COLOR]
background with a faint soft-shadow texture. Right half: [NEUTRAL CONTRAST COLOR like warm light
beige]. Top center, spanning both halves: a bold all-caps white sans-serif headline "[HEADLINE,
2-3 words, benefit or outcome]" set on two stacked [ACCENT COLOR] rectangular highlight blocks, one
per line. Center divider: a white circle with "VS" in bold [PRIMARY BRAND COLOR]. Left middle:
[OUR PRODUCT] with [SERVING OR USE DETAIL like the finished drink in a white cup] in front, soft
studio light. Right middle: [GENERIC COMPETITOR PRODUCT OR HABIT like a plain glass of black
coffee], unbranded, same lighting. Left bottom: three rows, each a white circle checkmark icon
with two-weight uppercase text: "[STRENGTH 1-3]". Right bottom: three rows, each an [ACCENT COLOR]
circle X icon with dark two-weight uppercase text: "[WEAKNESS 1-3]". Key word in each row in bold,
the rest in regular weight.

Must be sourced: every strength (brand materials); every weakness (a fact that holds for the
category, not an invented flaw); the headline if it makes a health or performance claim.

### 2 · us-vs-them-02.jpg: feature table + price gap
Suggested ratio: 1:1

Prompt: A clean white background. Top 55%: a comparison table with [4-5] rows separated by thin
light-gray horizontal lines. Left column: feature names in regular-weight dark navy sans-serif
"[FEATURE 1-4]". Two narrow columns on the right: dark navy checkmarks under the competitor column,
[BRAND COLOR] checkmarks under ours. Last row: "[PRICE ROW LABEL like 8-count refill]" with
"[COMPETITOR PRICE]" in large bold dark navy and "[OUR PRICE]" in large bold [BRAND COLOR]. A thin
gray hand-drawn curved arrow runs from the headline up to the competitor price. Bottom left:
stacked bold uppercase headline, first lines "[HEADLINE PART 1]" in dark navy, last lines
"[HEADLINE PART 2]" in [BRAND COLOR]. Bottom right: [GENERIC COMPETITOR PRODUCT], unbranded, next
to [OUR PRODUCT], both cropped at the bottom edge, each with a short bold label above:
"[COMPETITOR LABEL like Leading brand]" and "[BRAND]".

Must be sourced: both prices, with source and date (our site; a real, comparable competitor
product); every feature row (brand materials). Features must be ones both products can actually be
compared on.

### 3 · us-vs-them-03.webp: one product split down the middle, annotated
Suggested ratio: 9:16 or 4:5
Use when comparing two of the brand's own variants (fit, formula, size) rather than a competitor.

Prompt: A flat-lay of [OUR PRODUCT] on a plain [BACKGROUND like light warm-gray seamless paper],
soft even overhead light. The product is split exactly down its vertical center by a white dotted
line running the full height of the frame: the left half is [VARIANT A with its color and
details], the right half is [VARIANT B with its color and details], joined seamlessly as one
object. Above the left half: bold white condensed uppercase "[VARIANT A NAME]". Above the right
half: the same in bold white, followed by one word in [ACCENT COLOR] italic serif: "[VARIANT B
DIFFERENTIATOR like relaxed]". Three thin [ACCENT COLOR] annotation lines on each side point from
the product to short dark sans-serif labels: left "[A DETAIL 1-3]", right "[B DETAIL 1-3]". Brand
logo small and centered below the product.

Must be sourced: every detail label (the product pages for both variants).

## From the Adcrate playbook

### #7 · Us vs Them (photography gap is the argument)
Suggested ratio: 4:5
Example image: `pb-07-us-vs-them.png`

Prompt: A side-by-side comparison divided vertically. Left: muted gray-blue background. Right:
[PRIMARY BRAND COLOR]. Center top: a white circle with "VS". Left header: "[COMPETITOR CATEGORY]"
above a generic unbranded competitor product, flatly lit, with a list of X marks: "[WEAKNESS 1-5]".
Right header: "[BRAND]" above [OUR PRODUCT], hero-lit, with a list of checkmarks: "[STRENGTH 1-5]".
Brand logo bottom right.

Must be sourced: strengths and weaknesses, as above.

### #25 · Us vs Them color split with VS burst
Suggested ratio: 1:1
Example image: `pb-25-color-split.png`

Prompt: A comparison ad divided vertically into two equal halves. Left half: [PRIMARY BRAND COLOR]
background. [OUR PRODUCT] shown with energy: [ACTION DETAIL like chocolate dripping / liquid
pouring]. Brand logo in bold white upper left. Below the product: four benefits, each with a
green circle checkmark, in bold white uppercase: "[STRENGTH 1-4]". Right half: [CONTRAST COLOR like
pale cream] background. A generic unbranded competitor product: [DESCRIPTION like crumpled
foil-wrapped chocolate bar]. Header "[COMPETITOR CATEGORY]" in dark text. Below: four weaknesses,
each with a red circle X, in bold dark uppercase: "[WEAKNESS 1-4]". Center divider: a comic-style
"VS" burst in [ACCENT COLOR].

Must be sourced: every number in the strengths and weaknesses (nutrition labels, spec sheets). A
competitor number has to describe a typical product in the category, not one picked to look bad.

### #31 · Comparison grid, meme style
Suggested ratio: 1:1
Example image: `pb-31-comparison-grid.png`

Prompt: A structured comparison grid on a white background. Top row split 50/50: left, [OUR
PRODUCT] clean on white with [DETAIL like chips spilling out]; right, a generic unbranded
[COMPETITOR PRODUCT] on white. Below: three full-width rows, each split 50/50 by a thin black
vertical line and separated by thin black horizontal lines. Row 1: "[OUR ADVANTAGE 1]" vs
"[COMPETITOR WEAKNESS 1]". Row 2: "[OUR ADVANTAGE 2]" vs "[COMPETITOR WEAKNESS 2]". Row 3: "[OUR
ADVANTAGE 3]" vs "[COMPETITOR WEAKNESS 3]". All text in bold black serif or heavy sans-serif,
centered in each cell. No icons, no color, no checkmarks. It should feel like a meme-format
comparison shared on X or Reddit.

Must be sourced: rows 1-2 (ingredients, specs). Row 3 can be a subjective line ("Tastes amazing")
if it comes from real customer language.
